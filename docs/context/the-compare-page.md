## The compare page
**`/compare/<A>/<B>` — two stocks side by side (2026-09-23, owner's request).** **Two, never more**, which is the owner's constraint and also the design: a difference is inherently pairwise, and three columns of ninety fields is a table rather than a comparison. Member page, guests refused, like the studio and the pivot beside it.

- **No endpoint of its own.** `/api/stock` already returns the row, the profile, the earnings and the universe list, and it already holds the whole snapshot in memory to answer — so the page calls it **twice, in parallel**, and that IS the compare endpoint. I had proposed a dedicated `/api/compare` and was wrong: the singular route was already the right shape, and a second one would have been a second guest guard and a second thing to keep in step. Two calls are ~10KB each against `/api/stocks`'s 2.1MB.
- **The symbols ride in the PATH**, so a comparison can be bookmarked and sent, and so both entry points can pre-fill it. Both segments are optional and nothing is validated in the route — the page asks `/api/stock` for each, which is the thing that already knows the universe and already refuses a guest a symbol outside the preview. A bad symbol becomes a line on the page rather than a 404 over the whole comparison. The server sends the same file whatever the path; the page reads `location.pathname`.
- **Both entry points arrive with a side already filled**, which was the owner's instruction. The screener's `Compare` button carries **the top row of the current view** — `researchSymbol`, the same symbol the Research menu's per-symbol rows use, so it follows the sort and the portfolio filter — and only that one side, because the second stock is the choice the page exists to make. The stock page's `Compare ▸` sits beside the overlay picker and carries **itself plus whatever is overlaid right now**, so the page opens on the pair already on screen.

### The chart is REBASED, and the third line is the point
Both series are scaled to 100 at the **first session the two actually share**, because two share prices are not comparable — the stock page's overlay already established this, and the test uses the same trick to prove it (a ~100x price difference in opposite directions, so a raw chart could not put both on one readable scale). Read out of the **drawn SVG paths**: both must start within 1.5px of each other and then diverge.

- **The intersection of the dates, never the union.** Two stocks do not trade the same calendar — a foreign listing misses US sessions, a recent listing starts late — so a chart drawn on the union either invents prices or slides one line against the other. The caption says how many sessions the window actually holds.
- **A ratio strip underneath** (A÷B, rebased to 100) is the reading most people are trying to take off a two-line chart and mostly cannot: above 100, the first stock is ahead of where it started. It carries its own label where it is drawn, because its explanation is in the caption and on a laptop that is under the fold.
- `chartSVG` draws both — A as `closes`, B as an overlay — so the log axis, the gridlines and the ticks come for free. **A and B are the accent pair, never green/red**: those already mean up and down on every chart in this app, and two lines coloured by their own direction would each be making a different claim from the one the chart is for.

### The difference column is derived from the RENDERER, not from a second list
Each `V.*` helper in `rowcard.js` now reports its raw number (`n`) and its unit (`u`) beside the text, and `fieldValues()` carries both through. So the ledger is `FIELD_SPEC` drawn twice with a difference between, and **a field added to the screener gets a correct difference here with no edit** — the alternative was annotating ~58 entries by hand, which is the drift FIELD_SPEC exists to prevent. Both server callers (`mobileRow`, `/api/m/stock`) pick `t` and `c` off it by name, so the phone payload is not a byte larger.

- **The rule the refresh email already settled**: absolutes compared **relatively**, percentages in **points**. A margin going 18% → 21% is +3pt, not +17%.
- **A relative difference requires BOTH SIDES POSITIVE.** That one guard covers every trap at once — a P/E off a loss (arithmetic, not cheapness), net income that swung negative, a MACD line whose sign flipped — each of which would otherwise print a confident percentage meaning nothing.
- **A cross-currency money difference is WITHHELD, never converted**, with the two currencies named in the tooltip and a note at the top of the page. **Market cap is the one exception** and it is whitelisted by name: it comes back in USD even when the rest of the row does not, verified across eighteen foreign reporters.
- **Gross margin and net cash are not differenced when either side is a financial.** The rows stay — they are what the provider returned — but a lender's 100% gross margin against a manufacturer's 42% is not a comparison. The cards already drop these for banks; here the row has a second stock in it and dropping it silently would be the worse answer.
- **NO WINNER IS MARKED, and that is deliberate.** The obvious feature is a green tick on the "better" side, and it is the one that would quietly turn this into a recommendation engine — the thing the rest of the product declines to be. The column is neutral, labelled "Difference", and the note says so: how far apart two numbers are is a fact, which of them is better is not.

#### The ledger got bars, because 84 rows of digits is a wall (2026-09-23)
Measured on the live page before changing anything: **84 rows, 11 groups, 3,174px** — four screens of scrolling, of which 64 carry a difference, 20 are text that cannot be differenced, and 8 read identically on both sides. Two problems, not one: a number says nothing about the SIZE of a gap, and a fifth of the rows say nothing at all.

- **Two bars per row, in the A/B colours the chart already uses.** ONE RULE rather than one per unit: each bar is its own value as a share of the **larger of the pair, measured from zero**. Being proportional, it can never overstate — RSI 61 against 68 draws 90% against 100% and correctly reads as close, where a pair-relative scale would stretch two-thirds of a point across the full width. **Where either value is negative the zero moves to the middle** and the bars grow out from it, because a percentage has a meaningful zero and a bar that ignored the sign would draw a 30% fall as a healthy-looking bar.
- **A withheld difference gets NO BAR.** Drawing two bars across currencies, or off a loss, would make exactly the comparison the row above just declined to make — and the picture is the more persuasive of the two.
- **A toggle hides the rows where both sides read the same**, labelled with the live count ("Hide 3 matching") so the offer names its own benefit. Default OFF — a page called "every field" must not quietly drop rows — and the choice is remembered in `localStorage`, a per-device convenience like `/m`'s view and chart switches.
- **"Biggest differences first" was proposed and dropped.** It requires ranking incommensurable quantities: whether +15pt of margin is a bigger gap than +346% of market cap has no answer, and a sort order would have invented one. The bars already let the eye find the big gaps, which is what the sort was for.
- **A radar chart was considered and rejected**: the shape changes entirely if the axes are reordered, area is systematically misread, and 84 dimensions do not go on one.

**THE FLEX WENT ON THE `td` AND BROKE THE TABLE — 67 assertions passed straight through it.** A table cell made a flex container stops participating in the table's column sizing: the difference cell collapsed to 54px, left a 171px gap before the next column, and its bars overflowed backwards so that "+150%" printed on top of "$100.0B". The flex belongs on a wrapper INSIDE the cell.

- **A screenshot found it; the first assertion written for it did not.** That check looked for cells OVERLAPPING, and the symptom is a **gap** — a collapsed cell shrinks to its content and leaves the rest of the column empty. Cells are asserted to **tile** now (each starts within 1.5px of where the last ended) and the difference cell's content is asserted to stay inside its own box. Proved by reverting: the corrected pair fails on 20 rows and 11 spills, where the original passed on all of them.
- The lesson generalises past this page: **when a layout bug is found by eye, the assertion written afterwards must be checked against the broken version**, or it records the wrong symptom and passes next time too.
- `.cbars .cb` and its fill are both `display: block` — the `/quality` trap, where a percentage width on an inline span drew nothing while the test read the style attribute and passed. Every measurement here is `getBoundingClientRect()`.

Verified: **67 checks** on the page (the bars measured off their rendered boxes; the larger filling the track and the smaller in proportion; a close pair looking close; the zero moving to the middle with the loss growing left of it; no bar where the difference was withheld; the four cells tiling and the bars staying inside their cell; the toggle's count, the rows it removes, the height it saves, no heading left empty, and the choice surviving a reload).

### It found a field that had been rendering blank everywhere
**`['info','Size', (s) => (s.capBand || null)]` returned a BARE STRING where every other getter returns `{t, c}`**, so both readers took `.t` off it, got `undefined`, and the Size row drew a **label with no value** — in the hover card, on the stock page, in the tiles and on the phone, since the cap band shipped on 2026-09-21. It passed every structural check there is: the field was in the catalogue, both field pickers offered it, the row was in the DOM. The only thing wrong was that the cell was empty. `/compare` found it by putting an empty cell next to a full one.

**`field-shape-test.js` is the guard**: it builds a row where every property any getter reads is populated (from `fieldProps()`, so it covers a field that starts reading something new), runs all 92 getters, and fails on anything that is neither `null` nor an object carrying `t`. No server, no browser, no data, under a second. Proved by reverting — the old line fails two of its checks.

### Worth knowing
- **`/api/stock` did not apply the read-path stamps, so `capBand` came back undefined** — the Size row drew nothing on the stock page and was skipped entirely here. It calls `stampCapBand` now, like every other read path; the stamp is synchronous and reads `marketCap` and nothing else. Found on production, not by the suite: the fixture wrote `capBand` directly, which the snapshot in production does not carry.
- **`/api/stock` serves the snapshot's STORED verdict; `/api/stocks` re-derives on the way out.** So the compare page and the stock page can lag the screener by one refresh if the rules change. Compare matches the stock page it links to rather than the screener, deliberately — the same stock reading two ways on two pages you can click between is worse than either. A fixture therefore has to WRITE `action`/`actionFlag` here, the opposite of what the pivot suites need.
- **`/api/history` rounds closes to 2dp**, which at a ~$3 share price moves a window return by about a tenth of a point. Harmless for a chart (the comment says a 600px chart cannot show more) and the caption inherits it — the precision trap `/single` records at NVDA's split-adjusted $0.16 closes, in miniature. The test computes its expectations through the same rounding.
- Verified: **45 checks** in a browser (both pickers filled from the path and neither offering the other side; exactly two, never three; the rebasing read out of the drawn paths; the caption's returns, gap and session count computed from the window the range actually reads; each guard firing independently of the others; swap; one side and none; both entry points; no sideways scroll at 390px with the mastheads stacked and the ledger scrolling inside its own box) plus **12 role checks** with a password set (anon redirected, guest refused even for two preview symbols, the raw `.html` funnelled, member served) and **7** on the field shapes. The mobile, stock-route and screener suites were re-run unchanged.
