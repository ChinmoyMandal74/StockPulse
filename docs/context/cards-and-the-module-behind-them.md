## Cards, and the module behind them
**`private/cards.js` owns every card: its markup, its wording AND its styles** (2026-09-14). Its hosts are `/promo` and the phone’s saved posts, and a second copy of "Top gainers this week" would have drifted inside a week, the same reason `rowcard.js`, `screens.js` and `action.js` exist. The contract is one call: `Cards.build(id, ctx)` with `{stocks, myLists, size, opts, getBasket}` returning the card's inner HTML. **The module reads no DOM and issues no requests** — the host hands it control values by control id and a `getBasket(days)` that returns the archive payload or null while it loads, which is what makes all sixteen card variants testable off-page in a `vm`. Styles ride along in a template literal injected by `Cards.injectStyle()`, so a grammar added to one page cannot be styled only in the other.

**A card is dated by its DATA, not by the reader's clock (2026-09-16).** `dateStr()` stamped `new Date()`, so a card built on a Saturday — or after a refresh had failed — printed today's date over Thursday's prices, and that card goes on Instagram. It is the freshest `latestDate` across the rows now, derived in `build()`, parsed at **local noon** because `new Date('2026-09-16')` is UTC midnight and renders as the 15th west of Greenwich.

**The card prints the refresh TIME beside that date** (owner: "why can't you show the same on the visual itself") — `Sep 16, 2026 · 4:37 PM ET` in the header pill, from `ctx.updatedAt`, which the studio and the phone’s post endpoint both pass. **Always America/New_York, never the builder's own zone**: the card is a baked image, so whoever renders it would otherwise bake their clock into something other people read, and the market it describes keeps one timezone. A host that does not supply the time gets the date alone rather than an invented one.

**Both studio pages say when the prices were pulled** (`#freshLine`, owner's request — "this will give me confidence that it is latest data"): the refresh clock, how long ago, and the day the cards will carry, from the `updatedAt` that `/api/stocks` already returned and both pages were discarding. It goes **amber past 26 hours**, because the one thing worth noticing before posting is that the numbers are yesterday's. The phone's post sheet carries the same fact as "data as of …". Verified on fresh and week-old snapshots: the wording, the flag, and the card stamping Sep 9 rather than today.

**Cards label by NAME, not by ticker** (2026-09-14, owner's instruction). `nameOf(row)` is `shortName || name || symbol` and `symOf(sym)` looks a bare ticker up through it; every card's rows, tiles, tracks, rankings, verdict roll-calls, scatter labels and the Intro live-data slides run through them. A feed reads a company faster than a four-letter code, and the screener carries a Short name column now, so the string exists on every row. The columns were restyled with it — the mono face gives way to the sans one, widths roughly double, and every one truncates with an ellipsis rather than wrapping a post into two lines.

**The Movers time window has calendar windows beside the rolling ones (2026-09-15)**: *This week* (`wtd`) runs from the last close before the current week's Monday, *This month* (`mtd`) from the last close before the 1st — "current" being the week and month of the freshest bar, so a Saturday post shows the week just ended. *Week* and *Month* stay **rolling** (5 and 21 sessions — the last 7 and ~30 days) and are labelled so in the dropdown; the card text for Week changed from "this week" to "past week", since that phrase now belongs to week-to-date. **The snapshot carries no calendar anchors**, so `GET /api/period-anchors` (member, cached 10 minutes) reads each symbol's last close before each boundary (`closesBefore`, ~80ms a boundary) and the studio stamps `wtdPct` / `mtdPct` = price ÷ anchor on its rows; a row without an anchor is simply not ranked. **An anchor more than 7 days older than its boundary is dropped** — a symbol with a gap in its bars (AVB, no bars after 2026-08-24, at the time) would otherwise show a move spanning weeks under a "this week" label. Only `/promo` offers the two windows; `cards.js` supports them wherever a host stamps the fields. Verified: the endpoint against an independent query (NVDA −2.25% week, −3.36% month), and headlessly on real prices (10 checks — both windows rank their own leaders, Week still ranks the rolling 7 days, compare column, side by side).

#### …and year to date is the third of them (2026-10-02, owner's request)
**A `This year (since last year's close)` option in the Movers time window, and `This year` in the compare picker beside it.** The module already knew the key — `ytd: ['ytdPct', 'this year', 'year to date']` went into `MOV_PERIODS` with the screener's YTD column — so the window was reachable by a saved post and by nothing a reader could click. The whole change is two `<option>` lines.

- **IT NEEDS NO `/api/period-anchors` STAMPING, which is the one way it differs from its two calendar siblings.** `computeStocks` puts `ytdPct` on every row, so it rides the snapshot — and that is exactly why **it is the only calendar window that survives a saved post to the PHONE**: `/api/m/post` builds from the snapshot and never stamps the week- and month-to-date anchors, so a `wtd` or `mtd` post draws blank there. Worth knowing before that is read as a new fault.
- **Placed between 6 months and 1 year, where two conventions happen to agree**: calendar windows sit immediately before their rolling neighbour in this picker (*This week* before *Week*, *This month* before *Month*), and the screener's own columns run 6M, YTD, 1Y.
- **A STOCK THAT LISTED THIS YEAR IS ABSENT, NEVER RANKED AT 0.0%** — the blank-versus-zero rule, inherited free, because a row without the field is simply not ranked the way a row without an anchor is not. In the compare column it is an em-dash.
- **THE TWO PICKERS ARE A SECOND COPY OF ONE ANOTHER and nothing made them agree.** A window offered in the time picker and not in the compare one is a card that can rank by something it cannot compare against. The check is stronger than either list: **every key in `Cards.MOV_PERIODS` must appear in both, and neither may offer a key the module does not know** — so a window added to the module alone is caught without this suite restating the catalogue.
  - **The silent fallback is why that last half matters.** `MOV_PERIODS[perKey] || MOV_PERIODS.w1` means a picker sending a key the module has never heard of draws **last week's card under a heading nobody chose** — no error, no blank, just the wrong numbers. **Proved by reverting**: deleting the module's entry fails 12.
- Verified: **28 checks** in the real studio. **The fixture is the test** — each stock's YTD rank disagrees with its rank in *every* other window the picker offers, so a card still reading `oneWeekPct` comes out in a different order rather than coincidentally the same one; and the no-YTD stock is given the **best one-week return in the fixture**, so it tops the week card and its absence from the year card is a fact about the window rather than about a missing row. Plus the compare column, the losers direction, and a saved post round-tripped through the server and built by the phone's own route. **Proved by reverting five times**: the time picker's option fails 15, the compare picker's alone 3, the wrong slot 1, the module's entry 12, and wiring the key to `oneYearPct` instead of `ytdPct` fails 7 — a calendar window reading a rolling field being the whole reason YTD is not `oneYearPct` under another name.
  - **A `\n` IN A REVERT ANCHOR MATCHES NOTHING AND THE PATCH IS SILENTLY SKIPPED**, which reads exactly like a guard that never mattered — four of these five reverts reported that way on the first run for that reason alone. This file had named only `server.js` and `index.html` as CRLF; the files this change touches are CRLF too.
    - **AND THE LINE ENDING IS NOT A PROPERTY OF THE FILE — IT IS A PROPERTY OF THE WORKING COPY AT THAT MOMENT (corrected 2026-10-02, hours after the line above it was written).** I generalised that measurement to *"every tracked source file in this repo is CRLF, all 124, not one LF-only or mixed"*, and it is **false**: measured the same day, **64 all-CRLF, 60 all-LF, 0 mixed**. What makes it worse than a wrong count is that **it moves under you**. `core.autocrlf` normalises on CHECKOUT, so a `git stash` and `pop` rewrote `private/basket.html` from LF back to CRLF **in the middle of this session** — while an Edit/Write tool had rewritten it the other way an hour earlier. Git stores LF either way, so **the difference never appears in a diff** and nothing warns you.
    - **So the rule is DETECT, NEVER ASSUME — per file, at the moment the harness runs**: `const NL = read().includes('\r\n') ? '\r\n' : '\n';`. A harness carrying a hardcoded `'\r\n'` is correct until the next checkout, stash or branch switch, and silently inert after it. Keep the assert-every-anchor-hits-once guard that caught it, which is what makes either mistake loud.
  - **`page.selectOption` ON A VALUE THE SELECT DOES NOT CARRY TIMES OUT AT 30s RATHER THAN FAILING**, so a revert that removes an option **aborts the suite**, and an aborted suite reports "0 failed". Two of these reverts ran 8 and 16 of 28 checks for that reason. Set `.value` and dispatch the page's own `change` event instead: a missing option leaves the select empty, the card falls back, and the assertions decide rather than the wait. The documented trap, met again in a new place.

#### …and on the Chart card, as a date-anchored slice (2026-10-02, owner's request)
**A `This year (since last year's close)` option in the Chart template's Window picker, between Past six months and Past year.** This is the entry the YTD column's own section listed as a deliberate omission, and **the reason it gave was right and is unchanged**: `CHART_WINDOWS` maps bar COUNTS, and a count of sessions is not a calendar boundary on any date but one. What changed is the mechanism — the count is now only the FETCH size and the boundary is found in the date axis the host returns.

- **THE ORIGIN IS 31 DECEMBER, NOT THE YEAR'S FIRST SESSION**, because that is the close `ytdPct` anchors on. Rebasing one session later silently drops the year's first day: on the fixture that is **+100.0% against +66.7%**, with nothing on the card to account for the difference. **Proved by reverting**: fails 6.
- **IT AGREES WITH THE SCREENER COLUMN TO THE PAYLOAD'S PRECISION, NOT EXACTLY, and that is measured rather than claimed.** My first code comment said "exactly" and was an overstatement. `symbolSeries` rounds every value to 3dp, so re-dividing two of them compounds it — against production over **1,169 symbols: median 0.03pt, worst 0.31pt** (AEHR, +429.95 against the column's +430.26), 27 symbols past 0.1pt, and **the error grows with the move**. Left alone deliberately: more decimals on the wire costs ~0.6MB on the studio's largest fetch, and taking the tag from the row's `ytdPct` would print a number disagreeing with the point it is drawn beside.
- **ONE SLICE, ABOVE ALL FOUR MODES.** The one-stock line, the pair, the leaders and the whole-screen average all read `d.dates` and `series`, so slicing once in `tplChart` reaches every one where four copies could only drift — and the 50-day average comes along free, since `maSeries` already rebases to the first window date the archive holds.
- **THE SERIES HAVE TO BE RE-REBASED, not only sliced.** The host rebases to the first session it FETCHED, so slicing alone draws a year-to-date window off a year-ago origin — the right dates with the wrong numbers, which looks perfectly well formed. **Proved by reverting**: fails 5, with the card reading +307.4% under a "this year" kicker.
- **ONLY THIS CARD CAN SLICE, so only this card may be offered the window.** Sparks and the Spotlight read the same `CHART_WINDOWS` map and draw the axis as fetched — handed `ytd` they would draw a FULL YEAR under a heading reading "this year". `barWin()` makes them fall back to six months, so the map cannot lie to two of its three consumers, and that is asserted through the exported `basketDays` rather than left to the markup (a saved post is hand-editable). **Proved by reverting**: fails 2.
- **`over the ${winLabel}` reads "over the this year"**, so the two-stock note takes its phrase from `winPhrase` — "ahead by 50.0 points **so far this year**". The `.replace(/^Past /, '')` that used to sit there **could never fire** (every label is lower case) and went with it: residue reading as intent.
- **THE CARD NAMES ITS OWN ORIGIN.** Every mode's note says "the start of the window", which a bar count's kicker already pins ("past six months") and this one does not — and a posted image has no picker beside it.
- **Before the year's first session it says so** rather than drawing a one-point "line". Without the guard `lineChart` falls through to its generic *No stored history*, which blames the archive for a calendar fact. One or two days a year, and **proved by reverting**: fails 2.
- Verified: **38 checks** in the real studio. **The fixture's load-bearing half is a +20% gap on the year's first session**, so the right origin and the wrong one cannot be confused; the same path makes six months **−50.0%** and a year **+300.0%**, so a card that ignored the key comes out at a different number rather than coincidentally the same one. **The axis is the proof the slice happened** — asserted as `dates[0] === '2025-12-31'` exactly, since a correct end tag could sit over a line drawn across a whole year. Plus the 50-day average on the sliced axis, both end tags, leaders and list, a saved post round-tripped and rebuilt by the phone's own route at the same +100.0%, and a 12-combination fit sweep (3 artboards × 4 modes, nothing overflowing) because the note gains a sentence. **Proved by reverting nine times**, every one load-bearing: no slice 7, no re-rebase 5, the wrong origin 6, the phrase 1, `barWin` 2, the year-not-opened guard 2, the origin sentence 1, the module's entry 13, the option 16.
  - **THE SILENT FALLBACK IS WHY THE MODULE'S ENTRY IS WORTH ITS OWN REVERT.** `CHART_WINDOWS[v] || CHART_WINDOWS.m6` means a picker sending a key the module has never heard of draws **six months under a kicker reading "this year"** — measured in that revert as −50.0%. No error, no blank, just the wrong window.
  - **A REVERT THAT READS `.ytd[0]` UNGUARDED ABORTS THE SUITE**, which reports "0 failed" — the documented trap, met again. The module probe reads `(CHART_WINDOWS.ytd || [0])[0]`.
  - **THE MOVERS PICKER CARRIES A BYTE-IDENTICAL OPTION** (shipped an hour earlier), so the option line alone hits twice in `promo.html` and the patch is refused. The anchor takes the comment above it. *An anchor that is unique today stops being unique the moment the same control is added twice.*
  - **A FIXTURE'S WINDOW IS N SESSIONS INCLUSIVE, so its first index is `LAST - (N-1)`.** Both the six-month and one-year anchors were off by one and reported 293% against a planned 300% — the code was right.
  - **`/api/basket` DERIVES `since` FROM THE CLOCK, so an archive rewound past the window is legitimately empty.** The check that a bar window still draws beside the year-not-opened refusal asked for six months against a ten-month-stale archive and got nothing — proving neither thing. It asks for a year, which reaches back far enough.
  - **The date-axis labels are classless `<text>` holding the ISO date** — read off `lineChart` rather than guessed at. My first selector (`.xlab, .cx`) matched nothing and reported an empty axis, the speculative-selector trap the Disclaimer card records.

**The Movers card can carry the verdict** (2026-09-14, `movAdv`): off by default, else a rule set from `ADV_PROFILES`, scored in the browser through `advScored()` — the same cached engine pass the Advice cards use, so it costs nothing beyond the first profile. **The word rides UNDER the name, not in a column of its own**: "Sell Immediately" would need ~170px, and with the comparison column on there is not that much left; stacked it costs no width and about 2px of row height. **The rule set is named in the KICKER, not in a paragraph** — the first cut put it in an `s-sub` and the five extra lines of prose were most of what pushed the card over its own masthead; the attribution still has to be there, so it moved somewhere free. A row the engine cannot score simply has no second line. **The side-by-side layout is excluded** — it already runs two rankings in half the width apiece — and the promo control hides itself when Direction is "Both, side by side". 

**A card is a fixed artboard and cannot scroll, so a list too tall does not clip — it sits on top of the masthead**, because `.s-body` centres its content. The ranked list therefore picks its rhythm from the row count, the artboard and whether a verdict line is on (`ROOM` / `RHYTHM` in `tplMovers`): default, then `tight`, then `tighter`, each shrinking the bar, the name and the number **together**, since a short bar beside a full-size number reads as a mistake rather than as a denser card. Past the tightest rhythm the list is **trimmed** rather than overflowed — the same per-shape cap the side-by-side card takes — and because the rows are sorted by the move it is always the smallest that go, with the title saying "Top gainers" rather than a count so nothing on the card becomes untrue. Square at Top 15 with a comparison column lands there (11 rows); it overflowed before the verdict feature existed too. **Verified by a harness that measures the artboard independently of the builder's own estimates** — 72 combinations of shape × count × advice × compare × direction, 0 overflowing, tightest fit 46px of slack.

**Side by side on a Story fills the frame (2026-09-15)** — it used to render the post layout, dense past ten rows (names cut at 92px), bunched at the top of a 1920px artboard. On `sz-story` each row is two lines (name and move above, the bar full width beneath), the rows sit in `.mlist` and spread with `space-evenly`, and **the shorter column is padded with invisible `.mpad` rows** so the nth gainer stays level with the nth loser. Six rows or fewer get `.few` (40px type, 30px bars), because five rows spread through a story read as lines floating in a frame. Post and square are unchanged. Verified by measuring the real page, 3 shapes × 3 scopes × Top 5/10/15 × two windows: no overflow anywhere, columns level everywhere, the list uses 94-98% of the story height, at most 2 names in 30 truncated on a story.

**Two places deliberately keep the ticker.** The **Chart** card: its legend chips and end tags sit in inches of space beside a drawn line, where only a short code fits — the owner excluded it by name. And the Intro **mock** slide, which draws a fake screener row beside its star: that column really does show the symbol, so naming it would make the illustration lie about the product. The fundamentals scatter compromises instead — it names its eight outlier dots, anchors a label back towards the middle when its dot sits in the right half, and clips past 22 characters, because a name is many times wider than a ticker and would otherwise run off the artboard.

**`/cards`, the member room, was REMOVED on 2026-09-23** (owner: "I dont need cards anymore as I have promo"). It offered three data cards — Movers, Chart, Advice — at one shape, with controls in a bar rather than a studio panel. **Nothing was lost by deleting it**: `/promo` carries the identical guard (signed-in, not guest), is linked from the screener bar for every member, and is a strict superset — every template the member room had, plus the other two artboard sizes, the crop guide and the owner-only Announcement card. Keeping two hosts for one module meant every card change had to be checked twice. The page, its route, its `GATED_PAGES` entry and its three links (the Research picker, and two rows on `/admin`) all went; **`cards.js` and `cardshot.js` stay** — they are the module and the rasteriser, and the studio and the phone both depend on them. Guests were refused by both pages and are unaffected.
