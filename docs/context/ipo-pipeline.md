# The IPO pipeline (`/ipos`) — 2026-11-02

**The owner, the day Anthropic, PBC amended its S-1:** "Can we add anthropic as a ticker and get all the new pre-ipo filings .. may be create a new theme". Then: "Theme should be new listing".

## Why it is a page and not tickers
A company in registration has no price, so Twelve Data has no bars or profile for it. A universe row would be empty, `backfill-bars.js` would have nothing to load, and every price refresh would trip on it; a bare lookup of the proposed symbol could also resolve to a foreign listing. So companies in registration live on `/ipos` (members, as `/filings` is) and never touch the universe, the snapshot or the Signal.

**The theme is "New Listing".** When a company on the page starts trading, adding its ticker to the universe stays the owner's call (as for any ticker); when the owner adds one, it goes in under the theme `New Listing`, starting with ANT. The theme is empty until then.

## How it works (`ipos.js`, pure; wired in `server.js`)
- **Found from the daily index the morning filings check already reads** (`secdaily.js`, `/api/sec/daily`). As each day's index is fetched, its S-1, S-1/A, F-1, F-1/A, 424B4, 424B1, EFFECT and RW lines are queued (app_meta `ipo_pipeline`, de-duplicated by accession) **before** the check moves past the day. After the check, `ipoStep` takes up to `IPO_N` (4) companies off the queue each call; the route's `done` holds while the queue is non-empty, so the nightly's existing loop works it down. **No change to `nightly-ping.js` or the Fly machine.** A 429 anywhere stops the IPO step with the queue kept.
- **Who comes in.** Only an S-1, S-1/A, F-1 or F-1/A of its own brings in a company not seen before. EFFECT, RW and 424B go out for every kind of registration (a public company's S-3 or S-4 among them): the dry run found 16,868 lines from 5,237 companies since 2025, 8,014 of them EFFECT notices. Those forms only update a company already on the list.
- **Who is kept** (`judge`): not SIC 6770 (blank-cheque companies), and no 10-K, 10-Q, 20-F or 40-F filed **before the S-1 or F-1 that brought it here** (the trigger: the earliest queued one). A company with reports before that was already public: a follow-on or resale registration (Traws Pharma's S-1 of 2026-10-26), or one that listed before the period read and registers more shares now. The first version judged on the company's first registration statement instead, which kept companies that listed in 2022 and filed a resale S-1 in 2025 (1,469 kept); the rule above keeps 920. A company that has listed since its trigger (Figma) still counts. A company left out is stored with `tracked = 0` and its reason, so its next filing costs no request.
- **The older filings** (`olderFiles`, `mergeOlder`). The submissions file's `recent` list stops at about a thousand filings; the rest are in further files under `files`. CoreWeave's trading reports had pushed its March 2025 S-1 out of `recent`, so it was first stored with one EFFECT notice. When `recent` starts after the day needed, the older files covering it are read too: for a new company, a year before its trigger (so a report before the S-1 is seen however busy the list); for a kept one, back to its first filing stored.
- **What is kept**: the whole registration history from the submissions file (`HISTORY_FORMS`: DRS and DRS/A, which the SEC releases once the S-1 is public, S-1/F-1 and amendments, MEF, 424B4/424B1, EFFECT, RW), one `ipo_filings` row each, `insert or ignore`, never removed.
- **Status** (`status`): `listed` (the SEC lists a ticker **and** a final prospectus is on file, or, with none, as in a direct listing, the effectiveness notice is newer than the last registration statement: the SEC can hold a ticker before trading starts, and 81 companies with one were still amending their S-1 or F-1, Tracx Logis with a printed range among them), `priced` (a final prospectus), `withdrawn` (RW after the last registration statement), `filed` (a public S-1/F-1), `confidential` (released drafts only). Priced companies without a ticker are re-read once a day (`rechecks`), so the first trading day shows without waiting for another filing.
- **The cover page** (`parseCover`) of the newest final prospectus, else the newest public S-1/F-1: proposed symbol (read only next to "under the symbol"), the market from the 300 characters before it (`MARKETS`: the earliest named, the longer name winning where two start at the same place; bare "Nasdaq" and "NYSE" last, as SpaceX's cover has only "Nasdaq"; OTC tiers marked `OTC`), the price range ("between $X and $Y"; blank until printed), the final price, and the new shares the company itself offers. **The SEC ignores a Range header**, so `secHead` reads the stream to 600 KB and drops it; Anthropic's cover is in the first 200 KB of a 14 MB document. A cover is read only when its filing changes, and a cover without a range does not blank one printed earlier.
- **Storage** (`db.js`): `ipo_companies` (one row a company, replaced as it changes; `idx_ipo_companies_tracked`), `ipo_filings` (one row a filing, kept). Both small; not on `query-plan-test.js`'s big list. Not per-symbol, so not in `SYMBOL_TABLES`.
- **API** `/api/ipos` (members, 403 for guests): every kept company with its filings and sec.gov links, cached per instance until the queue's stamp (`at`) changes. `tracked` there means the ticker is in our universe, so the page links `/stock/<T>`.
- **The page** `private/ipos.html`: tabs In registration · Priced · Listed · Withdrawn · All, "Names an exchange" on by default (the exchange the SEC lists for a trading company, else the prospectus's; hides the many small self-underwritten offerings with no national listing), search; a company's filings open under its row. A registration with no filing for nine months is marked amber. Linked from the screener menu (`iposLink`, hidden from guests) and the island of `/ipos` links back to `/filings`.

## The first fill (`ipo-load.js`)
Reads every daily index from `--from` (default 2025-01-02), queues the lines and works the queue with the same `Ipos.step`. Dry run by default; `--commit` writes; `--commit --resume` works a stored queue without re-reading the indexes (after a 429); `--commit --recheck` judges every company again from scratch and writes each verdict over the old one (run once on 2026-11-02 after the trigger rule and the older filings came in: 920 kept, 1,125 left out, 773 of them already public and 352 SPACs); `--restatus [--commit]` works each kept company's status out again from what is stored, with no SEC requests (run after the listed rule: 81 listed to filed, 5 to withdrawn). Sequential, 400 ms between requests. The first fill took about 45 minutes.

As filled on 2026-11-02, the companies naming an exchange: 201 in registration, 8 priced, 411 listed, 47 withdrawn. Known names checked: Anthropic (filed, ANT, Nasdaq Global Select), SpaceX (SPCX, $135), Figma (FIG, $33), CoreWeave (CRWV, $40, nine filings), Circle Internet (CRCL), Chime (CHYM, $27), StubHub (STUB, $23.50), Klarna, Medline, Cerebras (all listed). Klarna's price is not read from its cover; a listed or priced company without one shows a dash.

## Tests
`ipos-test.js` (49 checks, no network, no database). Each guard was reverted once and caught: the follow-on rule and its trigger day, the SPAC rule, the older filings read, the market-next-to-the-symbol rule, the range kept across covers, the remembered company costing no request, a new company coming in only on its own S-1 or F-1, and the listed rule.

The route was tested end to end on the real server over an in-memory database with the SEC's addresses faked (anonymous 401 and sign-in redirect, guest 403 and redirect home, the daily route queueing and working the lines, a company left out costing nothing on a second run, the payload). Run with one company a call, removing `if (!ipo.done) out.done = false` left the queue unworked, which is what keeps the nightly's loop calling.
