## Which dates the fundamentals actually describe (2026-09-30, owner's request)
**A caption under the Fundamentals heading on `/stock`, naming every date that gives those figures a period:** the trailing-twelve-month period they cover, the fiscal year end where it is not December, when the company last reported, when it reports next, and when we pulled the profile. Plus two rows — **Dividend paid** and **Last split**. The owner's words were *"we don't show the Fundamental reporting date"* and then *"show all the dates that are meaningful"*; **"Keep Edgar out of this"** scoped it to the vendor's own fields, so the SEC card is untouched.

> Trailing twelve months to 31 May 2026 · fiscal year ends 28 Aug · reported 24 Jun 2026, after hours · next due 12 Nov 2026 · pulled 2 days ago

- **EVERY FIELD IS FREE.** `most_recent_quarter` and `fiscal_year_ends` ride on the `/statistics` call `fetchProfile` already makes and already pays 50 credits for; `dividend_date` and `last_split_date` sit beside the `ex_dividend_date` that was already being kept. **A day not recorded is gone for good** — the API only ever returns today's numbers — which is the same argument that took the profile from 16 stored fields to 58.
- **THE FOUR NEW FIELDS ARE DELIBERATELY NOT IN `emptyProfile()`, AND THAT IS THE WHOLE DECISION.** `PROFILE_FIELDS = Object.keys(emptyProfile())` and `profileGaps()` flags any stored profile MISSING one — so declaring them would make **all 1,188 profiles a `fields` gap at once**, and the nightly expires gaps before it rotates: ~170 rounds, ~2.9 hours against a 90-minute timeout, **~96,000 credits**, and a failed run. The `earningsRows` precedent exactly, and the `instrumentType` entry records the same arithmetic. The cost is that they are **null on every existing profile and fill over the ordinary 7-day rotation** — until then the caption shows only *reported / next due / pulled*, with no TTM period. **Proved by reverting**: declaring them fails 5.
- **A PROFILE FIELD DOES NOT REACH THE ROW UNLESS `computeStocks` NAMES IT.** It copies profile fields one by one rather than spreading, which shipped `instrumentType` blank for ever once already. All five are named explicitly, and the suite asserts it. **Proved by reverting**: dropping one line fails 1.
- **`most_recent_quarter` IS A PERIOD, NOT A FILING DATE** — measured before it was wired to anything: the announcement lands **23-29 days after** the vendor's date across the universe, so labelling it "reported" would be wrong by a month. It is *Trailing twelve months to <date>*, which is also what the figures beside it actually are. The announcement is a different fact and comes from `earnings_history`.
- **The vendor rounds a 52/53-week fiscal calendar to month-end** — MU and NVDA differ from their filings by 2-5 days. Harmless as a period label, and the reason the caption never claims to be a filing date.
- **The fiscal year end is shown only when it is NOT December**, since for most companies it says nothing. That is the one line that changes how every other figure should be read for the ~15% it applies to.
- **`readEarnings` RENAMES ON THE WAY OUT** — the table's `d` and `reported` become `date` and `time` on the payload. Reading the stored names gives `undefined`, and the announcement clause then **silently vanishes** rather than erroring. **Proved by reverting**: fails 3.
- **Dates are parsed at LOCAL NOON** (`+'T12:00:00'`), because `new Date('2026-05-31')` is UTC midnight and renders as the 30th west of Greenwich — the card-dating lesson, met again.
- **`captions` is a new `buildSections` option**, so a caption belongs to the page that knows the dates rather than to `FIELD_SPEC`. Opt-in: only `/stock` passes it, and the hover card and the tiles are unchanged.
- Verified: **32 checks** — the caption's clauses present and absent (a December year end omitted, a stock with no quarter showing no TTM clause), both new rows, the five fields on the row, and the four absent from `PROFILE_FIELDS`. **Proved by reverting four times**, every one load-bearing.
  - **A FIELD_SPEC row always renders with an em-dash; only an `extra` row is omitted.** An assertion that a missing dividend date draws nothing was testing the wrong contract.
  - **`period_type` is `'Q'`, not `'quarterly'`** — a probe filtering on the long word returned 0 rows and briefly read as empty data over a healthy 269,597.

### The same date on the SCREENER — a Reported column and a Reported row (2026-09-30, owner's request)
**"Can we show that date … on the screener as well in the fundamental section."** Both, at the owner's choice when asked: a **`Reported` column last in the Fundamentals group** (96 → 97 columns) and a **`Reported` row leading the `fund` group in `FIELD_SPEC`**, which puts it in the hover card, the tiles, the phone and `/compare` at once.

- **NO BACKFILL AND NO ROTATION WAIT, unlike the four fields shipped that morning.** `lastEarningsDate` has been in `emptyProfile()` all along, so every stored profile carries it, and **`computeStocks` already named it** — it was simply never displayed, surfacing only inside the Next Earn cell's *tooltip* (`· last 2026-06-24, …`) and as the screen-only `daysSinceEarnings` key. The whole feature is display.
- **It is in FUNDAMENTALS rather than beside Next Earn in Info, and that is the point of the request.** The two are a pair of dates, but this one is a **property of the numbers it stands with**: it says when the margins, growth and multiples beside it were struck. That is also why the `FIELD_SPEC` row LEADS its group rather than joining the dividend dates at the foot — a reader should see how old the figures are before reading them.
- **AMBER PAST 100 DAYS**, where the company has missed a normal quarterly cadence and everything in the group is a quarter stale — the colour's existing meaning here (*notice this, it is old*), the Price pulled column's rule. `.warn` is colour-only, so the cell stays a table cell; there is a check on that and the threshold is **probed at 100 AND 101**, since a threshold is a step function.
- **THE YEAR JOINS THE LABEL ONLY WHEN IT IS NOT THIS ONE** (`Jul 27 '25`). A two-year-old date reading `Jun 24` beside one from this quarter is exactly the kind of thing nobody notices is wrong — the Price pulled cell's same bargain with the time of day.
- **THE FILTER COUNTS DAYS SINCE, mirroring Next Earn's days-to**: a raw date string is nothing anyone can type against, the `pricedAt` rule. **TWO NAMES, ONE EXPRESSION** — `daysSinceEarnings` is the screen-only key the starter screens already filter on and `lastEarningsDate` is the column's, so a second copy of that arithmetic would be the drift `filters.js` exists to prevent.
- **SORTING NEEDED NO SPECIAL CASE, and that is luck worth asserting.** `valueFor` falls through to the raw field and an ISO date sorts lexically, so descending really is most-recent-first — the News column needed a branch only because `newsAge` is not a field. There is a check, because the day the stored format changes that silently inverts.

#### `/stock` STATES IT BETTER, SO THE ROW STANDS ASIDE THERE — `buildSections({ skip })`
A `FIELD_SPEC` row reaches `/stock` too, where the Fundamentals caption **already** says `reported 24 Jun 2026, after hours` — so the first cut printed the same date twice, six lines apart. Worse than untidy: **the two can genuinely disagree.** The caption reads `earnings_history`, rewritten every refresh round and carrying the time of day; the row reads the PROFILE's copy, which lags by up to the **7-day rotation**. Two different dates under one label is worse than either alone.

- **`skip` is a new `buildSections` option** — a list of `group|label` keys a page drops because it states the same fact better elsewhere. Only `/stock` passes it, for `fund|Reported`, the way only `/stock` passes `extra` and `captions`.
- **The caption was NOT trimmed instead.** That was the other candidate and it is the worse one: it would have left the page's single statement coming from the *staler* of two sources, and lost the time of day with it.
- **Proved by reverting**: dropping the `skip` draws `ReportedSep 20` directly under a caption already saying it.

#### "NEXT DUE 8 SEPT 2026" THREE WEEKS AFTER IT (2026-09-30, owner, from a screenshot of Loews)
**`/stock/L` read `reported 9 Jun 2026, after hours · next due 8 Sept 2026 · pulled 4 days ago` on 30 September.** Two faults in the caption, both mine, and the second only possible because of the first.

- **`8 Sept 2026` WAS NEVER A VENDOR DATE — it is our own arithmetic.** `fetchProfile` falls back to `last report + 91 days` when `/earnings` offers no upcoming one, and 2026-06-09 + 91 is exactly 2026-09-08. The row carries `nextEarningsEstimated: true` and **the Next Earn column has always marked that with a leading `~`** (`~Sep 8`, visible in the same screenshot, four inches to the left). The caption dropped the distinction and stated the estimate as fact.
- **AND A DATE IN THE PAST CANNOT BE "NEXT DUE".** Measured on the live screen the same evening: **51 of 1,188 rows carry a next-earnings date that has already passed, and EVERY ONE of them is an estimate — 0 confirmed.** That is the whole diagnosis in one number: a vendor-confirmed date never goes stale, our +91 guess lapses the moment the real report slips past it. 42 rows also last reported over 100 days ago, so the new amber column was already flagging most of them.
- **The replacement clause is about OUR RECORD, not about the company**, which may well have reported without the vendor telling us: `due about 8 Sept 2026, not yet recorded`. A future estimate keeps "next due" and gains the same hedge — `next due about 19 Oct 2099`. A confirmed date is not hedged, and there is a check for that, or the word would spread to every row.
- **THE ESTIMATE ITSELF IS DELIBERATELY NOT CHANGED, and that is a boundary rather than laziness.** `nextEarningsDate` is read by the Advice engine's earnings guard, by the Next Earn column and by the "Reporting in 14 days" screen — suppressing a lapsed one at source would move verdicts. **New data may feed display surfaces but must never feed the live Advice calculation**, and the inverse holds too: a display fault is fixed on the display. Worth raising separately; several of the 51 are **funds** (DRAM, ITA, KWEB, SOXX, BUG), which file no earnings at all and should arguably never have been given a date.
- **The vendor's record for L is independently thin** — 2026-06-09, 2026-04-30, then a **three-year hole back to 2023-04-30** — and the profile was pulled 3 days before the report, so this is the vendor's own gap rather than our staleness. Nothing to fix here; worth knowing before reading a long "not yet recorded" as a bug in the app.
- Verified inside `funddate-test.js`, that caption's own suite, which went 32 → **39 checks**: two new fixtures, a LAPSED estimate carrying the Loews shape exactly and a FUTURE one, plus a check that the confirmed date is not hedged. **Proved by reverting twice**: restoring the unconditional `next due` fails 2, and dropping the estimate hedge fails 2.
  - **The heredoc ate a backslash level for the FOURTH time on this project** — `'\n-- …'` in a patch script became a real newline inside a JS string literal and the file would not parse. Use Write/Edit for anything carrying an escape.

#### THE VENDOR'S EARNINGS FEED GOES STALE, AND OUR OWN FILINGS PROVE IT (2026-10-01, owner, from a Google Finance screenshot of VNO)
**"Our system says last reported 4-May-2026 whereas Google Finance says 3-Aug-2026."** Correct, and it is the vendor rather than us.

- **`/earnings?symbol=VNO` returns 27 rows whose newest is 2026-05-04.** Twelve Data simply does not have VNO's Q2. **The ordering hypothesis was WRONG and was checked first**: `reported` uses `arr.find`, which assumes newest-first where `upcoming` on the next line sorts — but the array IS newest-first and `find` picks exactly what a sort would. Measured before changing anything.
- **OUR SEMANTICS ARE RIGHT, which the filings confirm**: EDGAR says VNO's 10-Q for the quarter ended 2026-03-31 was filed **2026-05-04** — the same date the feed gives. `lastEarningsDate` really is the announcement; the feed is one quarter behind on this symbol. `/statistics.most_recent_quarter` meanwhile reads **2026-06-30**, so the vendor knows about Q2 on one endpoint and not the other.
- **Measured against our own stored EDGAR copy**: of the 43 rows whose last report is over 100 days old, **15 are genuinely behind**, 3 agree and 25 file nothing at all (funds, foreign filers). **A control group of 25 recent reporters: 0 behind, 22 agree** — so the check marks a real gap rather than everything.

##### The threshold is 40 days because the data left an empty bucket there
The gap between the SEC filing date and the vendor's announcement date, over 1,094 comparable symbols, is **bimodal**: 950 inside a week, 117 at 8-20, 11 at 21-35 (banks and retailers that announce in mid-July and file in early August), **nothing at all between 36 and 45**, then 16 symbols from 46 to 197 — every one a missing quarter. 40 sits in the gap.

- **A LOWER THRESHOLD FLAGS NORMAL BEHAVIOUR, and the first cut did.** At 20 it marked BLK, PNC, USB, UNH, SYY and DDS, whose feeds are perfectly current — they had simply announced three weeks before filing. **Only production showed that**: the fixture's three-population shape was written afterwards, so the suite now carries LAGY (+22, must NOT be marked) beside BHND (+91, must be).
- **MU validates it from the other side**: announced 2026-06-24 after hours, 10-Q filed 2026-06-25. One day. A naive "any difference" check would have flagged a symbol that is exactly in sync.
- `RowCard.SEC_BEHIND_DAYS`, shared — the screener's column and `/stock`'s caption must agree about what "behind" means, and two copies drift the first time one is tuned.

##### THE BOUNDARY, which the owner overrode knowingly and which still holds where it matters
The owner was offered "flag it on /stock only" and chose **"flag it on the screener too"**, with the cost named: the SEC card's own rule is *no snapshot stamping, no screener column*. What that rule is really protecting is the **verdict**, and that is intact:

- **IT IS A SEPARATE ENDPOINT FETCHED AFTER THE TABLE PAINTS** (`/api/sec/filed`), the `loadLatestNews` bargain — so nothing is written to the snapshot and the filing date is **never on the row the server scores**. The engine cannot see it even in principle.
- **`lastEarningsDate` IS NOT TOUCHED.** It derives `nextEarningsDate`, which the Advice engine's earnings guard reads, so a filing date assigned to it would be one rename from moving verdicts. The cell still shows the VENDOR's date and names the filing in its tooltip; the column, the filter and `daysSinceEarnings` all keep meaning one thing.
- **Asserted behaviourally, not by grep**: every row scored three ways — field absent, present, and **LYING** — and the verdicts must be identical, the proof `instrumentType` had to give. Plus a check that no served row carries `secLastFiled`.

##### Where the date comes from, and why no scan
`sec_state` gained **`last_filed`**, written by `writeSecFacts`, which already has the rows — so knowing it costs nothing at either end and `/api/sec/filed` is one small read of a ~1,200-row table. **Never `max(filed) group by symbol` over sec_facts**, which is 270,000 rows and a quota event on this database. Statement forms only (`isStatement` — an 8-K or a proxy is a mention, the rule `outranks` already enforces on the card), and `/api/sec` sends `lastFiled` computed the same way so the page needs no second copy of the form list.

- **`noteSecMiss` must NOT blank it.** A symbol that could not be fetched this time still holds the filings it had, and a transient SEC refusal reading as "this company has filed nothing" is the mistake that function already made once with the CIK, silently breaking the insider card for 64 filers.
- **`backfill-sec-filed.js`** fills the ~1,097 symbols stored before the column existed — local, dry run by default, because it reads the whole of sec_facts. Measured: **1,176 symbols, 1,097 set, 79 with no statements, 99.7s.** It writes through a targeted `setSecLastFiled`, never `writeSecFacts`, which replaces every fact row — the screens-incident rule that a restore must not pass through a transforming path.

##### Three scope traps in one change, all the same one
- **`payload` is a local of the boot IIFE**, so `paintFundCaption` calling `fundDates(stock, payload)` threw into `loadSec`'s catch and the clause silently never appeared. `fundDates` takes what it reads now (`earnings`, `pulledAt`, `filed`).
- **`secData` is an IIFE local too**, so a script-scope `secLastFiled()` helper reading it saw `undefined` — the same trap one level deeper, in the same hour. The filing date is **passed into** `paintFundCaption` now; nothing reaches for scope.
- **`window.baseStocks` is not a global** either, in the sibling suite. Three instances of one lesson: *in these pages, pass it in.*
- Verified: **17 checks** over a fixture carrying all three live populations (a quarter behind, the ordinary announce-to-file lag, in sync) plus a fund that files nothing. **Proved by reverting six times**: the 40-day threshold fails 2, no threshold 2, not fetching 2, replacing the vendor date 2, the caption clause 1, `lastFiled` on /api/sec 1. Plus `sec` 58, `funddate` 39, `reported` 35, `field-shape` 7 and `cols` 42 re-run unchanged, and `query-plan-test` still passes.

#### A pre-existing defect found in the markup beside it
**`data-key="nextEarningsDate" ="Next earnings date — …"`** — the word `title` had been **eaten**, so the Next Earn column had carried **no tooltip at all** and the header held an attribute literally named `=`. The shell-mangling trap this file already records for CLAUDE.md prose (`activeDim()` and two test filenames vanishing mid-sentence), in markup this time. Repaired, with a check that the tooltip is back and that no ` ="` remains in the header row.

- Verified: **35 checks**, over a fixture dated in **offsets from today** rather than literals so it cannot rot, with the two stocks either side of the amber line at exactly 100 and 101 days and **symbol order deliberately disagreeing with date order**, so a sort that did nothing could not pass by tidiness. **Proved by reverting nine times**, every one load-bearing: the body cell fails 8, the banner at 18 fails 2, `PAD_SPAN` 1, the year suffix 1, `>= 100` 1, the `title` repair 2, the `FIELD_SPEC` row 5, the local-noon parse 2, the `/stock` skip 1. Plus `field-shape` 7, `cols` 42, `funddate` 32, `filter`, `stockcard` 31, `compare` 71, `peers` 73 and `mobile` 99 re-run unchanged.
  - **A REAL OFF-BY-ONE THE FIXTURE CAUGHT.** The filter did `new Date(x.lastEarningsDate)` — **UTC midnight**, so west of Greenwich it lands on the previous day and every count came out a day high: the cell's tooltip said *10 days ago* while a `>10` filter returned that very row. The card-dating lesson, one line apart from a cell that already parsed at local noon. It had been live under `daysSinceEarnings` since that key existed, so the **"Drifting after a beat" screen's `0..21` window shifts by a day** — a correction, not a regression.
  - **`PAD_SPAN` was the one revert that cost nothing**, so it got a check rather than being left unproven. It is deliberately **static**: the value is the colspan `showLoading()` gives the Refresh-all progress line and the windowed spacer, neither of which is on screen in a five-row fixture — and it went stale at 51 once with nothing noticing.
  - **Two test faults worth keeping.** `window.baseStocks` is a local of the page's boot IIFE, not a global — the documented `payload` trap; both `Filters.filterValue` and `RowCard.fieldValues` are pure, so the rows go in as arguments. And a `FIELD_SPEC` getter returning `null` is **correct** — the row still renders with an em-dash, and only an `extra` row is omitted — so the contract to assert is what `buildSections` draws, not what `fieldValues` returns.
  - `&shy;` in the header was removed before shipping: it never broke the line, and `columnCatalogue()` resolves entities into the label `/columns` searches on. The catalogue picked the column up on its own, **89 → 90**.
