## Similar stocks — the peer table at the foot of `/stock` (2026-09-26, owner's request)
**Up to six companies in the same industry, closest by market cap, with price / today / 1Y / market cap / forward P/E / the Balanced verdict and a `vs` pill into `/compare`.** The stock page was a dead end: you arrive from the screener and the only way sideways is a picker listing 1,185 names alphabetically.

- **FORWARD P/E IS THE ONE COLUMN THAT DOES NOT TAKE `FIELD_SPEC`'s ANSWER** (added 2026-09-26, owner's request). `['fund','Fwd P/E', V.num(s.forwardPe)]` prints a loss-maker's **-41.7** verbatim, which is tolerable in a labelled row on one stock and actively misleading **down a column of six**, where the eye reads the most negative number as the cheapest. Live, **73 of 1,167** companies carry one, and the first real screenshot found one in the top six: APTV's Auto Parts peers read 10.4 / 18.0 / 7.2 / **Aurora at -13.5** / 10.7 / 7.5. A non-positive one is a dash and the note says why — **a multiple off a loss is arithmetic, not cheapness**, the Size card's rule and the Bubble card's, met a third time. `> 0` rejects the empty before coercing in the same expression, since `Number(null)` is 0. **Proved by reverting**: without the guard four checks fail and the column prints `-41.7`.
  - **This is a deliberate divergence from the hover card, `/stock`'s own field list and `/compare`, which all still print the negative.** Left alone rather than fixed globally: in a labelled single-stock row "Fwd P/E −41.7" reads as *this company loses money*, and changing `FIELD_SPEC` would move five surfaces for a request about one. Worth revisiting as its own decision.

#### The stock itself is the first row (2026-09-29, owner's request)
**"I want to see a line for Bristol-Myers on top in the similar stock section."** Without it the reader compares six companies against a seventh they have to hold in their head, scrolling back up the page for each number — which is most of the work this table exists to save.

- **ONE ROW BUILDER, and that is the whole value of the row.** `peerRow` was extracted from the `rows` map and is used for the anchor and the peers both. Formatting the anchor separately would leave the one comparison this table exists for running between two different formatters — the drift `RowCard.fieldValues` is here to prevent — and it would look perfectly correct on screen. The **Fwd P/E guard therefore reaches the anchor too**, which is the sharpest place for it yet: a loss-maker reading `-41.7` at the head of the column it is meant to be compared against is worse than the same number anywhere else.
- **It is NOT a peer and is counted as one nowhere.** `total` already excluded the anchor's own display name, `seen` already held it before the pick loop, and the caption still reads *"Others in Drug Manufacturers - General … 12 in the industry"* — which stays true because the extra row is visibly *this stock* rather than another one.
- **Three differences from a peer row, and no fourth**: the tint, a name that is **not a link to the page you are already on**, and **no `vs` pill** — a stock cannot be compared with itself. The cell is still emitted so the columns line up; reverting that produces `/compare/ANCH/ANCH`, which is the argument.
- **A TINT, nothing louder.** Every colour on this page already means something — green and red are direction, amber is *notice this*, the accent is a link — and "this is the stock you are looking at" is not a reading about the data. It is the hovered-row treatment, held on, plus a `this stock` chip.
- **THE CHIP COST THE PHONE ITS NUMBERS, and the existing suite caught it.** It is ~70px of a 390px screen, and it pushed Today off the visible box — the exact fault the 116px name truncation was written for. Hidden under 620px, where the tint says which row it is anyway. `.self` also takes the same `max-width` box as `.namelink`, or the one row that is not a link is the one row that breaks the column.
- **An older server sends no `self`** and the table simply opens with the peers, so deploy order is not a thing to think about.
- Verified inside the existing 56-check peers suite rather than beside it — it is that table's own property. **Four of its checks had to CHANGE rather than be worked around**: "the six the API chose" now skips `tr.me`, the Fwd P/E column carries seven values, the loss-maker moved a row down, and the phone draws seven. **Proved by reverting four times**: dropping `self` fails 9, the page not drawing it fails 9, keeping the `vs` pill fails 2, and not hiding the chip on a phone fails 2 — including Today back off the screen at 353px against a 333px box.
  - **A test fault worth keeping**: the new checks first compared the drawn name against `p.self.name`, but `p` had been reassigned by the cases above, so ANCH's row was being checked against the no-cap anchor's name. A variable reused across cases is not a payload.

- **IT COSTS NOTHING, which is what settled it.** `/api/stock` already holds the whole snapshot in memory to find the stock and build that picker, so peers are a filter over rows in hand. Asserted rather than claimed: the test wraps `global.__client` and counts round trips, and a request that builds a group makes **exactly as many as one that does not** (2 vs 2).
- **"Similar" is defined and the page SAYS which definition it used.** Same industry, ranked by the **log ratio** of market cap — caps here span six orders of magnitude, so a linear distance means "the other trillion-dollar companies" for a mega-cap and "everything under a billion" for a small one. Measured on the live 1,185: **909 stocks have 6+ industry peers, 174 have 3-5, 66 have 1-2, 14 are alone**, so the `PEER_MIN_INDUSTRY` floor of 3 widens **80 stocks (7%)** to their sector and leaves 93% on the finer cut. A widened group prints *"Only 1 other in Uranium, so this is Energy instead"* rather than silently answering a different question.
- **A FUND IS NOT A COMPANY, AND THE REFUSAL HAS TO RUN BOTH WAYS** (owner: "no peer group needed for etfs"). Refusing it only as an ANCHOR is the bug that shipped in the first run: a sector fund is classified into the industry it tracks, so `FUND1` sat fifth in the fixture's Semiconductors group at a "$6B market cap" that is really **AUM**. `companyType === 'ETF'` now excludes it from the pool as well. Live, that is SMH, SOXX, QQQ and fifteen others.
- **No peer table for a GUEST** (owner's call). The preview is twenty stocks, so a filtered group would be nought to two rows of a table whose whole claim is *here is the rest of the industry* — and one more route that has to remember to narrow.
- **Formatted through `RowCard.fieldValues()` server-side**, the `mobileRow()` pattern, so a number cannot read one way here and another on the row it came from — and the payload is ~1KB rather than the ~18KB six whole snapshot rows would cost.
- **IT MUST NOT BECOME A RECOMMENDATION.** No ordering by return, no tick on a "winner", and the note says so: *"not a ranking, and not a list of better alternatives"*. That is the line the rest of the product declines to cross, and a peer table is the easiest place to cross it by accident.
- 22 rows carry no industry yet and four of those carry no sector either; they simply draw no section. **A heading with an empty table would be worse than no heading.**
- Verified: **56 checks**. The fixture anchors at a MID-SIZED $50B with the industry's biggest company five rows down, so **alphabetical, largest-first and same-sector-ignoring-industry each fail**; plus the sector fallback, an anchor with no cap of its own (largest first, and the payload says so), the round-trip count, the formatter against `fieldValues` directly, and the page at 1500px and 390px. **Proved by reverting** three times — the unfiltered pool puts the fund fifth, and dropping the phone rule fails all three phone checks.
  - **A screenshot found both layout faults, as usual here**: `.rng` never resets `text-decoration`, so the `vs` pill arrived underlined; and at 390px a full legal name took the whole viewport with every number off-screen. Below 620px the name link truncates to 116px and the compare column stands aside.
  - **A short fixture name made two of those assertions vacuous.** With `Near One` in place of a real 39-character legal name they passed with the truncation reverted — caught only because the revert-proof was run. **A width assertion needs input at the length the provider actually returns.**
