## Email
**Every message goes through one shell.** `emailShell()` and `textShell()` in `server.js` wrap a dark header carrying the wordmark, a white card, and a footer with the tagline, the site link and a one-line note saying why this particular message arrived — the question a reader asks first about mail they did not expect. Four messages use it: the welcome, the password reset, the contact form and the refresh report.

- **Tables and inline styles, not the app's CSS.** Outlook renders through Word, most clients strip `<style>` blocks, and neither flexbox nor grid is available. The wordmark is text rather than an image, because images are blocked by default in most clients and a logo would leave a broken box exactly where the brand should be.
- **The palette is for a light background**, not lifted from the app. Mail clients invert or ignore dark themes unpredictably, and a screenshot-black email arrives unreadable somewhere.
- **`mailButton()` wraps the pill in a table cell** — Outlook ignores `border-radius` on an `<a>`, so the radius goes on the cell.
- **The From display name comes from `BRAND`, not from `MAIL_FROM`.** `fromHeader()` keeps only the address out of that variable, so renaming the product cannot leave a stale name sitting in everyone's inbox — which is exactly what happened when Ticker Lab became Tickr Lab.
- **A hidden preheader repeats the intro**, so the grey line clients print beside the subject is the real first sentence rather than whatever markup happens to come first.
- **The Instagram handle is `BRAND_IG` (`tickr_lab`), on the tagline's bargain** (2026-09-26): defined once in server.js for the mail shells, restated as text on the five static public pages because they cannot read a server constant, and **`tagline-test.js` names any page that drifts** — the day the handle changes, four footers would otherwise point at a profile that no longer exists. Stored **without the `@`**, so the one place a URL is built cannot get it into the path; the `@` is display and every surface adds it. **Always an absolute `https://` link, never a bare handle** — a mail client will not linkify `@tickr_lab` and the reader will not type it, and the **plain-text half is the one that gets forgotten**, where a bare handle is simply a dead string. Every public anchor carries `rel="me noopener"`: `me` is what lets the site and the profile verify each other.
- **THE WELCOME EMAIL IS OFF (2026-09-26, the owner's call while opening registration).** `SEND_WELCOME=true` turns it back on; the shell, the copy and the owner/member split are all kept, so this is a switch rather than a deletion. Every account created is one more message from a young sending domain that a corporate gateway is already quarantining, and a welcome is the least necessary mail here — the person is looking at the screener a second after reading it. **Gated INSIDE `sendWelcome` rather than at the two call sites**, so neither registration nor approval can start sending again by being edited. The approval request to the owner is a different decision and still goes.
  - **The copy stopped promising it in the same commit**: the login page said *"you will get a welcome email when you are in"* and `/help` said the same. A promise of mail that is no longer sent is the "every feature live" error wearing different clothes.
  - **Consequence worth knowing**: with approval ON, an approved member now gets no notification at all — they find out by trying to sign in. With approval off (the current setting) nobody is waiting, so it does not arise.
- **Registration sends two notes to two people**, both after the response and both with their rejections swallowed — the account and session already exist by then, so a slow or failing mail provider can never hold up or fail a sign-up. `sendWelcome()` greets the new member; `sendSignupNotice()` tells the operator, with `Reply-To` set to the new member so a welcome reply reaches them directly.
- **The sign-up notice is skipped for the very first account**, which becomes the owner — otherwise it is an email telling you that you have joined your own site. It is skipped again when the destination *is* the address that just registered. Both guards are verified. **Since 2026-09-14 it is the approval request** — subject "Approval needed", button to `/users` — and the welcome is sent by the approve route instead of the register route (the owner's own welcome still goes at registration).
- **`operatorEmail()` (`REPORT_TO`, else the owner account) resolves the destination for both the refresh report and the sign-up notice**, so the two cannot drift apart. `contactDestination()` stays separate because `CONTACT_TO` is a deliberately different inbox.

Sent through **Resend** over plain `fetch` — a REST call does not justify a fourth dependency. Needs `RESEND_API_KEY`, `MAIL_FROM` and `APP_URL`; without all three `MAIL_READY` is false and `/api/forgot` still returns its normal reply while logging that mail is unconfigured, so the endpoint never reveals the difference.

- **Sending is scoped to `mail.tickrlab.com`**, a subdomain, so a reputation problem cannot reach the domain the site is served from. SPF, DKIM and an `MX` for bounce feedback live under it; **DMARC sits at the root** (`_dmarc`), where one policy covers every subdomain.
- **DMARC is at `p=quarantine`** (checked against live DNS 2026-09-25; this line said `p=none` long after it had been tightened, so re-read the record rather than trusting it). `sp=` is unset, so subdomains inherit the policy, and alignment is relaxed by default — which is why a second sending subdomain needs no DMARC change.
- **The live records, so the next session does not have to dig them out**: SPF and the bounce MX sit on `send.mail.tickrlab.com` (`v=spf1 include:amazonses.com ~all`, `feedback-smtp.us-east-1.amazonses.com`), DKIM on `resend._domainkey.mail.tickrlab.com`, DMARC on `_dmarc.tickrlab.com`. `mail.tickrlab.com` itself carries neither SPF nor MX, which is Resend's current scheme and not a gap.
- **The Resend API key is restricted to SENDING.** `GET /domains` answers `401 restricted_api_key`, so domains cannot be listed or created through it — adding one is dashboard work by the owner, deliberately. Worth keeping: a leaked key cannot reshape the account.
- **`APP_URL` is `https://www.tickrlab.com`**, the canonical host — the bare domain 308s to it, and a link in an email should not depend on a redirect.
- **Nothing receives mail at the sending address.** Set `MAIL_REPLY_TO` to a real inbox, or add a forwarder (ImprovMX or Forward Email work with Vercel DNS; Cloudflare Email Routing does not, since it needs Cloudflare as the DNS host).
- Upstream errors are logged by type only, never echoed to the client — the response body can restate the request, and the key travels in the same headers.
- **`/contact` is signed-in only**, which is what makes it safe without a captcha or a honeypot: every sender is a known account and the address comes off the session rather than a form field. That is also why the address can be used as `Reply-To` — replying in a mail client answers the person, not the server.
- **The subject has CR and LF stripped, not escaped.** A newline in a header is how a subject line becomes extra headers; the body is the only place free text belongs. The subject is prefixed `[Tickr Lab]` and capped at 120 characters, the body at 4,000.
- **Destination is `CONTACT_TO`, falling back to the owner account's email**, so the form works even if the variable is never set.
- The daily cap reuses `chat_usage` under a `contact:` key prefix — that table is a generic per-key-per-day counter that happens to be named for its first caller.

### The email log — every message, recorded where they all pass (2026-09-25, owner's request)
**`/emails` (admin, an Email log row in the console's People & logs section) lists every message this app has handed to Resend.** It exists because the list's first real broadcast produced a question nothing here could answer: the confirmation reached the reader and the post did not, and the only record of either was a line in a Vercel log that had already rolled off.

- **WRITTEN AT THE CHOKE POINT, not at the call sites.** `sendMailResult` is the one function all eleven senders funnel through, so the log cannot lose whichever send somebody forgets to annotate — the only thing a caller supplies is `kind`. A log with holes in it is worse than no log, because it is read as if it were complete. The test asserts this **statically**: it parses every `sendMail`/`sendMailResult` call out of server.js and fails if one names no kind.
- **FACTS, NEVER CONTENT — the activity log's rule.** `mail_log` is `at, kind, to_addr, subject, ok, status, provider_id, error, bytes`, and there is deliberately **no column a body could go in**: bodies would be a liability to hold, enormous to store, and are not what the table is asked. The size is kept, because *accepted and two megabytes* is the shape of a deliverability problem. A test drives a marker string through a real send and then searches the whole table for it.
- **A FAILURE IS RECORDED, and that is the half that matters.** The naive version logs on success and turns the log into a record of what worked — hiding exactly the sends anyone opens this page to find. All four return paths write, the unconfigured one included, so "nothing is sending" is a page full of failed rows rather than a page that is empty for no stated reason.
- **The write is AWAITED, not fire-and-forget.** Nothing may run after the response on this platform, so a detached write is simply lost. It is one small insert against a send that already costs a network round trip, and a failure to log never fails the send — the bars rule, with a test that breaks `logMail` and checks the message still goes.
- **Two reads, deliberately.** The tail is capped at 500, so a total derived from it would quietly understate the moment there is more mail than the cap; the counts are a SQL `group by` over the whole window. The page says which is which.
- **"Accepted" is not "delivered", and the page says so rather than printing a tick that means more than it should.** The provider taking a message is the last thing this app can observe, which is why `provider_id` is on screen: it is what you quote to Resend.
- Kept `MAIL_LOG_KEEP_DAYS` (90), pruned beside `pruneRuns()`. `mail_log` is not in the query-plan test's big-table list — the tail seeks on `idx_mail_at` and the rollup is bounded by the same 90 days, the reasoning the activity and news rollups already use.
- **The outcome is the THIRD column, before the address.** It is what the page is opened for, and on a phone the first three are all that fit before the table has to be scrolled inside its own box. **An email address and a message id are single unbroken tokens**: breaking them to fit gave one character a line and a row 300px tall, so they are clipped with the whole value on the cell's `title`. Measured as DRAWN — the markup was right in both versions. **Proved by reverting**: `word-break: break-all` takes the row from 39px to 55px and fails two checks.
- Verified: **57 checks** (the static choke-point scan, the table's own columns, a real send end to end, three failure modes, a broken log still sending, the cap against the counts, the window, pruning, and the page — cards, the kind filter over a fixture with two kinds *inside* the tail, failures-only, the long-address row height, the phone) plus **18** on the roles (anon, guest and member refused the page AND the API, with the address checked absent from every refusal body) and **4** proving the scan catches both a removed kind and a newly added send that names none.

#### It earned its place the next morning, and overturned two diagnoses (2026-09-26)
**Reported as "I sent a simple text message to the Investcorp account but it did not get delivered."** The log answered it in one read: two messages six seconds apart, both accepted — `post-list` to `cmandal@investcorp.com` (id `01a0dd43-73bb-715c-b319-8854e5e0a28e`, 10.7 KB) and `post-test` to Gmail. Resend's dashboard then showed a **Delivered** event for the Investcorp one.

- **"DELIVERED" AND "NOT IN THE INBOX" ARE CONSISTENT — read this before diagnosing the next one.** Delivered means the receiving MTA answered 250; where the message is filed afterwards is a second decision by a different system, and Proofpoint, Mimecast and Defender all accept at the edge and quarantine internally. A gateway that distrusted the sender would have refused at SMTP or bounced, so a Delivered event **rules out SPF, DKIM, DMARC and domain reputation** — every one of which was the leading hypothesis an hour earlier, including mine.
- **It also killed the standing explanation for the FIRST failure.** That one was put down to 2 MB of images and 84 words of typo-laden placeholder; this message was 10.7 KB of clean prose and was filed the same way. **Content weight was never the cause**, and the note above this one used to imply it was.
- **`bytes` is why that could be settled in one glance**, which is the argument for keeping it: "accepted, and two megabytes" and "accepted, and ten kilobytes" are different stories and the column tells them apart without opening anything.
- **The provider id is what made the second half possible**, exactly as the column was built for — except that the **API key is send-only**, so `GET /emails/<id>` answers `401 restricted_api_key` and the delivery status is dashboard-only. Worth knowing before reaching for the API: the id is for a human to paste, not for this app to resolve.
- Two real defects surfaced and were **deferred by the owner** (Gmail delivers; a tenant's quarantine policy is not fixable from here) — the unset postal address and `MAIL_FROM` being `no-reply@`. Both are in [docs/backlog.md](docs/backlog.md) entry 10 with what each would take.
