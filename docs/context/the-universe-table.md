## The universe table — portfolios are groupings
**Since 2026-09-15 the stocks the screener tracks live in their own `universe` table (`symbol`, `added_at`); portfolios only group them.** Before, the universe was the union of the admin portfolios, so deleting a portfolio silently removed every stock only it held and purged their data — measured before the change: deleting Fin, Chips, Software and the industry-style lists would have taken ~150 of 271 stocks out. The owner wanted to delete those portfolios now that Sector and Industry cover them.

- **`store.readUniverse()` is the only source** — server (`readUniverse`), `purge-orphans.js`, `backfill-bars.js`. It returns the table **plus any portfolio member not in it** (belt and braces), ordered by `added_at`.
- **Filling it**: `init()` runs `insert or ignore into universe select distinct symbol from portfolio_tickers` on every cold start (idempotent, no marker — that is how the 271 arrived), and **`writePortfolios()` runs the same insert before it deletes the memberships**, so no portfolio write can drop a stock.
- **Routes**: deleting a portfolio and taking a ticker out of one never purge. `DELETE /api/tickers/:symbol` (the × in the All view) is the only way out: `removeFromUniverse()` deletes the universe row and every membership in one batch, then `purgeSymbol()`. `POST /api/universe {symbol, portfolio?}` adds a stock with an optional portfolio (the console's Add a ticker, "No portfolio — screener only" by default); adding to a portfolio still adds to the universe. Every portfolio answer (`portfolioAnswer()`) carries `{portfolios, universe}`, and the screener's `applyPortfolioMap(map, universe)` keeps a row for every universe stock.
- **Snapshot memberships are stamped on the way out** (`membershipOf` per row, `snap.portfolios` from the table) — a deleted portfolio would otherwise linger on every row until the next refresh.
- Verified against the real server on an in-memory node:sqlite in place of Turso (27 checks: delete a portfolio keeps stocks and data, remove-from-portfolio keeps, remove-from-screener purges and removes memberships and is not brought back by a later write, the add route's 409/404/400s, snapshot stamping) and the screener's reconciliation (5 checks).

##### I ALSO CLAIMED THEY CARRY NO MARKET CAP, AND THAT WAS WRONG (corrected 2026-09-30)
**The sentence used to end "…no size band (no market cap — a fund reports AUM), and below the day card's $1B mover floor. That holds after their profiles land." It does not.** A fund reports AUM and the provider returns it in `market_capitalization`, so the moment a profile lands the cap is populated. Measured after the Fill missing: **9 of the 11 carry one** — XLF $46.0B, XLK $43.7B, XLV $28.3B, XLI $23.6B, XLP $17.3B, XLY $14.3B, XLE $10.8B, XLU $7.5B, XLB $3.7B (XLC and XLRE not yet).

- **The error was measuring the wrong field.** The pre-add check read `sector` and `industry` on the 17 existing funds and found none; I extrapolated to the cap without checking it, and every fund I looked at happened to have no profile yet. **An extrapolation across fields is not a measurement**, and the giveaway was available: this file already says in the Size-band section that *"seven symbols legitimately have no market cap — all ETFs"*, which is a statement about symbols with no profile, not about funds.
- **What it actually costs, measured rather than feared**: the eleven are now **banded** (Mid-Large and Mid), so they appear in `/size/<band>`, in the pivot's Size dimension and in `/consolidated` grouped by Size; and nine of them clear the **day card's $1B mover floor**, so they can take a mover slot against real companies.
- **IT IS NOT NEW BEHAVIOUR, WHICH IS THE ONE MITIGATING FACT.** `SMH`, `SOXX` and `GLD` already did exactly this before the eleven existed — funds carrying a cap, banded, and eligible for a mover slot. The eleven take that from **3 to 12**. So this is a pre-existing wrinkle the addition made larger, not one it created.
- **`BENCHMARKS` still does not fix it and is still the wrong tool**: that list exists for the delete guard and the day card, and it would put a 409 on eleven rows the owner may want to prune.
- **The principled fix is now AVAILABLE and is deliberately not built**: `instrumentType === 'ETF'` identifies a fund directly, so the day card's movers and the size bands could exclude funds by what they ARE rather than by a hardcoded symbol list. Both are display decisions and both change what is on screen, so they are the owner's call. Noted here rather than done.
- **The general rule this leaves: when a claim rests on "the provider has none of X for this kind of instrument", check X itself, on rows that actually have a profile.** Every fund in the pre-add sample was unprofiled, which made the sample agree with the claim for the wrong reason.
- **SO THEY GET NO `BENCHMARKS` TREATMENT, and the asymmetry is the point.** That list does two jobs — the **delete guard** (409, because every group page draws the S&P and Dow lines, so dropping one would take the comparison off pages that have nothing to do with that row) and **removal from the day card's pool**. Neither applies: nothing draws an XLK line, so deleting one damages no other page, and the arithmetic above already keeps them out of every aggregate. Adding them would be a guard against nothing, and a delete guard on eleven rows the owner may well want to prune is a cost rather than a safety.
- **The point of the theme is `/theme/Sector Benchmark` and `/consolidated` grouped by Theme** — eleven rows, one per sector, read against each other and against the index. That is the reading the four index ETFs cannot give.
- **Backfilled BEFORE the next price refresh, which is the documented hazard and not optional.** `backfill-bars.js --commit --rate 100 --only …` wrote **49,842 bars in 0.2 min** (archive 4,582,378 → 4,632,220 rows, 1,181 → 1,192 symbols), finishing **20 minutes** before the next intraday slot. Without it the first refresh would try to build eleven archives inside one request, which is exactly the 504 of 2026-09-22. Every one now clears `LIGHT_MIN_ARCHIVE` (300) and is pulled shallow. **`--rate 100`, not the default 580**, so the throttle cannot starve a live price round.
  - **XLRE reaches back only to 2015-10-08 and XLC to 2018-06-19** — their real inception dates, Real Estate and Communication Services being the two sectors GICS created late. The other nine reach 2006-11-13, the 5,000-bar API ceiling. A short archive here is the market's, not the backfill's.
- **A ROW DOES NOT EXIST UNTIL A FULL ROUND REBUILDS THE SNAPSHOT.** `/api/stocks` serves the cached blob, so immediately after the add it still carried **1,181** rows and the eleven had none — which reads as a failed add and is not one. Zero was correct and expected: the listing is a STOCKS file and all eleven of these are ETFs. The field is `listingHits` now. The consequence stands — the warning could not fire and **no names were derived at add time** — so this is still not a clean bill of health, just not for the reason given.
  - **So a bulk add lands eleven nameless rows, and they stay nameless until a profile is pulled.** The owner reported it from the screener seven hours later — four named (the four whose profiles had landed) and seven blank. **A blank NAME column is the visible symptom of a missing profile**, which is worth recognising rather than diagnosing as a name bug.
