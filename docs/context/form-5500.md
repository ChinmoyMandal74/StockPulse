# Form 5500: size and business code for the Form D issuers

Backlog entry 61, E2 (2026-11-09). The owner, on the Form D viewer: "I see
only Industry, is there a sub-category somewhere", then "go ahead" with the
industry groups and Form 5500.

## What it is

Every employer with a retirement plan (and larger welfare plans) files a
Form 5500 each year with the Labor Department: the sponsor's EIN, its
**principal business code** (6 digits, from NAICS), and **how many people
are in the plan**. The Labor Department publishes every filing as yearly
data sets since 2009, two files a year: the full Form 5500 (larger plans)
and the short Form 5500-SF (under 100 participants, where most small
private companies are). Joined on the EIN the SEC profile of each Form D
issuer supplies (E1, `form-d.md`), it gives a Form D company a real
sub-industry and a headcount proxy, year by year.

## Care with askebsa.dol.gov

Another agency than the SEC, handled the same way: bulk files only, one
request at a time a minute apart (`--gap`, never under 30s), any answer but
a 200 stops the run and nothing is retried, each ZIP kept in
`%TEMP%\tickrlab-dol` and never fetched twice (streamed to `.part`, kept only
once whole and a ZIP), at most 40 requests in any 24 hours (counted in
`dol_requests`), one run at a time, nothing scheduled. The User-Agent names
the owner's contact address. The files are `https://www.askebsa.dol.gov/FOIA%20Files/{YEAR}/Latest/F_5500[_SF]_{YEAR}_Latest.zip`
(the bare `askebsa.dol.gov` answers 301 to it); "Latest" holds the latest
version of each filing and is refreshed monthly.

## What is kept

Only the plan filings whose sponsor EIN is a Form D issuer's: sponsor and
plan names, plan number, plan year, business code, participants at the
start of the year, active participants at the start and the end, the short
form's net assets at year end, the plan type codes, the sponsor's city and
state. **Not kept**: street addresses, telephone numbers, and the names of
the people who signed (all in the files). Rows are inserted, never
replaced: an amended filing is a new ACK_ID. `--rematch` reads the files
on disk again, no request, after the Form D profiles gain EINs.

## How the viewer uses it

- **Staff**: the largest count a plan reports (participants at the start
  of the year, active at the start or the end), the largest plan of the
  issuer's newest plan year. People in the plan: it can count former staff
  and leave out those not eligible, so it is a proxy. A plan that reports
  no count is a dash, never 0.
- **Business**: the code of the newest plan year (the plan with the most
  people when two disagree), shown under the Form D industry in the list
  and with its parent sectors in the side panel, with any other code an
  earlier filing gave.
- Filters: a business code or its start (5415, computer services), staff
  at least N; the list sorts by staff.

Department, every one a 200** (the 2024 short form was fetched by hand
first). 14,877,171 plan filings read; **215,269 kept, of 22,381 EINs**,

## Every employer (2026-11-09)

The owner, after Continental (a private company that never filed a Form D)
could not be found in the viewer: "Every employer in the viewer".
`f5500-load.js --employers --commit` reads the 34 yearly files on disk again
(no request) and keeps **every employer's own plans**, not only the Form D
issuers': `f5500_employers`, one row a tax ID a plan year (its largest
plan's headcount, plan assets summed over its plans, the filings counted,
the name filed under that year), and `f5500_latest`, the newest year of
each with its first year and 2019 for growth. A later file only raises a
count or adds a year; a file merged once is recorded (`f5500_emp_files`)
and never merged again. `f5500_latest` is derived and rebuilt whole.

**A company's own plan** (`ownPlan` in `f5500.js`): the plan-entity code
means different things on the two forms. On the full form 1 is a union
(multi-employer) fund and 2 a single employer; the short form is closed to
union funds and writes 1 for a single employer (2 there is a plan several
employers share). Found on Continental's own plan, coded 1 on the short
form and 2 on the full one; the first electrical-contractor count, with one
rule for both forms, was full of IBEW and NECA funds.

The viewer's **Employers** page (`/employers`, `employers-view.html`,
`employerquery.js`): search by name, trading name or tax ID; state,
business code, staff at least, still filing, also raised money by Form D;
sort by staff, growth since 2019, name; and a side panel with the staff by
year, every name filed under, and the Form D raises under the same tax ID.

The first build (2026-11-09): 34 files, no request; **1,621,937 employers**
with a newest year, 2,251,314 names on file between them (`f5500_names`:
every name and trading name each has filed under, so a search finds
Continental Electrical Construction, which files as FourGen Holdings from
2019). Illinois electrical contractors still filing: 266, about 10,100
staff between them.

## Running it

```
node --use-system-ca f5500-load.js                           # dry run, no request
node --use-system-ca f5500-load.js --commit --max all --gap 60
node --use-system-ca f5500-load.js --rematch --commit         # files on disk, no request
node --use-system-ca f5500-load.js --employers --commit      # every employer, from the files on disk
node --use-system-ca f5500-load.js --stats
```

Once a year is complete the "Latest" file still changes monthly with late
and amended filings; a later re-download means deleting that year's ZIP
from `%TEMP%\tickrlab-dol` first, as the loader never fetches a file twice.
