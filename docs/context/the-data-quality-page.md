## The data quality page
**`/quality` (admin, a row in the console's Data section) — one line per stock saying what is actually held for it, and what to run about it (2026-09-18).** Proposed and declined once as a fundamentals-coverage page, then built when the evidence changed: **bar depth never converges.** A new ticker gets ~300 bars from its first price pull and stays there until someone runs `backfill-bars`, where `fundamentals_history` days even out on their own. Every gap this app has had was silent — exchange and currency null for the whole universe for weeks, two stocks with no earnings history, one with 66 sessions — and each was found by querying the database by hand.

- **The archive is measured by SPAN, not counted.** `select symbol, count(*) from bars group by symbol` reads 1.08M rows, the shape that produced the Turso quota warning, and no index can answer "how many". `barsSpan()` seeks the first and last bar per symbol instead: **708 rows for 354 stocks against 1.08M**. Sessions are estimated from the span (×0.69) and the page says so in as many words.
- **The small tables ARE counted**, because they are bounded: `fundamentals_history` is universe × recorded days (~2,350 rows), `earnings_history` universe × 40 quarters (~10,400). Those three rollups are allowlisted in `query-plan-test.js` with that reasoning written beside them.
- **It reports the fix, not just the diagnosis** — a ready `backfill-bars.js --commit --depth 1300 --only …` for the thin ones, with a copy button. **`--only` is the point**: each symbol is replaced wholesale, so a blanket depth would truncate the stocks that already hold twenty years. A test asserts the deep stock is never in that list.
- **Nine flags, worst first**, each naming a specific consequence: no bars / too short to score (under `MIN_BARS`, 274 — the long-window returns are blank) / no 5Y (under 1,260) / stale prices / no profile / profile pull failed (the `fetched_at = 0` sentinel) / no fundamentals / no earnings / no exchange. Every summary card is a filter.
- **`readSnapshot()` was in the first cut and took it from 2.4s to 10.8s.** A ~1.3MB JSON blob, parsed to obtain a display name that `readNamesFull()` answers from two columns. **The seeks were never the cost**; measured 2,366ms for 354 stocks, cached five minutes, `?fresh=1` to force.
- **First real reading, 2026-09-18** (354 stocks): 253 clean, **9 too short to score**, 92 without five years, 1 with stale prices, 2 with no earnings. AVB had **11 sessions and no bar since 2026-08-06** — it had been quietly carrying a month-old close on the screener.
- **Two summary charts above the table (2026-09-20, owner's request).** The page was all counts and rows, and the SHAPE of the archive had to be computed by hand three times in two days. Both are drawn from rows the page has **already fetched** — `firstBar`, `lastBar` and the per-dataset fields — so they cost no query, add no endpoint, and the five-minute cache still holds.
  - **What the archive can price, by year.** One bar per year, counting the stocks whose bars **SPAN** it — not the ones that merely started before it. A stock that stopped trading in 2012 cannot price 2020, and counting it would overstate what a backtest from that year actually had. This is the number that decided everything in the trend backtest and was previously only obtainable by running a script. A year holding under a tenth of the universe is greyed, because it cannot form a basket; the caption names the usable floor and says that **a wall rather than a slope is the 5,000-bar API ceiling, not a listing date**.
  - **What is held, per dataset.** Seven tracks — Bars, 5Y, Profile, Fundamentals, Earnings, Exchange, Headlines — each a share of the universe, coloured green/amber/red, and **clickable to filter the table** through the cards' own `only` mechanism rather than a second one. It turns nine scattered flag counts into one picture, and makes the weakest dataset obvious at a glance (headlines, by a distance).
  - **`display: block` on both the track and the fill.** They are `<span>`s, and an inline box ignores width and height — the first cut set a percentage width on an inline element and drew **nothing**. The track still showed, because a background paints an inline box, so it read as an empty bar rather than as broken markup. **The test passed**: it read the style ATTRIBUTE, which was correct, instead of the rendered box. A screenshot found it, and the assertion now checks `getBoundingClientRect()`.
  - Verified: 24 checks against a fixture where every stock has a different shape — two stocks that DIE in 2012, one alone in 2003-05 so those years are thin, and one recent listing. 2010 must count 5 and 2020 must count 4; the obvious wrong implementation ("first bar before the year") says 6 for 2020 and makes the two years identical.
- **The `.card` class sits on the bezel AND the core** (the double-bezel pattern), so `querySelectorAll('.card')` attached two listeners per card and one click toggled the filter twice. `:scope > .card`. The `.warn` lesson in a new place: a class used for two purposes will be selected for one of them by accident.
- Verified: 32 checks against the real server on an in-memory database — a fixture where every stock has a DIFFERENT defect (deep and clean, a year, four months, none at all, deep but forty days stale), the span and its estimate, each flag told apart, the fix command excluding the deep stock, the cache returning the whole body rather than an empty shell, `fresh=1`, and the page's cards, filtering, note and lack of sideways scroll.
