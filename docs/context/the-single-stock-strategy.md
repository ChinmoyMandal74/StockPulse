## The single-stock strategy
`/single` runs the same trend rule on **one stock at a time** — not which stock to own, but whether to own the one you have picked, and how much of it. Reached from **Single** in the screener's bar, beside Strategy.

**It is an exposure control, not a stock picker**, and that changes what counts as success. The benchmark is holding that same stock, and the honest expectation is that it loses on return and wins on drawdown, because a rule that sits in cash part of the time must give up upside to avoid downside.

- **Nothing is precomputed — the page ships the prices and simulates in the browser.** That is the opposite of `/strategy`, and the reason is a correctness one: **the position cap is a nonlinearity.** `/strategy` stores runs at a 10% target and scales afterwards, which is exact for everything except the cap, so raising the target there does *not* re-apply it. The cap binds on 0.8% of stock-days at a 10% target and **10.8% at 15%**, so the published rule's 1× cap at 15% is a setting that page cannot honestly express. Recomputing from prices makes every slider exact.
- **The engine is `strategy.js`, unchanged.** One symbol is a universe of one. Two options were added for this page and both default off, so the stored portfolio grid keeps its meaning: `exitOnSignal` (rebalance the day the signal flips, not only on the schedule) and `opts.ppy` (periods per year — 252 here, 12 for the monthly series `/strategy` ships). A single stock is small enough to measure daily, so its drawdown is the real intra-period low rather than a month-end understatement.
- **`single-closes.json` is ~700 KB gzipped at 93 symbols (888 KB at 116), and the precision was measured rather than assumed.** The archive holds split-adjusted floats, so essentially every symbol carries more than two decimals. Rounding to cents corrupts a daily return by up to **5.45 percentage points** — NVDA's 2007 closes are $0.16 after every split since, where a cent is 3% of the price. 4dp costs 750 KB and is wrong by at most 0.007 points of CAGR; the shipped 1e6 scale costs 888 KB and is exact. Deltas rather than prices, because consecutive closes are close together and small integers compress.
- **Both legs start where the rule first becomes evaluable.** Comparing a strategy's post-warm-up years against a benchmark's whole history is how a backtest flatters itself for free.
- **"Match buy-and-hold" solves for the target volatility rather than scaling a result**, by bisection, because the cap makes the relationship non-linear. It is load-bearing: at the defaults the rule averages **29% invested** against a fully-invested benchmark, so the CAGR column is not a fair fight and the page says so. **At a 1× cap the rule often cannot reach the stock's volatility at all** — it saturates, which the note explains rather than leaving the link looking broken. Matched at 3×: AAPL **25.1% against 24.2%** (Sharpe 0.76 vs 0.74), MU **45.7% against 30.1%** (0.89 vs 0.68).
- **Sharpe is the fair column and CAGR is not.** Sharpe is return over volatility, so it barely moves when the rule runs at a fraction of the exposure — the exposure cancels, including the cash credit and its matching risk-free subtraction. The verdict line says which to read.
- **The sweep is the point of the page.** The identical rule at the identical settings across the whole universe, because one stock rebalanced monthly over eighteen years is perhaps twenty independent decisions, and the tickers this gets tested on are the ones that went up. **~90 simulations in ~180ms**, so it follows a slider drag on a 140ms debounce rather than being a build step. `runOne(symbol, full)` skips slicing the chart-sized arrays the sweep would throw away.
- **What it says at the published settings: beats holding on Sharpe in 22 of 87 stocks (25%)**, cuts the worst drawdown in all 87 (which is arithmetic, not skill — anything holding cash does that), median time invested 28%. It was 33 of 110 (30%) on the 116-symbol universe. On the **Faded 33** the median CAGR gap turns *positive* (+1.7%), which is the one hint of something: a trend rule earns its keep on stocks that fall, and those are exactly the names a survivor-only universe under-represents.
- **A hole longer than `MAX_CARRY` (10) sessions is left blank**, the same bound `/strategy` uses and for the same reason recorded there.
