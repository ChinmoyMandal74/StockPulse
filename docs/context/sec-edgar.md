## SEC EDGAR — the filings, on their own card, feeding nothing (2026-09-27)
**TWO EXCEPTIONS TO THE BOUNDARY BELOW, BY THE OWNER (2026-10-10).** This section says FINRA and insider data get no screener column. That was lifted for two readings: **Short build** and **Insider net**, in the Reactions group. See [reactions-group.md](reactions-group.md). Everything else here stays off the row, and nothing from either table reaches the Signal.


**`/stock/<SYMBOL>` carries a `SEC EDGAR` section: the company's own filed statements, back to 2007 for a long-listed name, every row linking to the filing it came from.** Asked for after measuring the gap — `bars` holds 1.9M rows back to 2003 and **`fundamentals_history` holds 20 days**, which is why `/backtest` is capped at two months.

**THE OWNER ASKED FOR YAHOO AND THE MEASUREMENT SAID NO.** `finance.yahoo.com/quote/<SYM>/financials/` is drawn from `query1.finance.yahoo.com/ws/fundamentals-timeseries/...` (cookie + crumb, both obtainable, 200 for RBLX) — and it serves **4 annual and 5 quarterly periods**, capped server-side (`period1=0` gets no more), with **no filing date anywhere in the payload** and values restated to today. Twelve Data already sells 6 quarters, so Yahoo's quarterly depth is *worse* than what is paid for, and without a publication date none of it is point-in-time. SEC EDGAR was built instead.

### Why EDGAR, in numbers
- **Free, official, permitted** with a declared User-Agent. `data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK##########.json`, one file per company, keyed off SEC's own `company_tickers.json`.
- **99% of the universe maps** — 1,167 of 1,180. The 13 misses are ETFs and two renamed tickers; a fund files no statements, which is the correct answer rather than a gap.
- **Depth**: MSFT net income to **2007-09-30**, gross profit 2007, operating cash flow 2008. RBLX to its 2021 IPO — each company's whole filed life.
- **Every observation carries `filed`, `form` and `accn`** — the publication date, the document type and the accession number. Nothing else available here has any of the three.
- **The restatement trail comes with it**: 49 of MSFT's 63 revenue periods appear in more than one filing, so **as-first-reported is recoverable** by taking the earliest `filed`. That is why the trail is STORED rather than collapsed to the latest — it cannot be recovered once discarded, and re-fetching 1,167 companies to get it back is a twenty-minute penance.

### THE BOUNDARY, which was the owner's condition for building it at all
> *"We cannot afford to have incorrect data and wrong logic in there which is the heart of the system and the differentiating factor."*

**This data reaches the SEC EDGAR card and nothing else.** No `FIELD_SPEC` entry, no snapshot stamping, no screener column, no filter key — because a column becomes filterable, then screenable, and a screen drives promo cards and the phone. It is **asserted, not intended**: `sec-test.js` fetches the real `/api/stocks` and reads the real rendered header, and fails if any filings field, accession number or filing date appears in either. **Proved by reverting**: stamping `accn` and `epsDiluted` onto the read path fails 2, naming both.

The two things that make it unusable in a verdict anyway are worth recording: **`shortPctFloat` is in no filing** and it only fires a "weak fundamentals" clause, so a replay would be silently *less* strict; and filings are quarterly steps that would have to be synthesised into daily rows. Fine under a chart, poison in a gate. Of the ten fundamentals the Balanced rules read, seven are reconstructible; `forwardPe`'s only use is ETF detection, which the CIK map answers better.

> **PARTLY SUPERSEDED (2026-09-29).** The short-interest half is no longer true: FINRA is loaded, 217,734 readings back to 2017-12-29 at 100% coverage, so `shortPctFloat` IS reconstructible from 2018. And the reconstruction itself now exists and is on screen — see **Adjusted Advice** below, which assembles the whole engine row from filings plus bars and scores it with the rules unchanged. What has NOT changed is the sentence this paragraph is really about: none of it reaches a live verdict.

**The advice BACKTEST is a separate conversation.** `/backtest` currently imputes *today's* fundamentals before 2026-08-30 — a look-ahead — so filings with a `filed` date would strictly improve it. Noted because it is the strongest case this data has; it must still never write back into a live verdict. **It was had on 2026-09-29 and deliberately not built**: the owner asked to see the fields first, which is what **Adjusted Advice** is. ### Three traps, each found by measuring and each still live
1. **`periodType` must be read off the SPAN, never off `fp`.** A 10-Q carries the quarter AND the year-to-date figure under the same `fp` — measured on RBLX: 28 quarterly observations, 10 six-month, 8 nine-month. Reading `fp` reports a Q2 at roughly double its real size.
2. **THERE IS NO Q4, and a cash-flow statement is YEAR-TO-DATE.** RBLX's only quarter-ends are March, June and September; and revenue reaches 86-99% of rows while operating cash flow reaches 56-67% — not a tag problem, the tags are right there. Both holes are consecutive differences down one fiscal year's cumulative ladder (Q1, H, 9M, FY, which share a `periodStart`): **Q2 = H − Q1, Q3 = 9M − H, Q4 = FY − 9M**. Where the quarter WAS filed, only its missing flows are filled and `derivedFields` names exactly which, so the page marks them rather than passing arithmetic off as a filing. FCF coverage went 56% → 81%.
   - **A POSITION is not a flow.** Assets at year end are assets at year end; a weighted-average share count does not subtract, and neither does a per-share figure computed on a different denominator each quarter. Those stay blank rather than being invented (`NO_DIFF`).
3. **`fp` LIES ABOUT THE LABEL TOO, one level up — and a screenshot caught it, not an assertion.** `fp` belongs to the FILING, not the observation, so a 10-K (fp=FY) carrying three-month comparatives produced a quarterly table with a column of rows reading **"2020-06-30 FY"**. The label is derived from the period now, against the fiscal year end **asked of the data** rather than assumed to be December — MSFT's ends in June, so September is **Q1**, not Q3.

4. **A PROXY IS NOT A FINANCIAL STATEMENT, and "the latest filing wins" is not enough on its own.** RBLX's FY2025 revenue and cash flow sit in the 10-K of 2026-02-11; a **DEF 14A of 2026-04-16 carries `NetIncomeLoss` and nothing else**. Taking the newest filing let that one-number row speak for the year, so every quarter differenced from it lost its inputs — live, a Q4 with a net income and eleven dashes. **A screenshot of the deployed page caught it**; every assertion had passed. `outranks()` puts a statement form (10-K/10-Q/20-F/40-F, and their /A) ahead of a mention, and only then does recency decide. The others are still stored — an 8-K sometimes carries real preliminary results — they just do not speak for a period while a real statement exists.

**THE INVARIANT THAT VALIDATES ALL OF IT: four quarters must sum to the year.** Measured on MSFT across five fiscal years and three concepts — revenue, net income, operating cash flow — **worst gap 0.0000%**. If the span classification, the ladder or the Q4 subtraction were wrong, they would not reconcile. RBLX shows 0.04% on one year, which is restatement drift between filings rather than an arithmetic error, so the test tolerates 0.5% and reports the worst.

### Shape
- **`secfacts.js`** at the root: pure parsing, no network and no database — the `news.js` shape, orchestration in server.js.
- **`sec_facts`** keyed **(symbol, accn, period_end, period_type)**, which is how a filing works: every concept on a row comes from ONE document, so a revenue can never be paired with a net income from another. ~350 rows per company, 15 concepts. `sec_state` holds the per-symbol fetch clock, separate for the reason `news_state` is separate. Both in `SYMBOL_TABLES`.
- **Whole-symbol replace with MULTI-ROW inserts**, chunked at 100 — the `tech_history` measurement, where the cost turned out to be per STATEMENT and batching was 23x.
- **`GET /api/sec?symbol=`** is its own route, fetched after the chart: ~30-60KB against the stock page's ~10KB, so folding it into `/api/stock` would make the price wait for it. **Guests can read it** (the owner's call — public filings), symbol-guarded like every other per-stock route.
- **A company with nothing filed gets NO CARD** rather than an empty one, the peers rule.

#### The ratios, not just the dollars (2026-09-28, owner: "you are showing the raw data, can you show the % and margins that are used in the Advice calculation")
**A `Figures / Ratios` switch beside the `Quarterly / Annual` one** — two switches doing one thing each, because one combined control would need four states to say the same thing. The ratios view shows **gross, operating, profit and FCF margin, and revenue and earnings growth year on year**, every one computed in `secfacts.js` from concepts already stored. No new fetch, no new column in the table.

- **THE COMPARISON STRIP IS THE POINT OF IT, not a footnote.** A reader seeing "Gross margin 60.0%" beside a verdict assumes that is what the verdict read, **and it is not**: the engine takes the vendor's trailing-twelve-month figure, restated to today and computed on a different basis — this file already records the two **~4 points apart on one company**. So the newest period is printed beside what the engine actually read, with the gap named and **marked amber past two points**. Amber because on this page it already means *notice this*; the gap is expected, not a fault.
- **LIKE FOR LIKE, OR THE STRIP LIBELS THE VENDOR — and the first cut did.** Shipped comparing the newest QUARTER against the engine's figures, it showed MU an **FCF margin of 42.4% against 8.5%, a 33.9-point "gap"** that was almost entirely period length: MU's revenue is up 345% year on year, so one quarter looks nothing like its trailing year. **The live numbers are what caught it** — the fixture's did not, because its quarters were flat.
  - The mapping is now exact, and the data itself pointed to it: the **margins are four filed quarters summed**, because the vendor's are trailing-twelve-month; the **growth lines stay on the newest quarter against a year earlier**, because the vendor's are too — which is why those two had *already* agreed to **+0.0pt** while every margin was 12 to 34 points out. `/help` and the screens section had recorded "earnings growth is last quarter YoY" all along.
  - **Four consecutive quarters or nothing.** `ttm()` refuses a set spanning under 300 or over 430 days, and refuses if any one part is missing — a gap summed as though it were a year understates every margin off it, silently, while still looking like a number. The first fixture skipped two quarters and the guard correctly returned null, which is how it was found.
  - Each row of the strip **says which basis it is on**, since the two are deliberately different.
  - **Proved by reverting**: back on the single quarter, the fixture's +0.2pt profit-margin gap becomes **+8.7pt**.
  - **AND THEN FOUR OF THE FIVE RECONCILED EXACTLY, which is the real validation.** On MU, once the bases matched: gross margin **+72.6% against +72.6%**, profit margin **+55.9% against +55.9%**, and both growth lines to **±0.0pt**. A card built to explain a discrepancy reporting four zeroes is the strongest evidence available that the filings are being read correctly.
    - **The one that did not is FCF, and it is DEFINITIONAL rather than arithmetic** — 29.0% against 8.5%. Ours is operating cash flow less capex, straight from the statement; checked against EDGAR, Micron files capex YTD and only **one** of the four quarters exists as a standalone 90-day observation (5.39B), so the rest are differenced exactly as designed and the total is internally consistent. With revenue and net income agreeing to the decimal, the gap is the vendor defining free cash flow more strictly. The note says so rather than leaving a reader to guess which is broken.
    - **A WARNING ABOUT INSTINCT.** I first read MU's 41.46B quarter and 90.27B TTM as obviously corrupt, because the real Micron earns ~$37B a year. They are correct: Q1 13.64 + Q2 23.86 + Q3 41.46 sums to the filed 9M of 78.96B, and the vendor independently agrees the latest quarter is +345.7%. **This dataset's Micron is in a supercycle the real one is not.** Check the internal arithmetic before trusting a memory of what a company "should" earn.
- **It costs no read.** The strip compares against `stock`, the snapshot row the page already holds — asking `/api/sec` for it would mean pulling the ~1.3MB snapshot into a route that exists precisely to avoid that. The first cut did exactly that and was reverted before it ran.
- **A MARGIN OFF A LOSS IS STILL A MARGIN** and is kept — unlike a multiple off a loss, which is arithmetic rather than cheapness. A −5.6% profit margin is a true statement about a quarter.

### Refreshing it
**A panel on `/admin`, not a page and not a deep link.** The price refreshes live on `/refreshes` because they share a round loop with the screener and that loop must exist once; this shares nothing — it is a batch call in a `while`.

- **`POST /api/sec/refresh` does ONE batch of five and says how many remain**, the `/api/news/refresh` shape, because a serverless function must never hold hundreds of fetches in one request (2026-09-18, a refresh cut mid-tail and logged `abandoned` over data that was fine). The page loops it.
- **`Fetch missing` is the primary button** — the new-ticker case, and the one that gets pressed. `Refresh all` is for when a quarter's filings land, and passes its own `since` so every symbol it just wrote does not look fresh and leave the loop chasing its tail.
- **A SYMBOL THAT CANNOT BE FETCHED IS STILL RECORDED AS CHECKED** (`nocik` / `nofacts` / `empty` / `error`). Without it the batch re-picks it for ever and can never report itself done — the news-staleness bug, which ran until it was stopped by hand. The test drives the loop to completion over a fixture containing a fund and a company the SEC 500s on.
- **No new env var**: `SEC_UA` falls back to `Tickr Lab (<REPORT_TO or MAIL_FROM>)`, a real contact address the SEC will accept. With none configured every route refuses and the panel says which variable to set.
- **Our dot is the SEC's hyphen** — `BRK.B` is `BRK-B` there, the same split Yahoo has. Both spellings are tried, because the failure is a silent miss.
- Sequential inside a batch: the SEC asks for at most ten requests a second and a companyfacts file is 1-5MB, so five in parallel is a burst for no gain.

#### It rotates on the nightly now, and NEVER inside the refresh (2026-09-30, owner: "fold the edgar refresh to nightly")
**`mode=rotate` picks the symbols whose filings were last checked more than `SEC_ROTATE_DAYS` (7) ago, oldest first.** Until this it was two buttons on `/admin` and nothing else: `Fetch missing` for a new ticker and `Refresh all` for when a quarter's filings land — so a company that filed a 10-Q went unnoticed until somebody remembered to press something.

- **A THIRD MODE, not a change to either existing one.** `missing` must keep meaning "no row at all" — the `/admin` button depends on it — and `all` must keep ignoring the clock. A cutoff folded into `missing` would have made that button re-fetch the universe. The test asserts `missing` still finds nothing once every symbol has a row.
- **OLDEST FIRST, which is what makes a cut-short run lose nothing.** A laptop job can be killed by a lid, so the batch order has to be the order of value: `stale.sort()` on `fetchedAt`, with a symbol holding **no `sec_state` row at all ranked ahead of every dated one** — that is the new ticker, and it has no card at all until it is fetched. A plan returning `readUniverse`'s own order would pick alphabetically and look identical in a 200.
- **`done` IS REACHABLE, which is the whole difference from a clock with no cutoff.** Every symbol a batch touches gets its `fetched_at` moved — including `nocik` / `nofacts` / `empty` / `error`, the rule above — so the overdue set shrinks monotonically and the driver's `while` terminates. Without the recorded miss a fund would be re-picked for ever and the loop could never report itself finished; that is the news-staleness bug, which ran until it was stopped by hand.
- **THE CRON SECRET NOW SATISFIES `POST /api/sec/refresh`**, beside an admin session — `isCron(req) || await isAdmin(req)`, the shape `/api/cron/refresh` and `/api/cron/alerts` already have. Deliberately not *instead of*: the `/admin` panel still drives the same route with a session.
- **IT RUNS AFTER THE ROTATION COMPLETES, AS ITS OWN PHASE — never in the refresh tail.** `secRotate()` in `nightly-ping.js` loops `POST /api/sec/refresh?mode=rotate&n=5` once the profile rounds are done. Work added to a refresh tail has surfaced as "the refresh failed" over perfectly good data three times on this project (news 2026-09-18, `techHistorySpan` 2026-09-19, `archiveStats` 2026-09-20), and this is the heaviest candidate yet: a companyfacts file is 1-5MB. **A failure here is non-fatal to the night** and a 404 reads as an older server, so deploy order does not matter.
- **RAISED TO ~150 A RUN ON 2026-10-08 (`SEC_MAX_BATCHES` 30)**: at 100 a run the ten weekday firings covered 1,000 a week against 1,274, so the rotation could not finish a lap inside its own 7-day cutoff — measured, 481 companies sat 7 to 14 days unchecked. What follows describes the original bound.
- **Bounded at ~100 symbols a run** (`SEC_BATCH` 5 × `SEC_MAX_BATCHES` 20, `SEC_GAP_MS` 1500), which at two weekday firings covers the universe comfortably inside the cutoff while keeping this address in good standing with the SEC — the same reasoning the insider walk's 120ms pacing records, and the same address that has already answered 429 and then 403.
- Verified: **10 checks** with the SEC stubbed at `fetch` before the server loads, so nothing is fetched from that address: the fixture gives five symbols **different ages in deliberately non-alphabetical order**, one of them inside the cutoff and one with no row at all. **Proved by reverting three times**: dropping the cutoff fails 2, dropping the oldest-first sort fails 3, and dropping the cron guard fails 5.
  - **`uni-boot.js` DEFAULTS `ADMIN_PASSWORD` TO `''`, WHICH PUTS THE SERVER IN OPEN MODE** — so the suite's two "unauthorised" probes SUCCEEDED, quietly consumed the two most-overdue symbols before the ordering check ran, and made correct ordering look broken. Any suite asserting a refusal must set a real password.
  - **A 403 FROM A ROUTE YOU HAVE JUST GIVEN A CRON GUARD IS DEPLOY LAG, NOT AN AUTH FAILURE.** `requireAdmin` answers **403** to a bearer token and the new guard answers **401**, so the status code says which bundle is live. ### Announced (SEC) — the Item 2.02 date on the screener (2026-10-01, owner's request)
**A column in the Fundamentals group immediately after `Reported`: the newest 8-K carrying Item 2.02, "Results of Operations and Financial Condition".** A second, independent answer to *has this company reported*, from the company itself rather than from the price feed.

**IT WAS ASKED FOR AS "the 8-K date" AND IS KEYED ON ITEM 2.02 INSTEAD**, because the reason behind the request was the earnings announcement. Measured on MU: **10 8-Ks in the last year, exactly 4 carrying 2.02** — one per quarter; the rest are 5.02 (officer changes) and 8.01 (other events). A latest-any-8-K column would mostly report a director's resignation.

#### IT CANNOT COME FROM `sec_facts`, WHICH IS WHY IT NEEDED A NEW SOURCE
**We hold ZERO 8-K rows for anyone** — measured across MU, NVDA, AAPL, VNO and JPM, not one between them. `sec_facts` is built from **companyfacts**, which carries only XBRL-TAGGED facts, and an earnings 8-K generally has none: MU's 8-K of 2026-09-30 carries **0 facts**, and companyfacts does not carry item numbers at any rate. The source is the **submissions API** (`data.sec.gov/submissions/CIK##########.json`), which does.

- **It rides on `sec_state`, not a table of its own** — one date per symbol, exactly like `last_filed` — and that is what makes it free: `/api/sec/filed` is **already** fetched after the screener paints, so there is **no new endpoint**, and the display-only boundary holds by construction. `readSecFiled()` now returns `{filed, results}` per symbol; the client keeps a `typeof` branch for the old bare-string shape, so deploy order does not matter.
- **`noteSecMiss` had to be told to keep it — the THIRD field that function has needed telling.** It comes from a different source, so a transient companyfacts refusal would otherwise blank it and read as *this company never announced*. The same mistake it made once with the CIK, which silently broke the insider card for 64 filers. **Proved by reverting**: the subquery removed fails 1.
- **NO `data-key`, so no filter box and no sort.** `data-col` gives it a column-view id and a `/columns` entry — the 90d spark column's own treatment — without becoming a filter key. `lastEarningsDate` stays the single thing the column, the filter and `daysSinceEarnings` all mean; the standing boundary is that SEC data never reaches the screener's selection logic. Asserted by counting: of the 20 Fundamentals filter cells, exactly the 19 whose header has a key may hold an input.

|---|---|---|

| 1–10 | 96 | recording lag, still the same quarter |
| 11–35 | 7 | |

A missed report is at least a quarter (~90 days) and anything under ~35 days is the same quarter recorded differently, so the line belongs in the empty bucket: **`ANN_AHEAD_DAYS` is 40**, flagging **20 of 1,062**. MU reads **+98** (2026-06-24 → 2026-09-30) and is amber; **NVDA reads 0** and is not.
- **It lands on the same 40 as `SEC_BEHIND_DAYS` and is deliberately a SEPARATE constant.** That one has to clear a structural announce-to-file lag of up to 35 days; these two dates describe the **same event** and have no structural lag at all. Tuning one must not silently move the other.
- **Proved by reverting**, but only after the fixture gained stocks at **exactly 40 and at 41**: with gaps of 98 and 0 alone, 10 and 40 classify every row identically and the measured number was unproven. A threshold is a step function and every bug lives on its edge.

#### ITEM 2.02 IS NOT EVERY COMPANY'S RESULTS CHANNEL — found by measuring, not anticipated
**51 of 1,062 have a 2.02 date that PREDATES the feed's own by more than a quarter.** Energy Fuels is the clear case: it announces under Items **7.01 and 8.01** and has exactly **one 2.02 ever, from 2016**. Printing that beside a current `Reported` cell would read as *last reported 2016*, so the cell shows an em-dash and the tooltip says the company announces under a different item and to read `Reported` instead. **Widening to 7.01/8.01 was rejected**: 8.01 is the catch-all "other events", which would put a director's resignation in a results column.

#### IT IS PART OF THE EDGAR REFRESH NOW, not a one-off load (2026-10-01, owner: "make sure we get the Edgar announcement date as part of the edgar data refresh")
**`secFetchOne` fetches the submissions file beside companyfacts and writes `last_results` on every path.** So the nightly rotation keeps the date current by itself, and `sec-results-load.js` is only the backfill.

### Insider transactions — Forms 3/4/5 (2026-09-27, owner's request)

**A second card on `/stock`: what the officers, directors and 10% owners did with their own money.** Officers must report their own trades within **two business days**, so it is a legally-mandated, near-real-time disclosure with no vendor in between — and it is the only thing in the app that is neither price nor accounting.

**THE TRANSACTION CODE IS THE WHOLE GAME.** Measured on 2026 Q2: of 78,328 non-derivative transactions, **5,326 are open-market purchases (6.8%)**. The rest is compensation machinery — 19,211 grants, 10,783 option exercises, 9,357 lots of shares withheld to pay tax. A grant is not a decision, withholding is automatic, and an exercise is usually a sale in a hat. **Only `P` and `S` are kept**; a screen that counts "insider activity" without that filter is measuring payroll. Across the universe, only **185 of 1,180 symbols had a purchase in a quarter** — a screen-sized answer rather than a wall.

- **KEYED ON THE ISSUER'S CIK, NEVER ITS TICKER.** 763 of 56,102 filings (1.4%) carry `NONE` or `N/A` as the trading symbol — my first cluster count had **`NONE(117)`** at the top, 117 unrelated companies fused into one fake company — while every filing carries a CIK. The ticker rides along for display only.
- **`filed` and `transDate` are both kept, and anything measuring this must use `filed`.** That is the date it became public; the trade is private until then, so using the trade date buys a look-ahead. Measured lag on purchases: **median 2 days, 59% within two, 87% within five**.
- **10b5-1 trades are marked.** Half of the quarter's rows carry the flag — a plan set up months ahead says much less about what the insider thinks today.
- **The summary counts PEOPLE as well as trades.** One director topping up three times is one person's opinion; three directors buying is the thing worth noticing, and a trade count cannot tell them apart. 266 of 1,134 companies had 3+ distinct buyers in the quarter.
- A trade with no price is counted but **not valued**, and the card says how many — a total over 8 of 11 trades is not a total.

**THE SAME BOUNDARY AS THE FILINGS CARD.** Display only: no `FIELD_SPEC` entry, no snapshot stamping, no screener column. `insider-test.js` reads the real `/api/stocks` and the real header and fails on a leak. **It is deliberately not a signal yet** — the literature likes clustered open-market purchases, and the horizon (1-6 months) is one of the very few that fits the owner's one-to-two-month constraint, but this project's log has taken eleven framings and found ten flat. It is shown as a **fact**, the way the volume-breakout marker and Cushion are, and would be tested separately before it is ever allowed to mean anything.
- **A boundary test cannot use the bare word "insider".** The Ownership group has carried an **Insiders** column since 2026-09-15 — `insiderPct`, the share held by founders and management, off the profile — which is a different fact entirely and belongs where it is. The check names what this feature would add.

**Loading is LOCAL — `insider-load.js`, the `backfill-bars.js` precedent.** The source is the SEC's quarterly **Form 345 data sets**, ~11MB zipped per quarter unpacking to ~60MB of TSV, back to **2006 Q1 (82 quarters)**. That has no business in a serverless request. Dry run by default; `--commit`, `--quarters`, `--from/--to`, `--force`. Measured: **8 quarters, 224,367 transactions, 5.9 minutes.**
- **THE URL PATH MOVED.** Recent quarters are under `/files/datastandardsinnovation/data/...`, older ones under `/files/structureddata/data/...`. Both are tried, newest first — a loader that knows one silently stops finding new quarters.
- **Windows-only, deliberately**: it shells out to PowerShell's `Expand-Archive` rather than adding a zip dependency to a project with three. The machine that runs the intraday schedule runs this.
- `insider_trans` is **not in `SYMBOL_TABLES`** — it holds every filer rather than our universe, so dropping a ticker must not delete a company's filing history. It is replaced a quarter at a time, by filing-date range.

**Two bugs this found, both mine, both in the code it built on:**
- **`insider_trans` was not on `query-plan-test.js`'s big-table list**, so nothing checked it — and the quarter-delete ranged on an unindexed `filed`, a full walk of ~660k rows, which on this database is a quota event rather than a slow query. The table is on the list now and `idx_insider_filed` exists; **proved by reverting**, which prints `SCAN insider_trans`.

Verified: **48 checks** — the code filter with grants proved present-in-source and absent-from-output, the fake tickers, nothing filed before it was traded, people-vs-trades, the store round trip, the card measured on the page with its links and its colours, a company with none drawing no card, the CIK regression both ways, the boundary, and the roles (guest yes on a preview stock, 403 outside it and on coverage).

#### The quarterly files run 90 days behind, so the recent end is walked daily
**Measured the day it shipped: the load reached 2026-06-30 against a calendar of 2026-09-28, and asking "who bought in the last 90 days" returned 3 names when the truth was 188.** The data sets are published after a quarter ends. That is tolerable under a card which prints a filing date on every row, and fatal anywhere that presents itself as current — which is exactly why an insider column on the screener was refused until this existed.

- **`POST /api/insider/daily` walks ONE DAY per call** off EDGAR's daily index, the news-refresh shape; `/admin` loops it. **A weekend or holiday has no index at all, and a 404 marks the day DONE rather than failing** — otherwise the walk stalls on the first Saturday. The test's fixture is two trading days inside 89, so the loop is driven to completion over 87 empty ones.
- **FILTERED BEFORE ANYTHING IS FETCHED.** 666 ownership forms land daily; EDGAR lists each under both the issuer and the reporting owner, so our issuer CIKs match the issuer line and the rest are never touched. Measured in the test: **2 documents fetched across 89 index reads.**
- **Appended, never replacing** — the daily path fills what the quarterly files have not published, and must not disturb a loaded quarter. The day cursor lives in `app_meta` beside `price_cursor`.
- **A QUARTERLY LOAD MOVES THE DAY CURSOR, and the cost of forgetting it is measured in SEC traffic rather than in wrong numbers (2026-09-27).** The bulk file covers a whole quarter, so a cursor left where the walk happened to stop sends it back over ninety days of filings the load has just delivered — ~9,000 document fetches against an address that has already answered 429 and then 403. Done in **`writeInsiderQuarter`**, the one place a quarter is written, so a future route cannot forget it. **Forwards only, and only on a load that landed**: a refused quarter writes an error row and must leave the walk alone, and re-running an old quarter with `--force` must never rewind past days already walked. The loader prints the resume date, so the advance is visible rather than assumed.
  - Verified: **8 checks**, and the load-bearing one is not the stored value but **which day the real route then asks the SEC for** — the fixture puts the cursor mid-quarter, loads the quarter, and drives `POST /api/insider/daily` with every index fetch stubbed to 404. **Proved by reverting**: without the advance it asks for `20260416` instead of `20260701` and 4 fail, which is the production symptom in miniature.
- **`/api/insider/coverage` reports how far BEHIND it is**, not just how much is stored, and the console says it in amber past a week. A table that looks full and stopped three months ago is the failure this path exists to prevent.
- **THE UNIVERSE FILTER LEAVES A PERMANENT HOLE FOR A TICKER ADDED LATER.** The walk narrows against `readUniverseCiks()` *before fetching*, which is what makes it affordable — so every day already walked was filtered to the universe as it stood then, and a stock added afterwards has a gap over exactly those days that nothing in the app will fill. The quarterly path does NOT share this: `Insider.build()` is pure and universe-free, so the bulk file carries every filer and a re-load with `--force` picks a new company up for free (~45s a quarter). **A new ticker also needs `Fetch missing` (the filings card) run FIRST** — `readInsider` joins on the issuer CIK, and the filings loader is the only thing that resolves one. See [docs/backlog.md](docs/backlog.md) entry 13.

**THE FORM 4 XML IS PARSED BY TARGETED EXTRACTION, NOT A DEPENDENCY** — a complete submission is ~5KB and the ownership block is regular. **Validated against the SEC's own quarterly extract of the same filings**, which is the only honest test of a hand-written parser: asserting it against my reading of the schema would only prove I read the schema the way I wrote the code. 14 real filings, **every field agreeing**.

That comparison found two things a schema reading would not have:
- **THE TWO SOURCES DISAGREE ON PRECISION, AND THE FILING IS RIGHT.** The bulk extract rounds a price to two decimals — 69.2232 in the filing is 69.22 there — and **79 of one quarter's P/S rows are rounded all the way to 0**. The parser keeps the filing's own number, and the comparison is made at the data set's precision rather than making the parser throw information away.
- **A ZERO PRICE IS NOT A PRICE.** Those 79 rows would have printed a confident `$0` from `shares * 0`; both paths reject it and the card shows a dash. **Reject the empty before coercing** — the fifth place this project has needed that sentence.
- **A FILING CAN NAME SEVERAL OWNERS — 1,229 of 56,102 (2.2%)**, usually a couple filing jointly or a fund and its manager, and **the two ingest paths were picking different ones**: the bulk path took the TSV's row order, the XML path took document order, so the same trade was credited to a different person depending on which loaded it. Both sort by CIK now. The COUNT is stored (`owners`) so a card can say "+1 other" rather than quietly crediting one spouse with the pair's trade.

Verified: **+18 checks** on the walk — termination over a weekend, one day per call, the filter-before-fetch ratio, filed-from-the-index against traded-from-the-document, the quarter undisturbed, de-duplication, the staleness reported, and a guest refused — plus **+13** on the parser.

##### Then the first real catch-up earned an HTTP 429, and then a 403 (2026-09-27)
**Driving that loop against the 88-day gap made 130 calls and advanced ONE day**, then the SEC stopped answering this address at all. The walk itself was correct — a day that fails does not advance the cursor, which is exactly right — and that correctness is what made it a hammer: **~110 of those calls retried a day the SEC had already refused**, because nothing in the response told the caller to stop.

- **A THROTTLE IS A WAIT, NOT A FAILURE, and the response has to say which.** A 429 now answers **HTTP 200** with `throttled: true` and words naming the local loader, rather than a 502 the page's loop treated as "try again immediately". The console backs off 60s, gives up after three in a row, and says a long gap belongs on `insider-load.js --daily`.
- **A mid-day 429 keeps what landed and does not advance.** The insert is keyed on `(accn, seq)`, so finishing the day later fills the rest without duplicating any of it — but a day cut short is not a day done.
- **`SEC_DAY_MAX` was 400 and WOULD HAVE SILENTLY TRUNCATED.** Measured on 2026-07-02, the day after a quarter closed: **819 filings for our universe alone**, against my estimate of ~100. `want.slice(0, 400)` would have dropped 419 filings and then marked the day complete — half-done and unrecoverable. It is 900, and a day over the cap is **reported and not advanced** (`tooBig`) rather than trimmed.
- **120ms between document fetches.** The SEC asks for under ten a second and throttles sustained access below that; a normal day is ~100 filings, so this costs about twelve seconds and keeps the address in good standing. `insider-load.js` paces at 130ms for the same reason.
- **A serverless request is the wrong host for a long catch-up, and this is why the local loader gained `--daily`.** Ninety days is ~9,000 filing fetches; one machine, paced, is the polite way to do it.

**And the same night Vercel raised a High Severity alert — a spike in 502s on the root route, 01:25 UTC.** Almost certainly self-inflicted and not the app: a 130-call loop holding functions, a 60-round `mode=all` filings refresh, and a 10-minute 224k-row bulk write into the production Turso, all at once. The site was verified healthy afterwards (health 200 in 150ms, `/api/stocks` 200 in 4.9s, no stuck refresh flag, the nightly `complete` at 1182/1182). **The rule this leaves is the one already written for research reads**: heavy work aimed at production is not free, and three heavy things at once is an outage of your own making.

### Short interest — FINRA's bi-weekly position, on its own card (2026-09-28, owner's request)

**A third card on `/stock`: how much of the company is sold short, how many days of volume that is, and where today sits in its own eight-year range.** Asked for as *"just download this data and show on the individual page for the time being and not use it anywhere else"* — so it is display only, loaded locally, and production never talks to FINRA at all.

**FINRA PUBLISHES TWO THINGS AND ONLY ONE OF THEM IS SHORT INTEREST.** The RegSHO **daily** file (`cdn.finra.org/equity/regsho/daily/CNMSshvol<date>.txt`, 547KB, 12,351 rows) is short **volume** — shares sold short intraday, most of it market-maker inventory that is flat again by the close, routinely 50-60% on a liquid name. It is the file everyone reaches for and it is **not loaded**. The **bi-weekly** consolidated file (`cdn.finra.org/equity/otcmarket/biweekly/shrt<date>.csv`, 2.75MB, 22,595 symbols) is the real position, and that is this.

**THE PAID FIELD IS THIS NUMBER.** `fetchProfile` computes `shortPctFloat = shares_short / float_shares × 100`; doing the same arithmetic with FINRA's position and our stored `floatShares` reproduces it to **0.00pt on NVDA, PTON, MU and JPM** (AAPL −0.08pt). So the provider adds nothing here, and the free source adds the **~8 years of history it does not sell at all** — 2017-12-29 onward, 210 readings for a long-listed name.

- **ONE CALL PER SYMBOL, not one per settlement date.** A symbol's whole history comes back in a single request — NVDA is 210 rows and **8.9KB**. The other shape, ~190 bulk files at 2.7MB, is 513MB to extract the same ~10MB. The API (`api.finra.org/data/group/otcMarket/name/consolidatedShortInterest`, keyless, POST) is **partitioned by `settlementDate`**, which its own 400 error tells you — so sorting needs that key, and the bulk file stays the right tool for the fortnightly top-up, where one file covers everyone.
- **`shortint-load.js` is local**, the `insider-load.js` precedent: dry run by default, `--commit`, `--only`, `--missing`, `--rate`. Measured: **10 symbols, 1,947 readings, 0.3 min**, so the universe is ~230k readings and ~35 minutes.

##### THE FIRST FULL RUN DIED AT 65% AND REPORTED SUCCESS (2026-09-28)
**Turso answered one batch write with an `HTTP 404`** — transient, the database was healthy a minute later — and the run stopped at **769 of 1,181 symbols, 142,682 readings**. Two separate faults, and the second is the one that hides the first.

- **THE WRITE SAT OUTSIDE THE TRY/CATCH.** Only the FETCH was guarded, so a database blip threw clean out of the loop and abandoned the remaining 412 symbols. It has its own catch now: a blip costs ONE symbol, never the run, and the symbol is left **unrecorded rather than marked checked**, so `--missing` picks it up — the insider walk's rule, where a day that fails does not advance the cursor. `ok`/`rows` are also incremented AFTER the write lands, or the receipt overstates what is actually stored.
- **`node script.js | tail` REPORTS `tail`'s EXIT CODE, NOT NODE'S.** The crash dump was right there in the output above a cheerful `[exited with code 0]`. This is the *inverse* of the lesson this file already carries for backfills — there, a batch succeeded and exited 1; here it failed and exited 0. **The rule that covers both: judge a load by the rows it stored, never by how it exited.** `readShortState()` answers that in one small read and is what was used to find the real figure.
- **The rate was also 3x my estimate**, and for the reason this file already records: the ten-symbol trial wrote into an EMPTY table at ~33 symbols/min; against a filling one it settled at ~10/min. **Budget a backfill by the archive it is writing INTO, not by the symbol count.**

#### It tops itself up now, and the cadence is DAILY because the data is fortnightly (2026-10-01, owner: "how best to schedule this")
**`GET /api/cron/shortint`, bearer-authenticated, run as its own phase by `nightly-ping.js` after the rotation completes — beside `secRotate()` and for the same reasons.** Until this, picking up a new settlement date meant remembering to run the local loader.

**THE CADENCE QUESTION WAS ASKED AS "why not weekly", AND THE DATA ANSWERS IT.** Measured off NVDA's 210 stored settlement dates rather than recalled: **exactly 24 a year, every year since 2018**, with gaps of 13–19 calendar days (85 at 15 days, 57 at 14, 25 at 16) and **not one of 7**. FINRA publishes the consolidated position twice a month — the 15th and the month end — so half of a weekly job would find nothing, and under the old tool that half was not free (see below).

- **DAILY, and that is not a contradiction.** A fortnightly *calendar* cannot line up with a gap that varies by six days: a fixed cron drifts off the real dates, sometimes firing before publication and sometimes twice for one settlement. A daily self-pacing check needs no calendar, catches a date within hours of publication, and **heals after any number of missed runs** — `noteTechMark`'s rule, which exists for exactly this.
- **THE DATE IS DISCOVERED BY ASKING FINRA, NEVER BY COMPUTING IT.** Settlement dates are the 15th and month end *rolled back over weekends AND market holidays*, which is where the 13–19 day spread comes from — so deriving them means maintaining a holiday list, and this project already refused that once: the intraday gate asks the provider whether the NYSE is open precisely so *"there is no holiday list to maintain"*. One POST for one liquid symbol (`SHORTINT_PROBE`, default NVDA) answers it exactly and cannot drift.
- **So a run writes nothing on almost every day, by design**: one `max(newest)` read, one POST, done. **Proved by reverting**: dropping the cutoff fails 2 and fetches the bulk file on a day with nothing new.
- **THE API CAN KNOW A DATE BEFORE THE BULK FILE IS POSTED**, so that is a reported state rather than a failure — `{pending: '2026-09-30'}`, and tomorrow's run collects it. An unreachable FINRA is the same: `{done, error}`, non-fatal, nothing written. This data is already **eight business days old** when it appears and nothing downstream reads it, so being a day late costs nothing.
- **ONE FILE COVERS EVERY SYMBOL.** The bi-weekly bulk file is 2.75MB and ~22,595 symbols against **1,192 per-symbol calls for the same ~1,192 rows**. The per-symbol API stays the right tool for a cold load of nine years; this is the right tool for one fortnight. `ShortInt.parse` already reads both shapes — it sniffs the delimiter and takes the bulk file's own date field — so **the split guard comes along for free**, which is the point of going through the parser rather than round it: FINRA does not restate for splits and ships a `changePercent` anyway (NVDA's 10:1 reads **+978.77%**), and `normalise` withholds it wherever `stockSplitFlag` is set.

##### `appendShortInterest` is the other half of a PAIR, and confusing the two would destroy the archive
`writeShortInterest` opens with `delete from short_interest where symbol = ?` — correct for a full per-symbol load, and catastrophic as a top-up: picking up one new fortnight would rewrite all **220,386** stored readings, ~2 hours at the measured ~10 symbols/min against a filling table. **The screens-incident rule, which cost thirteen screens their filters: a restore or a top-up must not pass through a transforming path.**

- **IDEMPOTENT BY CONSTRUCTION**, which is what makes a daily check safe to run as often as it likes: the primary key is `(symbol, d)`, so the same date twice writes the same rows twice and changes nothing.
- **`rows` is RECOUNTED, never incremented.** An increment drifts upward every time a date is re-applied, and a receipt that overstates what is stored is the exact failure this loader's own history records. The recount is a correlated `count(*)` per symbol **inside one statement per 200-symbol chunk** — on this database the cost is per STATEMENT rather than per row (measured 23x on `tech_history`), so 1,192 single updates would be the slowest part of a job whose data write is six statements.
- **`newest` only ever moves FORWARD.** Re-applying an older date must not drag a symbol's clock backwards and make it read as stale.
- **`shortNewest()` takes the MAX, not the min**, and it is one small read of `short_state` (1,192 rows) rather than `max(d)` over the 220k-row readings table. **Two symbols lag for ever for real reasons** — SQ stopped reporting when Block renamed its ticker (last reading 2025-01-15) and FI stands at 2025-10-31 — so the minimum would report us a year behind while 1,190 of 1,192 are current, and the job would fetch on every single run. **Proved by reverting**: the min fails 5.

#### NO DEPENDENCY, WHICH IS WHY THIS IS A CRON PHASE AND NOT A LOCAL LOADER
An xlsx is a ZIP of DEFLATE entries and node ships `zlib`, so `holdings.js` reads the workbook in ~40 lines of `inflateRawSync`. - `Expand-Archive` refuses a `.xlsx` extension outright (*".zip is the only supported archive file format"*), which is worth knowing before reaching for it to inspect one by hand — copy it to `.zip` first.

`fund_holdings (fund, symbol, d, weight, shares)` keyed on all three, plus `fund_state` for the per-fund clock — the `short_interest` / `short_state` shape exactly.

- **`fund` IS THERE FROM THE FIRST DAY though only SPY is wired.** Measured 2026-10-02: **SPY, DIA, MDY, XLK, XLF and XLV all answer 200 with a real xlsx at the same URL shape** — the sector SPDRs being the eleven we already hold as the Sector Benchmark theme. A second index is a config entry rather than a migration.

- **`fund_holdings` is on `query-plan-test`'s big-table list FROM THE DAY IT SHIPPED**, which is what `insider_trans` had to learn the hard way (its quarter-delete ranged on an unindexed `filed` — a full walk the guard could not see). `idx_fund_holdings_d (fund, d)` is what makes "everything the index held on date X" a seek; the primary key leads on symbol and cannot answer it. #### DAILY, because the weights move daily even though membership does not
Membership changes perhaps 20-25 times a year (the quarterly rebalance plus ad-hoc for M&A), which argues for a weekly check. **Weights move every session**, and the file is published daily and only for today, which argues for daily. Daily also needs no calendar, catches a change within hours, and **heals after any number of missed runs** — `noteTechMark`'s rule. On a day when the file's as-of date has not moved it writes nothing.

- **A FAILURE MUST NOT BLANK A SINGLE HOLDING, and here that matters more than anywhere this rule has applied before.** `noteSecMiss` made this mistake once with the CIK and silently broke the insider card for 64 filers; a membership flag emptied by a transient refusal would look like **504 deletions**, with nothing on screen to say otherwise. `noteFundMiss` records the miss and **carries `as_of` and `holdings` forward**, so the clock keeps saying how old the data genuinely is.
- **THE TRUNCATION FLOOR (450) IS THE GUARD THAT MATTERS MOST.** S&P targets 500 names and the count has sat between 500 and 505 for decades, so no real file lands under 450 — which makes a short one a partial download, an error page or a changed layout, never news. **Probed at exactly 450 and 449**, since a threshold is a step function. **Proved by reverting**: the floor removed, a 12-holding file applies and fails 10.
- **An honest User-Agent, CHECKED rather than copied.** The NASDAQ loader needs a browser-shaped one because that endpoint answers a bot-shaped agent by **never replying at all**. Measured here: SSGA serves this file identically with an honest agent, a browser one, and none at all — so there is no reason to impersonate anything, and `SSGA_UA` reuses `SEC_UA`'s fallback.
- **A ticker has to LOOK like one.** The file carries two non-equity rows — a cash line whose ticker is `-` at 0.269% and `2602335D` at 0.000003% — so a digit anywhere is refused while dots are kept. **Proved by reverting**: the cash-equivalent row becomes a holding, 504 goes to 505, and 13 checks fail.

There is no Date object in that function, so it cannot apply.

#### A REVERT PROOF THAT PREDICTED ZERO FOUND A REAL BUG
The route's as-of cutoff was written off as an efficiency guard — the write is idempotent on `(fund, symbol, d)`, so a repeat stores identical rows — and the revert was expected to cost nothing. **It failed 3.** `appendFundHoldings` guarded `as_of` to move forward only and wrote `holdings` **unconditionally**, so re-applying an older file left the clock reading *"2026-10-02, 504 holdings"* on a date that had 501. **The two fields now move together or not at all**, and only then does the cutoff revert settle at the 2 failures it should have: both assertions about the `already` response it exists to produce, with no data check failing.

#### THE FIRST LIVE IMPORT'S RECEIPT WAS WRONG, AND THE SUITE PASSED OVER IT

  - **A row's `textContent` opens with the markup's own indentation**, so `startsWith(sym)` matches nothing and the row finder silently returns `[]` — which read as an off-by-six offset and eight alignment failures over a table that was correct. Trim first.
  - `sitecols-test.js` fails 5 and it is **PRE-EXISTING** — it hardcodes a catalogue of 83 against the live 94. `/columns` parses the header row, so the catalogue grows on its own, as designed.

### A filed fundamental as a chart layer (2026-09-29, owner's request)
**A `FUND` button and a metric picker: revenue or net income, trailing twelve months, from the company's own SEC filings, drawn as a step in its own strip beneath the chart — the FIRST of the three optional layers there.** The owner asked for "a line on the individual stock chart on fundamental, maybe a different axis, using the data from SEC EDGAR", chose the pane when the axis was argued against, and then moved the pane out of the svg and set the order of all three layers.

**A SECOND Y-AXIS WAS THE THING TO REFUSE, and that is the design decision worth keeping.** Two independent scales can be slid against each other until any two series appear to track, and the "divergence" a reader then sees is an artefact of where each zero was put. This chart had settled that once already: the stock overlay is **rebased** rather than given its own axis, and `/compare` follows the same rule.

**IT WAS A PANE INSIDE THE SVG FOR AN HOUR, and the strip is better twice over.** The owner's instruction was *"Fundamental panel should come first and it should appear below the chart, Earning should be right below fundamental, and short can be where it is"*. As a strip the three layers read as **one stack in one place** rather than one being inside the chart and two outside — and it drops the four-way coupling a pane forced between `rowcard.js`'s bands and this page's box heights, which this file already names as a trap. The chart is back to its two documented heights, and there is a check that handing `chartSVG` a `fund` option now changes nothing at all.

- **ORDER, in the toolbar and under the chart, and they match**: FUND, EARN, SHORT. The metric picker travels WITH the toggle it configures — the tile controls' rule — and is hidden while the strip is off, since a control that can change nothing reads as broken.
- **ALL THREE DEFAULT TO OFF (the owner, the same day).** The rug and the short strip used to arrive switched on. `=== '1'` rather than `!== '0'`, so someone who deliberately switched one ON keeps it while someone who never touched it starts clean. That chart carries the price, two moving averages, up to five overlays, the trend ribbon, an RSI pane and a volume pane before any of these three.
- **IT COSTS NO ENDPOINT AND NO READ.** `/api/sec` is already fetched on that page after the chart, so the series rides it — `ttmSeries` is one more field on a response the page was already waiting for. The earnings rug's bargain exactly.
- **Repainted ALONE**, the short strip's rule: a full `paintChart()` would rebuild the plot, the ribbon and every overlay to switch one layer beneath them. That is the other thing the strip bought — as a pane it could not be.

#### The four decisions that make it honest
- **TRAILING TWELVE MONTHS, never raw quarterly.** A quarterly line is a seasonal sawtooth that says nothing about trajectory. `SecFacts.ttmSeries` rolls the identical four-quarter window `ttm()` already uses back down the trail, so the newest step IS the TTM row on the card below it.
- **FROM `latestFilled`, NOT `latestPerPeriod`, AND THE TWO CAN NEVER DISAGREE ABOUT A NUMBER.** Filling only ever supplies a figure the winning filing left BLANK, so where the card's TTM row exists the strip equals it, and where that row is absent the strip still has a line. Coverage 65% → 90%. That property is what makes two different bases safe on one page, and there is a check on it in both directions.
- **`periodEnd` WITH THE LATEST RESTATEMENT, matching the card immediately above it.** The other coherent pairing is `filed` with as-first-reported — `visibleAsOf` already does exactly that, and it is what `/adjustedbacktest` uses. **Mixing them is the worst option**: a point plotted at a 2024 filing date carrying a 2026 restatement is incoherent. The cost is that the step lands some weeks before the figure was public, **so the note says in as many words that this cannot be read for lead or lag** — that is a timing claim, and the backtest is where timing claims get tested.
- **A STEP, NEVER A SLOPE, WITH A BOUNDED CARRY.** A filed figure stands until the next filing replaces it; a slope between two quarters would claim it drifted day by day. `RowCard.stepSeries` lays the quarterly points onto the session axis and stops carrying after **400 days** — without a bound a company that stopped filing draws a flat line to today, which reads as *revenue held* rather than *the record stops here*.

#### Its own range, not zero — and the zero line that follows
The short-interest strip's rule and its reason: a trailing year sits in a narrow band over a short window, so a zero base spends the whole strip on empty space. **A line carries no measured-from-zero claim the way a bar does**, which is what makes that legitimate — and the note says the baseline is not zero and prints the range. **Where the DATA spans zero the zero line is drawn**, which is the whole difference between a revenue line and a net-income one: profitable or not is the reading, and a line with no zero on it cannot say which side it is on.

- **THE TEST IS THE DATA, NEVER A PADDED BOUND, and PRODUCTION is what caught that.** While it was still a pane the axis carried 12% of value padding, and MU's trailing revenue runs **$8.0B to $90.3B** — so the padded floor landed at **-$1.9B** and a zero line was drawn on a REVENUE chart, implying a crossing that cannot happen. A fixture with a narrow range passes either way; the check needs a wide positive series. The strip pads in PIXELS instead, so the value range is exactly the data's and the hazard is gone by construction.
- **TWO MORE FOUND BY SCREENSHOT while it was a pane, both about printing a padded bound.** The gutter labels read `$10.2B` at the top of a series whose highest filed figure was `$9.0B` — a number no company ever reported — and fixing that put the label exactly on the run that reaches the maximum, which on a rising series is the run at the right edge where the labels are. The strip carries no gutter labels at all: the range goes in the note, as the short strip's does.
- **NEUTRAL, deliberately** — `--text`, the short strip's colour. Green and red are the PRICE's direction on this chart, and *revenue rose* is not a direction the price went.

#### Worth knowing
- **ONE PATH COMMAND PER CHANGE OF LEVEL, not one per session.** A step holds its value for a quarter, so writing a segment at every bar put ~5,200 of them in a Max chart — about 42KB of markup for a line with forty levels. Measured live on MU at Max: **64 levels in a 774-character path**.
- **`preserveAspectRatio="none"` is what keeps the steps aligned with the price above**, and is exactly why the stroke carries `vector-effect: non-scaling-stroke` — without it the flat runs and the vertical risers draw at different weights. No `<text>` inside, for the same reason.
- **A pre-revenue company gets no line at all**, because `ttm()` refuses a sum whose revenue is under its floor and that floor is load-bearing where `ttm` feeds the margin comparison. Measured: 3 of 1,181 symbols. A second summation here would be the drift `secfacts.js` exists to prevent.
- **THE BOUNDARY IS UNTOUCHED**: no `FIELD_SPEC` entry, no snapshot stamping, no screener column. A chart layer is squarely on the display side of the line the SEC card's own condition drew, and there is a check that no filings series reaches a screener row.
- Verified: **50 checks** — the rolling window against hand-computed sums, a gap refusing every window that spanned it, either concept null on its own, the `latestFilled` coverage claim proved BOTH ways (the same fixture loses the newest window under `latestPerPeriod` and keeps it under `latestFilled`, at the same number), the carry and its bound, the drawn strip measured below the chart and inside the same 52px gutter as the other two, the chart's own height unmoved, **every path segment flat or vertical**, the zero line present and absent, the note's four claims, the order of the buttons and of the strips, and the self-gating. **Proved by reverting four times**: the strip drawn second puts the rug above it, defaults back to on fails 4, a slope gives 3 diagonal segments of 5, and a strip that never paints fails 2.
  - **TWO EXISTING SUITES ASSUMED THEIR LAYER ARRIVED ON** and had to be told, not worked around: `around-test.js` and `shortint-test.js` both measured a layer that is now off. They seed the remembered choice in an `addInitScript` — **but only where nothing is stored yet**, because setting it on every navigation puts the layer back after the suite switches it off and silently defeats the two checks that it is remembered per device. That cost two failures before it was noticed.
  - **`rowcard.js` ASSIGNS TO `globalThis`, NOT `module.exports`** — one file for the browser and Node, and server.js requires it for the side effect. `const RowCard = require(...)` returns `{}`, and every call on it is a TypeError.
  - **A fixture whose quarter-ends are 91-DAY STEPS does not land on calendar quarter-ends.** The gap check filtered `2025-06-30`, which the generator never produced, removed nothing, and passed over an intact fixture.
  - **A REVERT PROOF THAT DOES NOT RUN THE TEST REPORTS ZERO FAILURES**, which reads exactly like guards that were never load-bearing — all four did on the first attempt, because the harness built a `node` path out of `sys.executable`. It counts PASSes now and refuses to report on a run that did not happen.

### Around earnings — how one stock behaves around its own reports (2026-09-28, owner's request)
**Inside the earnings card on `/stock`: one faint line per quarter rebased to its report-day close, the median over them, an interquartile band, and four figures.** The owner asked for the aggregate rather than markers on the price chart — *"how does this stock typically behave around earnings"* — and explicitly ruled out drawing on the chart itself.

- **`earnstudy.js` is pure** (bars + events → the aggregate), the `news.js` / `secfacts.js` / `insider.js` shape, which is what lets every statistic be tested with no server. `/api/stock` gains one `readBars(symbol, 2600)` in the Promise.all it already runs, and ships ~2-8KB of aggregate rather than ten years of closes for the browser to re-derive.
- **THE REACTION IS t → t+1, and that single choice decides whether the card is true.** Measured on the live table: **34,499 of 34,754 events are After Hours** (235 pre-market, 20 unspecified), so the session a report lands on does not contain the market's answer. Verified against NVDA rather than assumed — `t → t+1` gives +8.7 / −1.8 / −5.5 / −0.6 / −3.2 / −0.8 while `t−1 → t` is small. **The drift is measured FROM the reaction**, so the jump is never counted twice.
- **`POST` is 42 sessions, which is not a round number.** A quarter is ~63 trading days, so a longer window runs into the NEXT report and starts measuring that instead.
- **THE BEAT/MISS SPLIT IS DEGENERATE ON REAL DATA and is withheld rather than printed.** NVDA has **24 beats against 2 misses**, AAPL 24 against 1 — companies beat nearly every quarter, so "the median move after a miss" is routinely a median of one, set in the same type as a median of twenty-four. `MIN_SPLIT` (4) counts the thin side and withholds its median, and the note says misses are rare, which is the useful fact.
- **Two numbers, because one describes it wrongly.** NVDA's signed median reaction is **−0.22%** and its median ABSOLUTE reaction is **2.34%**: it moves hard and not predictably in one direction, and either figure alone misleads.
- **The hit rate carries the stock's own base rate** — "44% against 49.3% on an ordinary session". A stock that rises most days hands a flattering number to a signal that knows nothing; the research log's rule, and it costs no second source.
- **A FILTER I NEARLY BUILT AND THE MEASUREMENT KILLED.** 9.56% of consecutive events sit under 45 days apart (Alcoa has a pair 8 days apart; NVDA has one 33 days before the next), so I assumed those were non-report rows diluting the medians toward an ordinary day. **Measured: they move MORE, not less** — NVDA's crowded pair has a 3.05% median absolute reaction against 2.34% for the spaced ones, MU's 5.42%, and dropping them moves NVDA's median by 0.27 points. They are real events. No filter, and the hypothesis is recorded so it is not rebuilt.
- **HTML labels, never SVG text** — the box is stretched to the page width — and every stroke carries `vector-effect: non-scaling-stroke`, or a horizontal line and a vertical one draw at different weights. There is a check that the svg contains no `<text>` at all.
- **Hidden with a per-device toggle** (`localStorage`, the `/compare` and `/m` call) rather than a prefs flag: a prefs flag follows the account but would cost this page a round trip it does not currently make, and a guest has no prefs key. **The button is always visible and labelled with the direction it will go** — a hide with no way back is a trapdoor.
- **It is DESCRIPTION, not a signal**, and the note says so: drift after earnings is untested here (backlog entry 1) and ten of eleven framings in the research log came back flat.
**AND THE RUG — when, in price context (the same day, once the aggregate was in).** One tick per report **under** the plot, never on it: the owner ruled out drawing on the chart, and a tick beneath a gap answers *"was that earnings?"* without fighting the price line. An `EARN` button sits with the other chart layers, remembered per device.

- **It costs nothing.** `earnRows` is already on the page for the earnings card, so the rug is a second reading of data in hand — no fetch, no query, no endpoint.
- **SELF-GATING rather than range-gated.** It draws whatever falls inside the window and hides when nothing does, so a 1W chart is clean with no rule about which ranges may have it, and a report that *did* land in a short window is still shown. Measured on the fixture: 3 ticks at 1Y, 0 at 1M, 9 at Max.
- **Aligned by the ribbon's own trick** — `margin-right: 52px` to clear the price axis, children placed by percentage — so the ticks, the crosshair and the plot cannot disagree.
- **IT WAS A ROW OF NEUTRAL TICKS FOR AN HOUR, AND THAT WAS THE OWNER'S NEXT MESSAGE** — *"the marker is not showing how good or bad the earning was, can't you do something to make it visually intelligent"*. A tick answers *when* and says nothing about *what happened*, which is most of why anyone looks. It is a **signed bar chart** now: **colour is the direction of the next session's move, height is its magnitude**, growing from a centre baseline, the strip at 38px (the owner offered the room — "you can also increase the height as this is just optional").
  - **I HAD ARGUED AGAINST COLOURING IT AND THE ARGUMENT WAS ONLY HALF RIGHT.** Green and red mean up and down on this chart, so a green **beat** tick beside a red session really would make a different claim from the one the colour carries everywhere else — but colouring by the **reaction** makes *exactly* the same claim as the price line above it. The objection was to the beat/miss encoding, and I had generalised it to colour as such. Beat or miss stays in the tooltip, where it costs nothing and claims nothing.
  - **THE CEILING IS THE 90th PERCENTILE OF WHAT IS IN VIEW, floored at 3%** — `rugScale()`, the `earnScale()` precedent from the surprise strip on the same page, and for the same reason: a single +40% reaction would flatten every ordinary quarter to a hairline. A bar past the ceiling is drawn full height, marked `.capped` and **says so on hover** rather than being silently clipped.
  - **A REPORT WITH NO NEXT SESSION YET IS A NEUTRAL STUB, NOT A ZERO-HEIGHT BAR.** The newest report has no t+1 until tomorrow, and a bar of no height in a chart where height is magnitude reads as *it did nothing* — which is a statement, and a false one. `.pending` is drawn as the old tick.
  - **Every bar keeps a 2px floor**, so a 0.1% reaction is a mark rather than a vanishing act — the Size card's `·` rule: a small thing drawn at its honest size still has to be visible.
- **THE EMPTY STRETCH IS EXPLAINED.** The bar archive reaches back twenty years and the earnings record does not, so the note says where the stored record starts — on a Max chart the absence is most of the picture and would otherwise read as "no reports happened".
- **`payload` IS NOT IN SCOPE INSIDE `paintChart`, and reaching for it killed the whole chart.** It is a local of the boot IIFE; `paintChart` lives at script scope. The first cut threw a ReferenceError, the chart never drew, and the page still *looked* half-alive because the earnings card is rendered by the page template. Use `earnRows`, which is the page's own script-scope copy.
- Verified: **+24 checks** — ticks positioned by date rather than by DOM order (`earnRows` is newest-first, so "ascending across the width" tests the emission order and nothing a reader sees), the unclassified quarter claiming no verdict, the count per range, the record-start note present on Max and correctly absent at 1Y, the toggle surviving a reload, and five on the encoding: direction, **colour MEASURED as drawn** (`getComputedStyle`, not the class name — a check on `.up` would pass against a stylesheet that painted both the same), heights in proportion, the 2px floor, and the note naming the scale.
  - **THE HEIGHT ASSERTION WAS WRITTEN WEAK AND CAUGHT BY ITS OWN REVERT-PROOF.** `h <= previous + tolerance` passes if every bar is the **same** height, which is the exact regression worth catching — the encoding would be decoration and nothing would say so. It is strictly taller now, with a real gap between the largest and smallest; flattening every bar to a constant fails it (8%/6%/4% all at 10px).
  - **Five of the earlier checks failed first and every one was the test, not the page**: it expected 9 ticks on a 1Y window that holds 3, asserted DOM order, and read the verdict off the newest event — which is deliberately the one with no estimate.

- Verified: **29 checks** on the statistics with no server (the fixture jumps on t+1, so anything reading the report day reads zero; it straddles the beat/miss floor with six beats and two misses so both branches run) plus **52** driving the real page.
  - **A screenshot found what the assertions did not**, twice: the bottom axis figure sat under the first x label, and zero rendered as "+0%".
