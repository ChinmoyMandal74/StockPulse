## The trend-only backtest (built and measured)
**A backtest that reads NOTHING but bars, so it can go back twenty years instead of two months (2026-09-19, owner's request).** The advice backtest is capped at two months because `fundamentals_history` begins 2026-08-30 and everything before that would be imputed. Trend, entry, RSI, volume, the moving averages and the 52-week position are all bar-derived, and `action.js` already carries a fundamentals-free rule list — **`etfRules`**, reached whenever a row has neither Quality nor a forward P/E. So there is no new engine and no fork: this runs the same rules the ETF rows run today, which is also what `action-backtest.js` has replayed over eighteen years.

**Decided with the owner before building**: the full all-technical verdict rather than the Trend word alone; a rolling sweep of many start dates rather than one window; a nightly table in Turso rather than committed JSON; and the bar backfill first.

- **Phase 0 — the backfill, done.** See the bar-archive section: 640,433 bars, the archive at **1.71M rows back to 2003**, and stocks reaching ten years up from 211 to **332**. Without it a nineteen-year sweep would silently have run on the deepest-held 39% of the universe, which is also its most survivor-biased slice.
- **Phase 1 — `techrow.js` and the `tech_history` table, done.**
  - **`techrow.js` exists because there were TWO row builders and this file claimed there was one.** `btRowAt` in server.js fed the advice backtest; `action-backtest.js` built its own inline. They had drifted in two ways that change verdicts: the 52-week high was the **intraday high** in one and the **closing high** in the other (measured 1.83 points apart on a test series, against a `near_high` threshold of −10), and `volTrend` was absent from the study's row entirely, so the `distribution` Avoid rule could never fire in eighteen years of it. Both now call the shared builder. Proven identical to the old server function by lifting it out of the previous commit and comparing 12,782 numbers across 900 sessions — worst gap **0**.
  - **`tech_history` stores the engine's INPUTS, not just its verdict** — `vs200`, `vs50`, `rsi`, `m1`, `m3`, `from_high`, `vol_trend`, `history_days` are every field the all-technical rules read, plus `close` so a sweep never touches `bars` at all. That makes the table **rule-set agnostic**: a page re-evaluates any preset at read time instead of being locked to whichever one the builder baked in. `action`/`flag` are kept as the Balanced reading for the fast path.
  - **Weekly marks, aligned across symbols.** The canonical mark is the last trading day of each week taken from the UNION of all dates, not per symbol — otherwise an equal-weight basket is not measured on one day. A symbol with no session within `STALE_DAYS` (8) of a mark is skipped rather than carried forward.
  - **It is REBUILDABLE**, unlike `fundamentals_history`: every field derives from bars. Delete the table and re-run `build-tech-history.js`.
  - **The nightly top-up is self-pacing** (`noteTechMark`): write a mark when the newest one is seven days old. No weekday arithmetic, no holiday list, and it heals after a missed week — where "write it on Fridays" would silently skip whenever a Friday was a holiday. It rides the Refresh-all gate beside `fundamentals_history` and `noteAdvice`, and costs nothing: every technical it stores was already computed for that row. **It stores the ALL-TECHNICAL verdict, deliberately not `row.action`** — mixing the two would make the column mean one thing before today and another after it.
  - **Multi-row inserts, and the measurement that forced them.** `writeTechHistory` first sent one statement per row in batches of 500, the shape every other writer here uses. Against production that measured **~1,000 rows a minute — five hours for the table.** The cost is per STATEMENT, not per row: 200 rows in one `values (...),(...)` runs at **23,525 rows a minute, a 23x speedup**, and the same 333k rows in about thirteen minutes. Worth remembering before any other bulk write here.
  - **REBUILT ON THE DEEPENED ARCHIVE, 2026-09-28: 891,537 rows, 1,152 symbols, 2001-03-21 → 2026-09-28**, against 332,883 / 421 / 2004-01-14 before it. **571,519 rows written in 9.6 minutes — ~59,500 rows/min, against the 23,525/min this file records** — so the multi-row insert scales better than the first measurement suggested. 0 failures, 0 retries, no deadline fired.
    - **The usable floor is what actually moved. 733 symbols priceable in 2008 against 278**, rising to 1,152 in 2026. The 2.9M-bar deepening that morning is what bought it, and this is the surface it was bought for.
    - **The sub-cent floor landed in `techrow.js` FIRST** — see the bar-archive section. Verified afterwards on the rebuilt table rather than assumed: **all 112 of APLD's sub-cent-close marks in 2013-2015 read `Hold`, none carries a `vs200` or `m1`, and none reads `Buy`.** The first version of that check counted `vs200` across the whole window and proved nothing, because APLD oscillated around a penny and a mark at or above a cent may legitimately have one — **the test has to be the sub-cent marks alone.**
    - **THE RESUME CAN NEVER CONVERGE, and it is not a fault in the rebuild.** `have[sym] === n` is an exact COUNT test against the builder's own canonical calendar — the last trading day of each week — while `noteTechMark` adds nightly marks dated by the market day, which land on whatever weekday the Refresh all ran. Measured: MU holds Wednesday marks (09-02, 09-09, 09-16) beside the canonical Fridays. Both are legitimate readings and they coexist; the cost is that **749 of 1,152 symbols mismatch for ever and every full rebuild rewrites ~570k rows** (0.8 min on a no-op pass). Worth knowing before reading a resume line as damage.
  - *(superseded by the rebuild above)* **The table was COMPLETE as of 2026-09-20: 332,883 rows, 421 symbols, 2004-01-14 → 2026-09-18.** The final resumed pass wrote 104,960 rows in 278 minutes.
  - **A dropped socket does not throw for HOURS, and the retry only fires on a throw.** That same run lost **159 minutes — 2h39m of its 4h38m — to one chunk**: the write at 60,000 stalled at the 72-minute mark and the `terminated` error did not surface until 231 minutes. `writeTechHistory` awaits `db.batch` with no deadline, so between the socket dying and libSQL noticing there is nothing to catch and nothing printed. From the outside it is indistinguishable from a hang: **the process stays alive, CPU sits near zero, stdout says nothing** — and that is exactly what it looks like when it is working normally too, because the job is network-bound. **So do not judge this build by CPU or by silence; judge it by the row count.** I called a healthy run dead on those signals and was wrong.
    - **Fixed the same day: `withDeadline(label, ms, run)` in db.js, and every `writeTechHistory` batch now runs under one.** It races the request against a clock, so silence becomes an ordinary throw — which `noteTechMark`'s caller already catches and logs, the rule that a history write never fails a refresh. `DB_BATCH_TIMEOUT_MS` (60s) is the default, chosen to suit a REQUEST path; the two real callers each say why they differ: **`noteTechMark` passes 30s** (optional work in the tail, and a missed mark self-heals because the writer is self-pacing) and **the offline builder passes 15 minutes**, because a *healthy* batch there takes 3-6 minutes against an archive this size. That still turns 159 minutes of silence into one retry.
      - **The request is NOT cancelled** — libSQL offers no handle for that — so this is only safe because every caller is an idempotent upsert. A caller that is not must not use it. The abandoned request settles minutes later and `withDeadline` attaches its own `catch`, or that late rejection arrives unhandled and takes the process down long after the code that started it gave up.
      - **`timer.unref()` was in the first cut and is a trap: never add it back.** An unref'd timer does not hold the event loop open, so in a CLI script — the builder, the one place this most has to work — a hung request leaves nothing else pending and **Node exits 0 mid-write instead of throwing**, turning the hang into a silent success. Caught by the test, which is itself a bare script; an assertion names the cause.
      - Verified: 13 checks, and the fixture is the point — **every hang is a promise that never settles**, not a rejection, because that is what actually happened. A stubbed throw would have proved nothing.
  - **`Buy with Risk` never appears** — `etfRules` has no path to it. The trend-only backtest has five usable tiers, not six.
  - **What the archive actually supports**: marks span 2004 → today, but 2004-2007 carry ONE symbol (the 5,000-bar API ceiling puts most deep symbols at 2006-11, plus 252 sessions of run-up). **The usable floor is 2008 with 278 symbols**, rising to 421 today — which does cover the financial crisis, the one stretch where a trend rule should prove itself.
  - Verified: the extraction (10 checks, identical to the last commit's function), the store round trip (18 checks — the round trip, replace-not-duplicate, windowing, purge, blanks staying null, and that **every rule set re-derives from a stored row**), the nightly mark through the REAL refresh path (11 checks — a Refresh all writes, a second the same day does not, a week later does, a plain Refresh never does, and the stored verdict re-derives), and the query plans against 344,000 rows (every read SEEKS; a one-year window is 22,360 rows in 60ms).
  - **Two fixture traps, both caught by an assertion passing on an empty set.** The refresh test stubbed the provider to refuse everything, so the refresh produced no rows and "every mark re-derives" passed over zero marks; it needs a price stub serving the fixture's own bars, **including the benchmark** — SPY is fetched on every refresh and is deliberately never archived, so returning an error for it failed the whole round. And the store test hand-wrote `action: 'Buy'` beside technicals that genuinely read Strong Buy; the fixture now derives the verdict the way the builder does.
- **Phase 2 — the sweep and the page, done.** `GET /api/trend-backtest` and **`/trend-backtest`** (admin, a row in the console's Research section beside the advice one).
  - **A FIXED forward horizon** (1M/3M/6M/1Y as 1/3/6/12 MONTHLY marks), never "carry to today" — that would give a 2008 window eighteen years and a 2026 one a fortnight, and averaging those compares nothing. *It was 4/13/26/52 weekly marks until 2026-09-20; see the read cost below for why whole months, and note the page had always said "held three months" while the code meant 91 days.*
  - **The benchmark is the equal-weight universe over the same window**, so the question is whether the rules SELECTED. Excess is basket − market, per window.
  - **A window with no picks is CASH, not a missing observation** — it returns 0 and its excess is minus the market. Dropping those would delete exactly the 2008-shaped windows a trend rule exists for, and would flatter every remaining one. **Thin windows (under 3 picks) are flagged, never filtered**: a filter there is a thumb on the scale.
  - **The two honesty numbers are computed and printed, not left to the reader.** The **effective sample** — monthly starts at a 3-month horizon overlap ~3:1, so ~220 windows is ~74 independent readings — is a CARD, not a footnote. And the **pool per window**, since it grows from 89 to 129 (and will grow further) across the span, so an early window is a narrower claim.
  - **`Buy with Risk` is shown struck through and disabled**: `etfRules` has no path to it, so the trend backtest has five usable tiers. Hiding it would read as an oversight.
  - **THE FIRST FULL-UNIVERSE READING (2026-09-20, the complete 332,883-row table — this is the number the module was built to produce).** 3M horizon, Strong Buy + Buy, Balanced, from 2008: **222 windows spanning 2008-01-02 → 2026-06-03, on a pool growing 268 → 417**, and **74 independent readings** after the 3x overlap discount.

| | median excess | hit rate | avg picks |
|---|---|---|---|
| all years (222 windows) | **−0.32%** | **44.6%** | 108 of 336 |
| pre-2020 (144) | −0.27% | 47.2% | 106 |
| post-2020 (78) | −0.89% | 39.7% | 112 |

##### RE-MEASURED ON THE REBUILT TABLE (2026-09-28) — 2.7x the rows, and it got WORSE
**891,537 rows across 1,152 symbols, 733 of them priceable in 2008 against 278.** Same question, same settings: 3M horizon, Strong Buy + Buy, Balanced, from 2008.

| | median excess | hit rate | avg picks |
|---|---|---|---|
| all years (222 windows) | **−0.51%** | **35.6%** | 288 |
| pre-2020 (144) | −0.38% | 36.1% | 279 |
| post-2020 (78) | −0.92% | 34.6% | 304 |

Mean −2.17%, p10/p90 −5.5/+1.6, worst −85.8%, best +6.2%. **Against the 421-symbol reading of −0.32% and 44.6%, the hit rate fell by nine points in both eras.** That is the direction the universe section predicts and the reassuring one: *if a result improves after the universe shrinks, suspect the universe* — here the pool grew and got deeper history, and the verdicts looked worse. The standing conclusion is unchanged and now rests on far more data: **the model orders downside and does not pick winners.** The "barely a selection" caveat also stands — 288 picks is still about a third of the pool.
 **The verdicts do not select: the basket loses to its own universe by a small margin, in both eras, and beats it less than half the time.** That is the research log's standing conclusion — the model orders downside and does not pick winners — now measured on twenty years rather than inferred from two months, and it agrees with the 18-year `action-backtest.js` re-measurement taken the same day on 1,588,936 stock-days.
    - **The likeliest reason is that this is barely a selection at all**: Strong Buy + Buy qualifies **108 of 336 stocks**, about a third of the universe, so the basket is close to being the benchmark with a third of the names dropped. A test with more discrimination (a top-N cut inside the tier, or Strong Buy alone) is the obvious next question and has not been run.
    - **The distribution is narrow and left-skewed** — p10 −4.4 against p90 +2.7, worst −36.8 against best +6.7. The big negative tail is the 2009 whipsaw the partial reading already showed, and it is what a trend rule does at a violent bottom, not a defect.
  - *(superseded by the reading above)* **First real reading (partial table, 89→129 symbols, 3M horizon, Strong Buy + Buy)**: median excess **−0.65%**, hit rate 42%, and the crisis behaves exactly as a trend rule should — picks fall 22 → 2 through the 2008 fall while beating equal weight by 3–15 points, then **1 pick through the March 2009 snap-back** for −28% and −40% excess. That whipsaw is also what the −132% worst window at 1Y is, and it is not a bug. Across all windows the rules slightly underperform, at every horizon, which is the research log's standing verdict now measurable from a page.
  - Verified: 31 checks on the sweep (basket, benchmark, excess, horizon and the overlap discount all against hand-computed values; a symbol with no end price excluded from BOTH legs; cash windows; theme scoping; the rule set re-evaluated from the stored inputs) and 31 on the page (both charts drawn and actually distributions, the table agreeing row-for-row with the API, sorting, the URL round trip, and **the honesty prose asserted as text** — a page that reports a median over overlapping windows without saying how few are independent is the exact failure this module exists to avoid).
  - **`#cards > .card`, not `#cards .card`** — the double-bezel puts the class on the shell AND the core, so the descendant selector counts every card twice. The `/quality` trap, met again in a test rather than in the page.
  - **The sweep read the WHOLE table, and that was three minutes (2026-09-20).** Measured against production: **181.8 seconds cold**, 0.4s warm, for a sweep whose arithmetic is **154ms** — so essentially all of it was moving 232,000 rows out of Turso, on a table still growing toward 333,000, against a documented response wall of about three minutes. The page was days from simply failing, and nothing on it would have said why. Three narrowings, in the order they mattered:
    - **The DATES.** The sweep starts on month-firsts, and with WHOLE-MONTH horizons it ends on one too — so one cached read of **273 marks out of 1,185** serves all four horizons. In weekly marks the end dates were arbitrary weeks, which meant reading the other three weeks of every month for the sake of them. This is the reason the horizon is months now; the statistics are if anything better, since "started in March 2012 and held three months" is what the page always claimed.
    - **The COLUMNS.** Balanced reads the stored verdict and the close and nothing else, so it asks for **four of fourteen**; a non-default rule set re-runs the engine and gets its own cached copy with the inputs. **The trap that buys is handing the slim row to a rule set that evaluates** — every input would be undefined, "No trend data" would fire on every row, and every window would quietly go to cash, which looks like a result. There is a test that runs a non-Balanced sweep with the slim copy warm in the cache and asserts it still finds picks.
  - **MEASURE ON AN IDLE DATABASE, or you will design around the wrong number.** Every figure in the bullet above was first taken while the `tech_history` bulk build was writing to the same database, and contention inflated them by 3-70x. Re-measured idle, at the table's full 332,883 rows:

| read | contended | idle |
|---|---|---|
| the whole table, 14 columns (the old path) | 181.8s *(at 232k rows)* | **126.2s / 106.7MB — and `terminated` on another run** |
| month-first marks, 4 columns (Balanced) | 64.6s | **5.7-7.7s** |
| month-first marks, 14 columns (other rule sets) | 166.8s | **23-25s** |
| `select distinct d` for the mark calendar | 429.9s | **~250ms** |

    **The old path is MARGINAL rather than merely slow, and that is the real finding.** Two idle runs minutes apart: one carried all 332,883 rows in 126.2s, the next answered `terminated`. 106.7MB is past the 83MB at which this file's own wall table records a failure, so the page worked or failed by luck on every cold instance — the hardest kind of fault to diagnose, because reproducing it is a coin toss. The narrowing takes the Balanced path to **5.7-7.7s and 13.4MB**, roughly **17-22x**, and puts it nowhere near the wall. **A third change was built on the bad number and then reverted the same day**: a `tech_marks` table holding nothing but the ~1,200 mark dates, justified by that 429.9s. Idle, the query it replaced is ~250ms against the table's ~100ms — 150ms inside a six-second operation, which does not buy a second copy of derivable state plus a write to keep in step. Production still holds the now-unused table; it is harmless and empty of consequence. The lesson is the measurement discipline, not the table.
  - **`readTechMarks` (a whole range, every column) is kept for offline work and nothing on a request path may call it.** It is the shape that cost the 181.8 seconds.

Two numbers it must print or it misleads: the **effective sample** (monthly starts at a 3-month horizon overlap ~3:1, so ~230 windows is ~75 independent observations — the research log's own discount rule) and the **symbol count per window**, since the pool shrinks going back.
