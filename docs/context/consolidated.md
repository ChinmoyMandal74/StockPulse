## Consolidated — every group in one table (2026-09-28, owner's request)
**`/consolidated`: one row per group, so the groups can be read against each other.** The owner's words: *"I do not have a screen to show how Mag 7 as a theme has done compared with Memory or cybersecurity."* Nothing did — a group page shows ONE group against the index, `/compare` is two **stocks**, and the pivot crosses two dimensions to answer *where is the weight* rather than *how has each one done*. Member page, guests refused, like the studio and the pivot beside it; a `Consolidated` button in the bar before Compare.

- **IT COSTS ONE `/api/stocks` READ AND NOT ONE ARCHIVE ROW**, which is what made it small enough to be worth building. An equal-weight group return over a window IS the mean of its members' own returns, and `oneWeekPct … fiveYearPct` are already stamped on every snapshot row — `basket.html` has been averaging them for a single group all along. No endpoint, no anchors, no cache: the pivot's economics, and cheaper than the pivot's own time axis.
- **BOTH WEIGHTINGS, AS A TOGGLE (the owner's call when asked).** Equal-weight is the default because it is the convention every group page already uses, so nothing on screen changes meaning; cap-weighted is what people usually mean by "how has Mag 7 done", since the real basket is dominated by its largest two. **One aggregator serves both**, so they cannot drift into two code paths.
- **Two exclusions, and they are different rules that must not be conflated.** A **missing return** is absent, never a zero — `Number(null)` is 0 and finite, and a fabricated flat stock drags a mean (the sixth place this project has needed that sentence). A stock with **no market cap** cannot carry a weight at all — a fund reports AUM, not capitalisation — so it leaves the cap-weighted average while still counting in Stocks. `w > 0` rejects null and zero in one test, and the note says how many went.
- **INDUSTRY IS A CHILD OF SECTOR, NEVER A FOURTH TOP-LEVEL CHOICE (owner, the same day: “industry should be a child drop down once I select Sector”).** Group by is Theme, Sector and Size; picking Sector reveals an **Industry** picker offering *Off — show the sectors*, *All industries*, and each sector present. The reasoning is the pivot’s own measured one — **0 of 99 industries span two sectors** — so a flat list is ~100 rows each of which is one sector’s slice, and the screener’s bar picker already nests for the same reason. `activeDim()` is the ONE place that answers which cut is on screen and over which stocks, so a heading, a link and a note cannot disagree; leaving Sector drops the drill rather than keeping it pointed at a cut that is gone.
- **ACROSS ALL INDUSTRIES THE NAME ALONE IS NOT ENOUGH (owner, from a screenshot: "it is hard to know which industry is in which sector").** A **Sector** column appears in that one mode, links to the sector page, and sorting by it groups the industries under their parent. **Deliberately absent in the other two modes**: drilled into one sector it would print the same word on every row, and grouping by Sector it would repeat the first column. It is **derived from the members, never assumed** — if an industry ever spans two sectors the cell reads `mixed (n)` rather than silently picking whichever member came first, which is also how the "0 of 99 span two" measurement would be caught going stale. A `mixed` cell is not a link, because it is a diagnosis rather than a place.
- **The blank bucket, the multi-value warning and the placement count are the pivot's rules, restated here** because they are properties of grouping rather than of that page: a stock with no sector is in NO sector rather than one called "null", and a stock in two themes is counted in both, so the rows are not meant to total the screen.
- **The benchmarks are their own kind of row, and are removed from the pool first** — left in, the S&P's own ETF sits inside Technology and inflates it. Being one stock each, equal and cap weighting give them the same number.
- **The note says how far apart the two weightings are across the whole screen**, so the toggle's stakes are stated rather than left to be discovered by flipping — and it is worded for the day they coincide, since "disagree by 0.0 points, which is the size of the choice you are making" reads as broken.
- Verified: **52 checks** on the drawn table plus **12** on the roles and **14** on the sub-cent floor. The fixture makes the two weightings disagree loudly — Mag 7 holds a $1T stock up 10% and a $10B stock up 100%, so equal-weight is +55.0% and cap-weighted +10.9% — and Memory separates the two exclusions from each other, +9.0% equal against −22.0% cap. **Proved by reverting** twice: a toggle wired to nothing fails exactly 3, and a drill that forgets to narrow to its sector fails 2 — the fixture gives Technology two industries and Healthcare a third, so a single-industry fixture could not have caught it.
  - **The roles suite caught a real defect**: the bar button reached guests. The neighbouring buttons are hidden **client-side in the guest branch** rather than by a `member-only` class, and the new one was missing its line. The check reads the **drawn box** now, not the HTML source — the markup is served to everyone, so a source check tests the wrong thing — and it asserts the member's button IS drawn, or deleting the feature outright would pass.
  - **A fixture fault worth keeping**: two groups tied at exactly +10.0%, so "clicking the heading reverses the order" was really testing which one the `Map` happened to build first. **No two groups may tie on the sorted column.**
