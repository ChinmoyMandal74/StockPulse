## Price as of — how old the stock page is (2026-10-02, owner's request)
**A quiet line under the price in `/stock`'s masthead: `Price as of Oct 2, 2:09 PM`, amber once the pull is over a day old.** Asked for after the owner reported ASML reading **$1857.6 / +2.7%** on the stock page against **$1865.5 / +3.2%** on the screener, with the verdict differing too (Strong Buy against Hold).

**NEITHER PAGE WAS WRONG AND NOTHING DISAGREED.** Measured before changing anything: `/api/stock?symbol=ASML` and the screener's `/api/stocks` returned **byte-identical values on every field** — price, all seven returns, the verdict. The two screenshots were one or two intraday slots apart. **The cause is an asymmetry rather than a bug**: a visible screener tab polls `/api/status` every five minutes and reloads when the snapshot moves, and **`/stock` has no poller at all**, so two tabs left open across a slot drift. The verdict moving with them is Advice behaving intraday, as designed.

- **THE SCREENER COULD ANSWER THE QUESTION AND THIS PAGE COULD NOT** — it has the Price pulled column and the status pill; `/stock` had nothing. That is the whole gap this closes. Every other data surface here already states its own freshness (the status pill, the Price pulled column, the studio's amber 26-hour line, the phone's "data as of"); `/stock` was the omission.

#### The route applied ONE of the four read-path stamps
**`/api/stock` called `stampCapDerived` and not `stampPricedAt` or `stampAdviceAge`**, so `pricedAt` and `adviceDays` were undefined on every stock page — confirmed on production across ASML, BRK.B and MU — and **Price pulled and Days held each drew a label with an em-dash**. `/compare` withheld both for the same reason.

- **The comment directly above the fix records this exact class of bug being found once already** (`capBand`, found by `/compare` putting an empty cell next to a full one) and it did not stop the next two. *The stamps are a SET; a read path applies all four or it has a blank row nobody notices.*
- **The irony worth keeping: the field that would have answered the owner's question in one glance was the blank one.**
- Applied **in parallel, each with its own catch** — the screener's rule: independent single-row reads, and a slow auxiliary read should cost one row rather than 500 the page.

#### The decisions
- **THE VALUE COMES FROM `FIELD_SPEC`'s OWN FORMATTER** (`RowCard.fieldValues(s)['short|Price pulled']`), never a second copy of the rule, so the masthead and the screener's column cannot drift on what counts as stale or how a time is written. **Proved by reverting**: a hand-rolled `toLocaleTimeString` loses the amber and fails 1.
- **THE DATE IS KEPT EVEN WHEN THE PULL WAS TODAY, deliberately differing from the screener's column.** That column drops it to save width in a 90-column table; a masthead has no such constraint, and this element exists precisely for the reader who left the tab open overnight — for whom a bare `2:09 PM` is the ambiguity the feature was built to remove.
- **ASKING IS NOT RECEIVING, and the tooltip carries it**: the pull time, the newest stored bar, and that the two are different facts. Ten stocks were once pulled on time and were not on the day's bar, AVB carrying a close from five weeks earlier — so a fresh clock must not be read as a fresh price. **Proved by reverting twice**: 1 each.
- **Never pulled is a different fact from pulled long ago** — `Price as of —` with a tooltip saying why, never a fabricated time. **Proved by reverting**: fails 3, and the page throws (`Cannot read properties of null`), which is what the guard is for.
- **Amber past a day**, the colour's existing meaning here (*notice this, it is old*), taking `.warn` for colour only — all `.warn` does in app.css. Scoped `.px .asof`: nine unscoped class names on this project have captured later markup.
- **THE DUPLICATE ROW STANDS ASIDE** — `short|Price pulled` joins `fund|Reported` in `buildSections({ skip })`. Reading the field's own formatter means the row below would print a **byte-identical** string a screen further down. Unlike Reported the two cannot disagree (they are one value), so this is purely duplication and the masthead states it better. **Proved by reverting**: fails 1, printing the clock twice.
- **It costs 20px and wraps nothing.** Measured as an A/B on the same page.
- Verified: **25 checks** over a fixture whose three stocks differ ONLY in the price clock — fresh, **23 hours and 25 hours** (a threshold is a step function, so it is probed either side of the field's own 24-hour line), and never pulled. **Proved by reverting nine times**: the two stamps 6, the price stamp alone 5, the readout 18, the bar tooltip 1, the asking-caveat 1, the amber 1, the never-pulled guard 3, the duplicate row 1, a second copy of the formatter 1.
  - **CLUSTERING CHILD TOPS IS THE WRONG WRAP TEST ON THIS MASTHEAD, and it failed first over a layout that was fine.** That heuristic comes from the screener's bar where every control is roughly one height; `.head` is **baseline-aligned** with a 46px `h1` beside a 26px chip, so their tops legitimately differ by ~16px **on one line** and any threshold near that flips when the line's height changes. The honest measure is how far each item MOVES between the two states: a wrap is a whole line, and the only thing that moved was `.outs` by **10px of the 20px growth** — exactly half, because those pills are `align-self: center` and a taller line re-centres them. Arithmetic, not a wrap.
  - **TWO REVERTS ABORTED RATHER THAN FAILED on the first run, one of them reporting "0 failed"** — indistinguishable from a guard that never mattered. A revert that breaks the render makes `waitForSelector` throw, and the A/B block touched `el.style` on an element that was no longer there. Both waits swallow their timeout and every read is defensive now.
  - **A relative `fetch` has no base URL on `about:blank`** — the API probe has to run after a navigation.
