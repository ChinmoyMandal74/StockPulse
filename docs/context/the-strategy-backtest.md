## The strategy backtest
`/strategy` is the first thing here that simulates a rule rather than measuring an association: signal, position, rebalance, equity curve. Reached from **Strategy** in the screener's bar. **The word "backtest" was freed for it** — what used to carry that name is now the forward-returns view.

**Why a separate page from the lab.** The lab is per-symbol and asks whether an indicator is associated with what happens next. A portfolio is not about a symbol, and its outputs — equity curve, Sharpe, drawdown, turnover, correlation to holding — fit none of the lab's panels. Its controls are different too: sizing, rebalancing, filters, costs.

- **The rule is Moskowitz–Ooi–Pedersen time-series momentum**: long what rose over the lookback, short what fell, each position sized `targetVol ÷ its own realised vol`, rebalanced monthly, the last month of the lookback skipped. Stock selection is a dropdown over **All plus every portfolio**, so a rule can be run on Mag 7 or on the Faded 33.
- **The day is earned before the rebalance, and that was a bug first.** Rebalancing first decided a weight from today's close and then earned today's move with it. The sizing was the worse half — `vol[i]` contains the very return being earned, so a rule facing a gap sized itself down for a move it could not have seen. Measured on a synthetic series with a 48% gap landing on a rebalance day: **the wrong order booked 5.98%, the right one books 39.98%.** Note the direction — here the lookahead *understated* the return, which is why "does it look too good" is not a sufficient test for one.
- **Monthly returns, turnover and average exposure are shipped, not finished statistics.** That is what makes cost, target volatility and the cash rate sliders rather than assumptions: cost is charged against real turnover, vol scaling is linear in the returns, and the cash credit follows exposure — so all three apply after the fact. At a monthly rebalance across a hundred names, cost is often the difference between a positive and a negative result. Monthly rather than daily is what keeps the payload manageable; it costs drawdown resolution, and the page says so.
- **One file per universe, plus an index (`strategy-index.json`).** The grid is 192 variants and every universe's copy of it is ~13 MB — a multi-megabyte download to read one entry in a dropdown. The page fetches the index, then the chosen universe, and caches each. The largest is ~950 KB raw, **~230 KB over the wire**, and only one is ever fetched. Size tracks the 192 variants x 225 months, not the number of symbols, so pruning the universe barely moves it.
- **The grid covers six dimensions**: lookback (21/63/126/252), direction, the 200-day filter, **leverage cap (1× / 3×)**, **rebalance (daily / weekly / monthly)** and **volatility window (20 / 60)**. The last three were added after a piece of published long-only research specified 1×, daily and 20 — three things the page could not express. `skip` (21) and the stored `targetVol` (10) stay fixed.
- **Idle cash earns, and that is a slider.** A long-only rule behind a 200-day filter sits in cash for much of its life; crediting it nothing understates it by whatever bills paid. The rate is credited on `1 − exposure` (capped at 1, so a levered book earns nothing on cash) and buy-and-hold gets none, being always fully invested. An **Invested** card reports average gross exposure, because that single number explains a modest CAGR better than anything else on the page.
- **The cash rate is also subtracted as the risk-free rate in Sharpe, and skipping that half was a bug.** Crediting 4% on a book sitting 87% in cash and *not* subtracting it reported **Sharpe 2.79** — the T-bill's ratio, borrowed. Sharpe is excess of cash by definition. Both the strategy and the benchmark take the same rate, or the comparison is rigged. The invariant that proves it: for a fully-uninvested book the Sharpe is **−0.09 with or without the credit**, since adding a constant and then measuring excess over it changes nothing. A `s > 1e-12` guard returns `null` rather than a ratio for a constant series, which otherwise printed **3.45 for a T-bill** out of floating-point residue.
- **"Match buy-and-hold" exists because a comparison at different exposures is meaningless.** The stored runs sit at ~29% gross exposure, so their raw CAGR looks terrible against a fully-invested benchmark while their drawdown looks wonderful. Neither number says anything until the volatilities match.
- **A gap is carried forward for at most `MAX_CARRY` (10) sessions; a longer hole, and a symbol not yet listed, stay null.** Without *any* carry a single missing print reads as a 100% loss and back again. Without the **bound**, GPS — which has a **1,373-session hole between 2019-11 and 2025-03** — was held flat at $16.78 for five and a half years: realised volatility of exactly zero, so `targetVol ÷ vol` produced a weight of **3,227,535×** and the leverage cap silently *became* the position; it then earned nothing for the whole stretch and booked the entire re-listing as one **+33% day**. A hole is absent data, not a quiet stock. 10 is chosen to clear a foreign listing's holiday calendar and nothing more — 005930 (Samsung) legitimately misses up to 6 consecutive US sessions over Chuseok, and is the only other symbol in the archive with an internal gap over one.
- **`MIN_VOL` (1% annualised) floors the divisor** as a second guard, so the failure is impossible rather than merely unlikely. After both fixes the largest raw weight anywhere is **7.83× on VRT**, a genuinely quiet stretch, which is what the cap is for.
- **The leverage cap barely binds in this universe, and the control says so.** At a 10% target these stocks are volatile enough that `target ÷ vol` lands under 1× on **99.2% of stock-days**, so 1× and 3× agree to within 0.1 points of CAGR. It would bite in a calmer universe, or at a higher target — but note that target volatility is applied afterwards as a multiplier, so **raising it does not re-apply the cap**. The cap belongs to the 10% the grid was run at, and the hint says that too.
- **The first result on this project that does not vanish before 2020.** At matched volatility, 5 bps, nothing earned on cash, **on the 116-symbol universe**: CAGR **+16.7% against hold's +18.6%**, Sharpe **0.97 against 0.95**, max drawdown **−28.1% against −43.4%** — and in **2008–2019 alone, Sharpe 0.95 against hold's 0.84**. Re-measured on the 93-symbol universe it reads **+18.8% against +21.8%**, Sharpe **1.09 against 1.03**, max drawdown **−22.1% against −44.1%**. It earns that by sidestepping 2008, which is visible in the curve and is mechanically what a trend rule does. It is a *risk* result, not a return result, and it remains hostage to a universe with no bankruptcies in it.
- **The published long-only formulation beats holding, once the SMA bug above is fixed.** Set as specified — 252-day lookback, mandatory 200-day filter, 1× cap, daily, 20-day vol — at matched volatility and 5 bps: CAGR **+14.8%** against hold's +18.6%, Sharpe **1.00 against 0.95**, max drawdown **−18.2% against −43.4%**. Credit 4% on the cash it holds and subtract the same 4% from both Sharpes and it is **0.80 against 0.75**, ahead in *both* halves (0.63 vs 0.61 pre-2020, 1.04 vs 0.96 after). **This is the corrected reading**: the first pass reported 0.66 against 0.75 and "does not beat holding", which was `smaSeries` excluding 49 symbols rather than a property of the rule.
  - **All of the above is on the 116-symbol universe. On the 93-symbol one the "both halves" claim no longer holds**: Sharpe 0.86 against hold's 0.84 over the whole period, but **0.66 against 0.69 pre-2020** — it loses the half that matters. Same code, same settings, 19 fewer laggards. Treat the pre-2020 half as the test and this as a reminder of how little separates the two answers. The `Set the published long-only rule` link on the page puts all five knobs there in one click.
- **Daily rebalancing is only optimal below about 3 bps.** At matched volatility the spec rule turns over **199% of capital a month** against 55% monthly. Measured crossovers: daily leads weekly to **2.77 bps**, weekly leads monthly to **15.26 bps**, monthly above that. Research quoting a daily-rebalanced vol-scaled rule at zero cost is quoting the most flattering version of the most expensive setting — which is why cost and cash yield are both on screen, since they pull opposite ways and omitting both is not neutral.
- **`smaSeries` returned all nulls for any series with a leading null, and that silently broke every `+200D` result.** A symbol that listed after the window's start has leading nulls; the function bailed at the first one and returned an empty series, which then failed the `sma[i] == null` guard on every session — so **49 of 116 symbols held no position at all in any 200-day-filter variant**, while still counting in the divisor. The filter was not being selective, it was disqualifying nearly half the universe. Fixed to restart the window on a hole rather than end the series; **113 of 116 now get a usable average** (the other three are too short). Every `+200D` number published before 2026-09-10 was wrong, including the first read on the long-only spec, and the correction *reversed* its conclusion.
