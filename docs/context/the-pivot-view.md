## The pivot view
**`/pivot` — the screen counted by two dimensions at once (2026-09-20, owner's request), a button in the screener bar beside Promo Studio.** Member page, guests refused like the studio: a preview of five stocks has no shape to show. Rows and columns are chosen from Sector, Industry, Theme, Today’s move, Size by market cap, Balanced advice, Advice move, Trend state, Entry state, Company type, Exchange and S&P 500, plus the two time axes (which are columns only); every cell is a count, and clicking one expands the stocks in it underneath the row.

- **No endpoint, no query, nothing on the rows-read meter.** Every dimension is already on a snapshot row, so the page reads `/api/stocks` once and cross-tabulates in the browser — measured 2.1MB and 11.6s cold, then every change of dimension is instant. Exploration means changing the axes constantly, which is exactly when a round trip per change is the wrong shape.
- **THE CARDINALITIES WERE MEASURED BEFORE IT WAS DESIGNED**, and they decided the design: sector 11, industry **87**, advice 6, trend 6, type 3, exchange 4, theme 26 — over 430 stocks. Sector × advice is 66 cells at ~6.5 stocks each and reads well; **industry × anything is 522 cells for 430 stocks and is mostly empty**, with the average industry holding five companies. So a dimension past `WIDE_CAP` (24) values **folds its tail into one "Other" bucket**, and the test proves the fold loses nobody — the totals still reconcile.
- **Reconciliation is the assertion that matters.** A pivot whose totals do not add up is worse than no pivot: it looks authoritative and is wrong. Every cell is summed against its row total, the row totals against the grand total, and the grand total against the stock count.
- **A THEME IS MULTI-VALUED, and that breaks the arithmetic everyone expects.** 33 stocks sit in more than one theme and **189 in none**, so with Theme on an axis the cells add up to more than the universe. The page says so in words — "the cells add up to 9, not 8 — 1 stock is counted in more than one bucket" — rather than printing a total that quietly disagrees with itself. `multi` is a property of the dimension, not a special case buried in the sum.
- **A blank is a BUCKET, never a dropped row.** 20 stocks have no sector yet, because profiles arrive on rotation; omitting them would make every total wrong while looking tidy. Shown as `—`, sorted last, and counted in the note.
- *(superseded 2026-09-21 — see **Total market cap as a measure** below; the rule it states still holds for every other money field.)* **No money measures, deliberately.** Summing revenue would add SEK to USD: `currency` reads USD for all 430 because it is the TRADING currency, while revenue and net income are in the company's **reporting** currency, which is not stored. Counts cannot lie that way. Market cap would be safe and is left for when it is missed.
- **A ladder is not the alphabet.** Advice, trend, entry and type order by their own ladders; everything else orders by size, because "where is the weight" is what a pivot is opened to answer.
- **A FOOTNOTE UNDER THE GRID SAYS THE CELLS ARE CLICKABLE** (2026-09-27, owner's request). The drill is the page's main control and the only things announcing it were a tooltip — which speaks after the pointer has already arrived — and one clause of the intro paragraph, a screen away by the time anyone is reading numbers. `#ghint`, written by `render()`.
  - **The instruction LEFT the intro in the same commit.** Stating it twice inside one screen is the duplication this project has been pulled up on before; the paragraph says what the numbers ARE, the footnote says what to do with them, where the doing happens.
  - **Built from what was DRAWN, not from what the measure ought to produce.** A time axis has row averages and no column totals, and an empty grid has neither — so the "a total opens the same way" half is appended only when a `td.drill` exists, and the whole line is hidden when no `td.cell` does. Promising a control that is not there is worse than silence.
  - **IT IS HIDDEN WHILE A SELECTION IS OPEN, and that is load-bearing rather than tidiness.** `sizePanes()` budgets the two panes from the grid's TOP and the child caption, so **anything unaccounted for between them comes straight off the bottom of the window** — and with both panes capped the budget lands the child table exactly on the foot of the viewport, so there is no slack to absorb it. Measured: **−1px of slack hidden, −27px visible**, the footnote's own 26px box. It is also spent by then: the child table carries its own crumb and a Clear selection button.
  - **`ghint`, never `hint`.** An unscoped class here would be the sixth on this project to capture later markup, and `.sub` already did it on this very page.
  - Verified: **20 checks** — drawn, measured as sitting between the two tables, the wording, the totals clause against a grid that really has drillable totals, the intro no longer saying it, hidden on a pick and back on Clear, and the phone. **The pane check needs BOTH panes at their caps or it proves nothing**: a first fixture of nine stocks left 222px of slack and passed with the footnote deliberately left showing, so it is twenty sector rows and sixty stocks, and the assertion first checks `scrollHeight > clientHeight` on each pane. **Proved by reverting**: dropping the one hide clause fails 2, the child table hanging 27px off the bottom of the window.
- **THE SNAPSHOT'S `action` IS IGNORED ON READ** — `scoreActionInto` re-derives the verdict, company type, trend and entry from each row's technicals every time. A fixture that writes `action: 'Strong Buy'` therefore tests nothing: the first version of this test did exactly that, got one verdict for all eight stocks, and its ladder assertion passed on a **single column**. The fixture carries real technicals now, checked against `Action.apply` beforehand to confirm they produce five different verdicts.
- Verified: 30 checks on the real page — reconciliation in both the single- and multi-valued cases, the blank bucket, the ladder order, drill-down (expanding, the count matching the list, each row linking to its stock page, closing again), swap, the same-dimension-twice guard, the wide fold keeping every stock, and no sideways scroll at 390px.

### Total market cap as a measure (2026-09-21, owner's request)
**A `Measure` picker beside the two dimensions: Number of stocks (the default) or Total market cap.** This file had left the door open — *"Market cap would be safe and is left for when it is missed"* — and it is safe for the reason recorded under the Size column: **the cap comes back in USD even when the rest of the row does not**, verified across 18 foreign reporters. **It is still the ONLY money measure this page may have**: revenue and net income are in the company's reporting currency, which is not stored, so summing them would add SEK to USD and produce a confident, meaningless number.

- **A measure is one number per stock** (`MEASURES[k].each`), and the ordering, the wide fold, the row and column totals, the child rows and the drill all reduce over it — so adding one is an entry in that map rather than a parallel path through `build()`.
- **The totals are now REDUCED OUT OF THE CELLS** rather than accumulated beside them. They were a correct parallel running count that could have drifted from what the grid draws; derived this way **a row total cannot disagree with the row above it, whatever the measure**. The grid is walked as rows × columns rather than by splitting cell keys on `|`, because sector and industry names are the provider's, not ours.
- **THE EMPTINESS TEST IS THE STOCK LIST, NOT THE VALUE.** A cell holding two funds sums to zero and is still a cell with two stocks in it: it reads `$0`, stays clickable, and the note says **how many stocks have no cap and that "a count and a total need not agree"**. Drawing it as the empty dot would hide them, and a sum that silently omits rows is the quietest kind of lie.
- **Ordering follows the MEASURE, deliberately** — showing money while ordering by count would put the biggest number halfway down the table. The test pins it: two sectors tied at 3 stocks fall back to the alphabet under counts and reorder by size under money.
- **The double-counting warning is judged on PLACEMENTS, never on the measure.** With money on screen `placements` is dollars, and "adds up to $4.1T, not 573" would be nonsense — so `placeCount` is kept separately. The count wording is kept **word for word** ("the cells add up to 9, not 8"), because a rephrasing lost the directness; money gets its own sentence saying a cap is added once per placement.
- **Changing the measure keeps both axes and the open sector** — it is the same table answering a different question, and dropping the reader's place would make comparing the two answers a chore. Changing an *axis* still clears them, since their keys name values that are leaving the screen.
- **The drill names the total** (`2 stocks, $125.0B in total`), or the number in the cell above cannot be checked against the list under it. The child-row reconciliation is compared with a **relative epsilon**: summing dollars leaves a floating-point residue that a strict equality would report as a mismatch over two numbers identical to the penny.
- Verified: 26 checks — every row's cells summing to its own total and the rows to the grand, each column footer against its own column, hand-computed totals ($175B / $8B / $183B), the `$0` cell staying clickable with its explanation, the drill's total, the hierarchy reconciling **in dollars**, the reorder-by-money, the expansion surviving a measure change, and counts coming back unchanged. The two earlier pivot suites still pass.

### Balanced advice moves, as a measure AND a dimension (2026-09-23, owner's request)
**"Analyse Balanced advice moves from the previous trading day to today… so I can slice by Sector, Theme."** Both halves shipped, because they answer different questions and the pivot expresses them differently:

- **`moves` — a MEASURE, "Advice moves"**: how many stocks in each cell changed verdict since the previous close. Deliberately UNSIGNED. The page orders, shades and totals on the measure, and a signed one would shade only its positive half and draw a −20 cell the same as an empty one.
- **`advmove` — a DIMENSION, "Advice move"**: Upgraded / Unchanged / Downgraded. Direction belongs on an AXIS: "Technology 27" says where the churn is, crossing it says what happened. Cross the two and the grid says both at once.

Both read fields already stamped on every snapshot row — `action` and `advicePrev` — so this costs a comparison and no fetch. Measured on the live screen the day it shipped: **136 of 942 moved, 63 up and 73 down**.

- **`ActionRules.ACTIONS` RUNS WORST-FIRST** (Sell Immediately → Strong Buy), so an upgrade is an INCREASE in index. The page reverses that list for display, and using the display order here would label every upgrade a downgrade. The test's two sectors have the same number of movers in opposite directions precisely so that inversion cannot pass.
- **A stock with no previous verdict is a BLANK, not "Unchanged".** New to the screener is a different fact from held its verdict, and the measure counts it as neither — the two-kinds-of-blank rule this project keeps re-learning.
- **The note carries the up/down split**, because it is not derivable from the grid unless the dimension is on an axis, and it names the dimension that would show it per cell.
- **The drill gained a "Was" column**, filled only where the verdict moved: a column repeating today's verdict on 800 unchanged rows is noise, and the blank then means "held". **REMOVED 2026-09-28 (owner), with Trend and Cushion, replaced by the 3M and 6M returns** — see the drill's own note below. `advicePrev` is still on the row and the Advice move measure still reads it; only the column went.
- **THE DRILL'S COLUMNS ARE FOURTEEN, AND WHICH FOURTEEN CHANGED ON 2026-09-28 (owner).** Out went **Was, Trend and Cushion**; in came **3M and 6M**, placed between 1M and 1Y so the row reads left to right as time passing. All three that left were readings of the advice engine, and the Advice column beside them already gives the verdict — a drill opened from a cell is read for *which stocks are in here and how have they done*, so the two missing horizons earn the width more than a second and third opinion on the same row. **Two suites had to be corrected rather than the change worked around**: `pivot-test.js` asserted “the FIFTEEN fields” and named Cushion, and `pivot-advmove-test.js` asserted the Was column was present and filled. Both now assert the new set **in both directions** — the horizons present AND the three readings absent — so neither half can quietly revert.

**THE NOTE'S WORDING WAS HARDCODED AROUND MARKET CAP and had to move onto the measures first.** `const money = measure !== 'count'` meant any new measure inherited the sentence about funds reporting AUM. Each measure now owns `everyStock`, `missingNote`, `placeNote` and an optional `summary`; only `count` sets `everyStock`, because it is the one whose total IS the stock count.

**`tint: 1` IS A DEAD FLAG UNTIL THE VALUES ARE IN `LADDER_TINT` — and this project has now shipped that wrong twice.** The first time was the move bands. This time a screenshot showed Unchanged carrying a dot while Upgraded and Downgraded did not, and only because the band ladder already defines that same word and means the same by it. There is a check now that reads the rendered dot colours and asserts the two ends differ.

- Verified: 20 checks — the measure's per-row counts and a grand total of the movers rather than the universe, a 0 cell still clickable, the note's split and its absence of fund wording, the dimension's three columns reconciling, the blank bucket, the drill's Was column opened on an UPGRADED cell (opening whichever cell held a number landed on Unchanged, where the column is correctly blank and the assertion tested that a blank is blank), and a saved preset carrying both new keys.
- **The fixture had to supply real technicals AND a `prevTech` overlay.** The server re-derives both verdicts on read, so hand-written `action` / `advicePrev` values are discarded — the first cut did that and every stock came back with no previous verdict at all. The documented trap, met again.

### Time as a column axis — the last 12 months, or the last 12 years (2026-09-27, owner's request)
**"One column per month… it will help to see how stocks perform over time."** `Last 12 months` and `Last 12 years` are column dimensions; the cell is the group's **equal-weight return** for that period, newest first. Rows stay any dimension, so it answers *how did each sector do each month* in one grid.

- **THE REQUEST SAID BOTH** — "Last 12 Month" and "one column per last 12 years" — and the answer was to build both, because **they are the same query**: thirteen boundaries and the last close before each. The owner confirmed.
- **THIRTEEN ANCHORS, NOT A BAR WINDOW.** Twelve years of daily bars for the universe is millions of rows and past the ~83MB wall this file records. `closesBefore` is one indexed seek per (symbol, boundary) on the `(symbol, d)` primary key — ~15,000 single-row reads, the shape it was rewritten into when the 5Y anchor went from 748,859 rows to 254. `GET /api/pivot-periods?kind=`, cached ten minutes per kind.
- **A RETURN IS A MEAN AND DEPENDS ON THE COLUMN**, where every other measure is a per-stock number summed. `cellVal(list, c)` is the one place that aggregates, so the grid, the row totals, the child rows and the drill cannot do it three different ways. **A mean cannot be summed out of the cells**, so the row total is the *average of that row's own periods* ("Avg", not "Total") — still reduced from the cells the grid draws, so it cannot disagree with the row above it.
- **THE FOOTER IS THE S&P 500, not a column total.** Averaging group means down a column would weight a two-stock sector like a two-hundred-stock one. It is deliberately **not drillable**: one ETF's own return has no list of stocks behind it.
- **COLOURED BY SIGN, never shaded by size.** The heat ramp says *bigger*, and a −8% month is not smaller than a +2% one; the shading control disables itself for a signed measure.
- **A STOCK ABSENT FROM A PERIOD IS ABSENT, NEVER A FLAT ZERO.** No opening anchor, no return — inventing one from a stock's first trade would print a return for a period it did not live through. So an earlier column is an average over fewer companies, and **the note says how many have all twelve**: the survivorship point, in the one feature most able to hide it.
- **ALL TWELVE PERIODS ARE DRAWN even when nothing has data for one** — the Size picker's rule. "2015: nothing reaches back that far" is an answer; silently drawing four columns after asking for twelve looks broken. Found by the test, not by reading.
- **The newest period is PARTIAL** (month- or year-to-date) and the header says *so far*, because that is what "the last twelve months" means to a reader on the 20th.
- **A time axis is NOT double-counting** (`noDouble`): a stock sits in every period it has a return for, which is the same stock measured twelve times. The "counted in more than one bucket" warning would be true arithmetic and a false alarm.
- **Columns only, and the measure is forced.** Twelve periods down the side with a taxonomy across the top is the same grid read the wrong way round; and a count of stocks per month is the same number twelve times, a market cap per month is today's cap printed twelve times — so the picker locks to the return and shows what it is locked to rather than vanishing.

- **`junk` is returned by the endpoint**, so a rising count is visible rather than silent.

### Include S&P — a filter of its own, not a dimension (2026-10-04, owner's request)
**`Include S&P`: All stocks / In the S&P 500 / Not in the S&P 500, always on screen, between Shading and Filter.** The owner's words: *"I don't want s&p filter on Rows and Column filter like this, I want to create a separate filter called Include s&p."*

- **IT WAS ONE `DIMS` ENTRY, WHICH IS EXACTLY THE PROBLEM.** The Filter picker is built from DIMS rather than from a list of its own, so a single entry bought a row axis, a column axis and a page filter at once — which read as economical when it shipped and is the reason it turned up in two places nobody wanted it. **On an axis it is a two-row grid whose interesting half is always the other dimension.** The entry is deleted, so it is gone from Rows, Columns *and* the generic Filter together.
- **DELIBERATELY NOT LEFT IN THE GENERIC FILTER AS WELL.** Two controls for one cut can be set to different things, and nothing on the page would say which won. There is a check that no axis menu so much as says the words.
- **ANDed with the generic Filter, applied in `pageRows()`** — the one place everything downstream starts, so the cells, both sets of totals, the shading denominators, the drill-downs and the note all describe the same population without any of them knowing the filter exists. **Proved by reverting**: making the cut *replace* the other filter fails 1, the intersection coming out at 3 where it should be 2.
- **UNKNOWN IS NOT OUTSIDE, and that is the whole reason the read did not change.** `spMember` is null until a holdings file has been imported, so the value still comes from **`Filters.filterValue(s, 'spMember')`** — the one function that knows the field is three-state. `out` means **known to be outside**, not "not known to be inside", so an unchecked stock is in **neither** cut and the note says how many and why; otherwise the two cuts quietly fail to add up to the screen. **Proved by reverting twice**: `spOf(s) !== 'Yes'` fails 2, and a `s.spMember ? 'Yes' : 'No'` ternary fails 5 — on an instance that has never run the import that one would put all 1,278 stocks in the `out` cut.
- **Counted over the WHOLE universe in the menu**, like the Filter's own values and for the same reason: a menu describing the table you have already narrowed to cannot tell you what you would get back.
- **`cleanPivots` REBUILDS A PRESET FROM AN EXPLICIT FIELD LIST**, so `sp` had to be named there or every saved pivot would open on the default cut with nothing saying why — a key it does not know is dropped. A preset saved before this reads as `all`, in `samePreset` as well, or an old one would read "(edited)" the moment it was opened. **Proved by reverting**: the server drop fails 4, `samePreset` 2, `applyPreset` 3.
- **Checked production before deleting the dimension**: neither of the two saved pivots used it, so nothing was stranded. Live afterwards: **498 in, 780 out, 1,278 all**, both presets still listed, no page errors.
- **The values are spelled as `Cards.spCuts()` spells them (`all` / `in` / `out`) and are NOT shared with it.** That catalogue lives in `cards.js`, which this page does not load; a third home for three labels would be worse than two, and matching the spelling means a later unification is a deletion rather than a translation. **The part that genuinely must not be duplicated — the three-state read — already is shared.**
- Verified: **39 checks**. The fixture's load-bearing property is that **the cut CROSSES the sector** — each sector holds members and non-members — so a cut wired to the wrong field comes out at a different grid rather than coincidentally the same one, and every state sums the grid back up. **Proved by reverting eleven times**, every one load-bearing: the cut unapplied 9, replacing instead of ANDing 1, unknown-as-outside 2, the raw-field ternary 5, the dimension restored 4, the note 5, the menu counts 2, `currentPreset` 2, `samePreset` 2, `applyPreset` 3, the server key 6.

One check comparing the route's own stamp against the intended members said so in one line.

It now saves through the **Save this pivot** button (handling the `prompt`), and the revert then fails 2. *A round-trip written against the API is not a test of the control that writes it.*

Worth stating because the run also surfaced a real copy bug while they were being fixed: the note read *"1 stock has not been checked … and **are** in neither cut"*, and there is now a check that the sentence agrees with its own count.

### Saved pivots (2026-09-23, owner's request: "something similar" to the promo presets)
**The promo-preset model applied to /pivot, and the owner chose that scope explicitly when asked**: ONE site-wide list in `app_meta` under `pivot_presets`, every member reads it, only the owner writes. `GET /api/pivot-presets` is `requireMember` (so guests are refused, matching the page, which redirects them); `PUT` is `requireAdmin`. `cleanPivots()` rebuilds from scratch, `PIVOTS_MAX` 20, cached 60s per instance.

- **A preset is the QUESTION, never the answer** — both axes, the measure, the shading and the page filter. Opening one tomorrow cross-tabulates tomorrow's screen, which is the whole point and the same reason a saved promo post stores controls rather than a picture.
- **The open sector and the drill selection are deliberately NOT stored.** They are where you happened to be looking, not what you wanted to keep, and restoring yesterday's stock list into today's counts is the one thing a saved pivot must not do.
- **Keys are SHAPE-checked, not listed against DIMS/MEASURES.** Those live in pivot.html, and restating them server-side would drift the first time a dimension was added — the rule `cleanViews` follows for the screener's columns and `cleanPosts` for the studio's controls. `applyPreset()` falls back rather than throwing, so a preset naming a dimension that has since been renamed opens to a working grid.
- **`pageVal` takes any printable text, deliberately.** It is a sector, a theme or a band name that came out of the data, not an identifier. Narrowing it is exactly how every screen silently lost its `<` and `>` filters on 2026-09-15; the page escapes it where it is shown.
- **A saved filter value that no longer exists says so.** fillPicks already falls back to the first value rather than filtering to nothing; the preset path adds a line naming what it could not find, because silently showing a different cut is worse than admitting the saved one is gone.

**THE ONE DESIGN BUG, caught by the test rather than by reading it.** The first cut derived the picker's selection from whether the screen still MATCHED a preset, so the moment an axis moved the picker deselected and **Replace vanished — at exactly the moment it becomes useful**, since you change the pivot and then want to point the preset at it. `chosenId` is kept apart from `samePreset()` now: the selection survives divergence and the option reads "(edited)", the idiom the screener's Screens menu already uses. An exact match still adopts the selection, so arriving at a saved pivot by hand names it rather than reading "Not saved".

- Verified: 26 checks against the real page and the real server. **The round trip moves EVERY control away before reopening** — the promo suite passed for weeks without doing that, reading values that had never left the page while 23 controls were being silently dropped. Plus the grand total matching what it was when saved, the stored shape carrying no drill state, Replace keeping the name, validation (no axes, an unsafe key, duplicate ids, punctuation in a value), and the member seeing the list with no write buttons.
- `.psaid` and the whole row are scoped under `#presetBar`. Five unscoped class names have captured later markup on this project, one of them (`.sub`) on this very page.

### Sector expands into its industries (2026-09-21, owner's request)
**Industry is a DRILL of Sector, not a peer dimension, and the data says so:** measured on the live screen, **0 of 99 industries span two sectors** — every one belongs to exactly one. So as a row dimension it drew 99 rows of which each was a single sector's slice, and crossed with Sector it was a block-diagonal grid with one non-empty cell per column. `DIMS.industry` carries **`childOf: 'sector'`** now, `ROW_DIMS` filters those out, and a sector row carries a caret that opens its industries underneath it.

- **It stays a COLUMN dimension.** Advice × Industry is still a real question; only the Rows entry went. **The caret is switched off when the columns are already Industry** — the child rows would restate the grid — and the page says that rather than leaving a dead control.
- **One sector open at a time (the owner's call).** Two hierarchies on screen and the eye stops knowing which child rows belong to which parent.
- **A caret is drawn only where a sector holds TWO or more industries.** One child row is a copy of its parent, which is a control that does nothing — and it is exactly the blank-sector bucket's shape, so the rule handles that case without naming it.
- **THE RECONCILIATION IS THE FEATURE.** The children are counted through the *same* `cFold.map` and de-duplicated multi-value list as the grid above, so they add up to their parent's own total — and the note **says so on the page**, printing the sum. The hard case is a multi-valued column: a stock in two themes inflates its sector's total, and the industry rows have to be counted the same way or they disagree with the row directly above them. A hierarchy whose children do not sum to their parent is a pivot that lies while looking authoritative.
- **No fold on the child rows**, measured: the widest sector holds **16** industries against a `WIDE_CAP` of 24, so a child list is always a list rather than a wall.
- **Child cells drill to stocks like their parents do**, keyed `sub:<industry>|<column>` — the prefix is what keeps the two sets of keys apart, so a sector and an industry that happened to share a name could never share a drill. Expanding or collapsing drops every child drill, or a key left in `open` silently re-opens a stock list the next time any sector is expanded.
- **The same-dimension "step aside" now avoids child dimensions.** It took the first key in `DIMS` — which is `industry` — so setting rows to whatever the columns already were silently landed on Industry-as-columns and the caret vanished from a state the reader chose nothing to get into. Found by the test walking into it.
- **`.sub` was already taken on this page** — the intro PARAGRAPH eleven rules up the stylesheet (font-size 13px, max-width 78ch, its own colour) — and `<tr class="sub">` inherited all of it. **The FIFTH unscoped class on this project to capture later markup**, after `.warn`, `.card`, `input[type="text"]` and `.mk`. It is `tr.kid` now. **A screenshot found it; all 38 assertions passed straight through it, because the numbers were right.** There is now a check comparing a child row's *computed* type size and column width against its parent's.
- **The caret was `--faint` at 10px and read as a bullet**, not a control — it is `--muted` at 12px, and the test asserts the rendered box rather than the markup (the `/quality` lesson).
