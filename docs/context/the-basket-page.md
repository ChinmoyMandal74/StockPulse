## The basket page
**`/portfolio/<name>` — one portfolio aggregated, for shared portfolios and a member's own lists (`my:<Name>` in the URL), reached from a chart-arrow beside every row in the portfolio picker (2026-09-14).** A screener view, deliberately not a tracker: no positions exist anywhere in this product, and the owner ruled out P&L tracking explicitly. First cut is three cards; more modules (composition, movers, earnings, news) are the planned second pass.

- **The curve** (`GET /api/basket?name=&days=`, requireMember): equal dollars at the window start, held — `mean(close/start)` per session over the union-of-dates axis, symbols with no history at the window start sit out and are counted in the caption. **The benchmark is the whole universe on the same convention**, not SPY: the benchmark is deliberately never archived, and "did my list beat my own screener" is the better question anyway. One `readBarsFor(all, since)` covers both curves (basket ⊆ universe); measured 1.27s at 1Y × 93 symbols, cached per range client-side. Ranges 1M/3M/6M/1Y; the chart is a hand-rolled dual-line SVG (no `preserveAspectRatio="none"`, so SVG text labels are safe here).
- **Stance**: trend-state stacked bar + counts, % above the 200D, Balanced advice rollup, and today's advice changes within the list.
- **Returns next to the whole screen**: mean of each stock's own horizon return (1W→1Y), list vs all vs difference — an equal-weight reading, the caption says so. Verified live: Mag 7 +11.0% 1Y against the universe's +60.4%, both curves anchored at exactly 1.0.
- Personal lists resolve against the CALLER's own `user_portfolios` via `prefsKey`, so one member can never read another's; guests are refused by requireMember and the page redirects them.

### Sector, Industry and Size are groups too — one page, four scopes (2026-09-27, owner's request)
**"I don't think I have anything that summarizes data and charts for a sector, industry, theme or size."** A theme already had one; the other three had nothing (`/pivot` cross-tabs them as counts and total cap, which answers *where is the weight*, not *how is Semiconductors doing*). **`/sector/<name>`, `/industry/<name>` and `/size/<band>` are the SAME page as `/theme/<name>`**, because a group differs from a theme only in which stocks are in it.

- **The owner was offered a separate page and chose one.** The alternative was a second page for the three new dimensions, which is two copies of every card added afterwards — the drift `rowcard.js`, `action.js` and `cards.js` all exist to prevent. **The whole server change is ~18 lines of resolution**: `basketPayload` turns a name into a symbol list and everything downstream (the date axis, the equal-weight curve, the universe benchmark, the per-symbol series) was already scope-agnostic.
- **The wire name carries the dimension as a prefix** (`sector:Technology`), the way `my:` already did, and the payload reports `scope` so the page can word itself. `/theme/<name>` and `/portfolio/<name>` are untouched.
- **`groupIndex()` IS CACHED (60s), and that is not decoration.** Resolving a sector needs the snapshot, the page refetches per range click, and `readSnapshot()` is the ~1.3MB read that took `/quality` from 2.4s to 10.8s. One read a minute per instance instead of one per click; the `tile_config` pattern. Being a minute stale means a stock that changed sector overnight, which does not happen between two clicks.
- **`capBand` is STAMPED on the way out and never stored**, so the index derives it with `stampCapDerived` rather than reading it — otherwise `/size/<band>` is empty until something else happens to have stamped that snapshot.
- **A stock with no sector is in NO sector group**, rather than one called "null". The blank-bucket rule, and there is a check that it is still in `All`.
- **The 404 names the dimension** — "No such sector" is the whole diagnosis when a link carries a value the taxonomy has since renamed.

**Four cards were added, and they serve all four scopes at once** (the owner took every one when asked):

- **What it is made of.** The bar is **market cap**, the number beside it is **companies**, and *where those two disagree is the finding* — "Technology +18%" means something different if two names are 60% of it. What it breaks down BY follows the scope: a sector into industries, an industry into size bands (its sector is constant by construction), everything else into sectors. Each row opens that group.
- **Leaders and laggards**, over the window the range buttons chose — so the card and the curve above it cannot tell different stories. The verdict rides along, labelled as the Balanced rules' reading.
- **Fundamentals against the whole screen.** **MEDIANS, never means**: one 300x multiple drags a mean somewhere no company in the group sits. A **forward P/E at or below zero is excluded** (a multiple off a loss is arithmetic, not cheapness — the Size card's rule, the Bubble card's, and the peer table's), and **financials are dropped from gross margin only**, counted and said rather than silently. The gap is in the figure's own units — turns of a multiple, points of a percentage — never a percentage OF a ratio.
- **The stocks themselves**, 50 at a time, with the return column following the range — biggest first until 2026-10-02, **sortable on every column** since (below).

#### The comparison is the INDEX now, not your own universe (2026-09-27, owner: "remove All screened… you can add S&P 500 and Dow 30")
**The blue "All screened, same convention" line is gone from the chart and from the returns table; the S&P 500 (SPY) and the Dow 30 (DIA) are drawn in its place.** An equal-weight line over everything screened answered *did this group beat the rest of my list* — which **changes meaning every time a ticker is added** and means nothing to anyone the page is shown to. An index is fixed, public, and something a reader already has a feel for.

- **One symbol through the SAME `equalWeightIndex`** the basket uses, so both are rebased at the same point on the same date axis. A second code path is how the picture and the numbers would come to disagree — there is a check that every benchmark line starts at exactly 1.
- **`PAGE_BENCHMARKS` carries the labels in server.js**, so basket.html restates nothing. `stock.html` keeps its own list of four: those are toggles with their own colours, a different control.
- **The lines are accent and violet, never green and red** — those two already mean up and down on every chart here, and an index coloured by its own direction would make a different claim from the one the chart is for.
- **`notBenchmark` went with the line it existed to serve.** Nothing computes "your universe, equal weight" any more, and a predicate nothing calls is residue that reads as intent. **`BENCHMARKS` stays** — it is now the delete guard.
- **THE FUNDAMENTALS CARD DELIBERATELY STILL SAYS "ALL SCREENED", and says why on its own face**: the S&P 500 and the Dow are *ETFs* here, and a fund has no margin, no growth and no P/E to compare against. There was nothing to put in the column; removing it would have gutted the card rather than improved it.
- **THE FOUR BENCHMARKS CANNOT BE REMOVED FROM THE SCREENER** (the owner's instruction). Every group page draws their line, so dropping one would take the comparison off pages that have nothing to do with that row — **damage that surfaces somewhere else entirely**, which is exactly the kind a guard is for. `DELETE /api/tickers/:symbol` answers **409** naming the reason; the screener's × is `disabled` with the same sentence on it, because a control that looks live and then errors is worse than one that explains itself. Removing a benchmark from a *theme* is untouched — that purges nothing.
  - The page's `BENCH_LOCK` mirrors `BENCHMARKS`; **the refusal lives in the route**, and a stale copy in the page can at worst offer a click the server then declines in its own words.
- Their bars ARE archived — checked against production, all four hold the full window. This file's older note that "the benchmark is deliberately never archived" predates the Benchmarks theme and is stale.
- Verified: **+16 checks** — the `universe` field gone, two labelled lines in its place, each a real series the length of the axis, each rebased to 1, and **SPY's line differing from the group's** (a benchmark that silently averaged the group would pass everything else). Plus the legend, three drawn lines told apart by stroke width, the returns table's exact four rows with no screened row, the fundamentals card keeping its column AND carrying the reason, all four DELETEs refused at 409 with the symbol still in the universe, **an ordinary ticker still deleting** (or the guard has locked the route), and the disabled × beside a live one on the screener. **Proved by reverting**: dropping the delete guard fails 8.

- **Entry points**: the Sector, Industry and Size pickers in the screener's bar each gained **the chart-arrow the theme picker already carried**. Order is coarse to fine, then membership: sector, industry, size, themes.
  - **`text-decoration: none` is not optional on an `a.chip`** — nothing on that page resets it, and the peer table's `vs` pill arrived underlined for exactly that reason. The chip keeps its muted colour at rest (a row of tinted pills would shout); the pointer and a lift on hover are the whole affordance.
  - **A stock with no industry or no cap draws FEWER chips**, never `/industry/undefined` — the field arrives one profile at a time, so that is the common case rather than an edge one.

#### How much they move together — is a group one bet or many? (2026-10-04, owner's request)
**A card on every group page: the average pairwise correlation of its members' daily returns, how many independent holdings would damp the swings as much, the member least like the rest, the closest and furthest pairs, and a matrix where the group is small enough to read one.** Asked for off a list of fifteen quant concepts — six of which are already in this app under other names, four of which are impossible here for want of an options chain or an order book. The other two that were worth building are [docs/backlog.md](docs/backlog.md) entries 23 and 24.

- **IT COSTS NO QUERY AND NO BYTES.** `basketPayload` already returns `series` — every member rebased on one shared date axis — for the chart, so this is a loop over data the page is holding. **Memoised on the window and the membership**, because `paint()` runs on every sort click and every Show-more, and at the 150-member cap that is 11,175 pairs.
- **RETURNS, NEVER LEVELS, and that is not a refinement — it is the difference between right and meaningless.** Measured over 72 real pairs BEFORE building: the rebased-levels figure lands between **−0.96 and +0.90** away from the returns figure and **flips sign** — AAPL/MSFT is **+0.12 on returns and −0.27 on levels**. Two shares that both drift upward look alike on their price lines whatever they do day to day. **Proved by reverting**: the fixture's average goes 0.52 → **1.00** and 12 checks fail.
- **THE 3dp ROUNDING IN `symbolSeries` IS IMMATERIAL — 0.0039 at worst over those same pairs**, and that is what makes computing in the browser off the rounded payload the right call rather than a compromise. The same rounding turns a very small move into an exactly flat one, which is also what a carried-forward price gives; neither is distinguishable from the other here, the date axis already drops the sessions where that would be common, and both are inside the 0.0039.
- **THE WINDOW MOVES THE ANSWER MORE THAN ANYTHING ELSE** — AAPL/MSFT is **0.12 over 120 sessions and 0.44 over 1,000** — so the card follows the page's own range buttons and prints the session count it used. There is a check that the count really changes with the buttons.
- **A PAIR IS COMPLETED PAIRWISE**, so a ticker added last week is compared over the sessions it actually has rather than dropping the pair whole. Under `CORR_MIN_PAIR` (20) shared returns the pair is dropped and counted as thin. **Proved by reverting**: requiring both series complete fails 29.
- **A MEMBER NOBODY COULD BE COMPARED WITH IS OUT OF THE DENOMINATOR.** Leaving it in the N reports a basket as better spread than its pairs say, which is **the direction that flatters**. **Proved by reverting**: fails 2.
- **Effective N is `N / (1 + (N−1)·ρ̄)`** — exact for an equal-weight basket of equal-volatility members, and **both assumptions are stated on the card** rather than buried: this page's basket really is equal-weight, the volatilities really are not. It falls back to the plain count where ρ̄ ≤ 0, because the formula then returns something larger than N; **proved by reverting**, which prints *9.0 of 3*.
- **THE SIGN COMES FROM THE ROUNDED VALUE.** An exactly orthogonal pair computes to −1e-17 and printed **"−0.00"** while being marked as a diversifying cell — a minus sign in front of a zero, the `+0%` lesson from the earnings card in a second place. Caught by the suite's own first run.
- **ONE HUE, AND THE NUMBER IN EVERY CELL.** Correlation has no good end, so green and red — which mean up and down on every other surface here — would read a verdict into it. The shade is an aid and the figure is the data; **a negative cell is unfilled and outlined**, because unfilled at +0.01 and unfilled at −0.50 must not look identical.
- Capped at `CORR_MATRIX_MAX` (14) for the grid and `CORR_MEMBERS_MAX` (150, biggest first) for the computation, both said on the card when they bite.
- Verified: **42 checks** over a fixture whose every expected value is **exact by construction** — Hadamard rows, so the twins are 1.00, the mirror −1.00, the orthogonal pair 0.00 and `p+q` exactly 0.71 — plus two deliberately short histories for the pairwise and thin paths. **Proved by reverting eleven times**, every one load-bearing.
  - **TWO REVERTS CAME BACK NOT LOAD-BEARING AND BOTH WERE THE FIXTURE.** It had no short history, so the pairwise guard could not bite; and it had no MIDDLING positive pair, so "shaded by strength" could not be told from "shaded at all" — a zero cell is unfilled whatever the scale in between does. Three strengths in order is the honest check.
  - **A READ IN THE DETAIL ARGUMENT ENDS A RUN** just as surely as one in the assertion: with the card in its empty state `get(...).g.slice()` threw and a revert that fails 29 reported as 1. Every accessor is total now.
  - **`group-page-test.js` NEEDED CORRECTING, NOT WORKING AROUND**, twice over: a hardcoded *"seven cards"* that said nothing about WHICH seven (it names them now, which also catches a card going missing), and a bare `.tblwrap` selector that picks the **first** wrapper on the page — now the matrix, not the stock table it means.

#### The stock table sorts on every column (2026-10-02, owner's request)
**Click any heading in `THE N STOCKS` to re-sort; click it again to reverse.** It was market cap descending and nothing else, which is the right default and the wrong only option — the table carries price, today, the range return, the cap and the Balanced verdict, and on a 200-stock sector "which of these fell hardest today" had no answer on the page.

- **IT SORTS THE WHOLE GROUP, THEN TAKES THE FIRST 50 — never the other way round.** The list is capped at 50 with a Show 50 more button, so slicing before sorting reorders only the page you happen to be looking at: **plausible on a 24-stock industry and wrong on a sector**, and it looks perfectly well formed either way. **The fixture is what makes that provable — 60 stocks against the cap, with the winner of every sort planted DELIBERATELY OUTSIDE the first 50 by market cap**, so a sort that touched only the drawn rows could never surface it. **Proved by reverting**: slicing first fails 8. The note says so too, naming the number it ordered.
- **THE VERDICT SORTS ON THE ENGINE'S LADDER, NOT THE ALPHABET**, through `ActionRules.ACTIONS.indexOf` — the index IS the rank. **`ACTIONS` runs WORST-FIRST**, so the raw index already ranks Strong Buy highest on a descending sort and needs no reversal; the pivot's own advice-move measure records what happens when that list is read in display order. The fixture separates the two ladders at position two (Buy against the alphabet's "Sell Immediately") and at the tail, because descending they agree on the leader. **Proved by reverting**: sorting it as text fails 4.
- **A BLANK IS NOT A ZERO AND GOES LAST WHICHEVER WAY THE COLUMN IS SORTED.** No verdict is not the worst verdict and no price is not free. The blank row is given the LARGEST cap in the fixture so the default sort puts it FIRST, which is where a blank-as-zero bug is most visible. The note says it in one clause. **Proved by reverting**: fails 4, the priceless row leading an ascending price sort.
- **THE RETURN COLUMN FOLLOWS THE RANGE BUTTONS, so the comparator reads the field rather than freezing one.** A sort that stopped following 1M/3M/6M/1Y would still look sorted. One fixture stock leads on 1Y and sits mid-pack on 1M, so changing the range must re-sort. **Proved by reverting**: fails 1.
- **A FIRST CLICK OPENS ON THE INTERESTING END** — descending for a number, ascending for a name. Nobody opens a price column to see the cheapest stock first. **Proved by reverting**: all-ascending fails 9.
- **THE ARROW BOX IS ALWAYS RENDERED and only the active column fills it.** An arrow that appears and disappears changes the header's width and jogs the whole column as you click between them; the screener positions its own arrow absolutely for the same reason. **Proved by reverting**: the conditional span moves the columns and fails 1.
- **Reachable by keyboard and announced to a screen reader** — `role="button"`, `tabindex="0"`, Enter and Space, and `aria-sort` on the live column. **Proved by reverting** twice: the click handler alone fails 17, the keydown handler 1.
- **Sorting does NOT collapse an expanded list** back to 50, and the second tiebreak is always market cap, so rows level on the sorted column keep the order the page opened in rather than shuffling.
- **Nothing is persisted.** A sort surviving a reload is a table in an order the reader did not choose on a page they have just arrived at — the filter row's rule.
- Verified: **43 checks** on the real page, plus `group-page-test.js` (58) re-run unchanged. **Proved by reverting ten times, every one load-bearing.** `consol-test.js` fails 12 at HEAD too, confirmed by stashing.
  - **A REVERT THAT ABORTS THE SUITE REPORTS "NOT LOAD-BEARING".** Slicing before the sort leaves 50 rows as the whole list, so `Show 50 more` is never drawn and a bare `page.click('#moreRows')` **throws** — 28 of 43 checks ran and the harness reported 6 failures where there are 8. Assert the button exists, then click the handle; the `page.selectOption` trap in a new shape.
  - **The glyphs in the source are LITERAL UTF-8, not `▾` escapes**, so a double-backslashed anchor looked for text the file does not contain — and the source line ends `` </span></th>` ``, so an anchor carrying that trailing backtick matched nothing either. Two separate ways for one anchor to miss.
