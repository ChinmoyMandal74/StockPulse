## Intraday price refreshes
**Prices refresh every 30 minutes through the US session, 9:40 AM to 3:40 PM New York time, on trading days (2026-09-15).** An external scheduler (**cron-job.org**, owner's choice over GitHub Actions, whose schedule can run 5-30 minutes late or skip under load) calls `GET /api/cron/intraday` with `Authorization: Bearer $CRON_SECRET`, set in New York time at minutes 10 and 40 of hours 9-15, Monday-Friday. **The server decides whether to act**, so the schedule can be broad and daylight saving needs no thought:

1. **Weekday, 9:38 AM-4:00 PM New York** (`nyClock`, two minutes of scheduler slack). Outside it the call returns without logging — the 9:10 call lands there every day by design.
2. **Nothing else running** — a Refresh all, Fill missing or nightly run in `refresh_state`; two jobs in one minute breach the 610 credits. Logged as a `skipped` run.
3. **NYSE open** by Twelve Data's `market_state` (`XNYS.is_market_open`, **1 credit**, verified on this plan 2026-09-15) — it knows holidays and early closes, so there is no holiday list to maintain. Logged as `skipped` when closed. An unreadable answer proceeds (a holiday run costs 272 credits and harms nothing).
4. One plain price refresh through the same `computeStocks` → `finishLiveRefresh` path as the Refresh button: snapshot rewritten, bars upserted (today's provisional bar, healed by the overlap), 12 stocks' headlines topped up, **no email**, logged as an `intraday` run with measured credits (~273 a run, ~3,550 a trading day; no daily cap on Pro).

- `?dry=1` runs every check and reports the decision without refreshing or logging. Measured: 13 runs on a summer day and on the Monday after the clocks change; boundaries at 9:37/9:38 and 16:00.
- **Open screener tabs follow the data**: every five minutes a visible tab calls `/api/status`, which now carries `updatedAt` from the snapshot's own column (`snapshotUpdatedAt()` — the ~1MB payload is not read), and reloads in place when it is newer. Sort, filters, tab and scroll survive (page state the reload does not touch); it waits while pulling, in a forward-returns view, and while the ladder panel or the star popover is open. Verified headlessly: 8 checks.
- **Advice is intraday too**: mid-session the Advice columns and the Advice move picker read "so far today" against a provisional bar. The status line shows the update time.
- `/refreshes` shows the kind as *Intraday prices*, `skipped` runs with their reason, and a line in the Last nightly tile: today's intraday runs, the last one, failures and skips.
- **Setting it up is on cron-job.org, not in this repo** — nothing runs until that job exists.

#### A single-round refresh re-priced the same 500 stocks for ever (2026-09-24)
**The owner asked why only 500 of 1,165 stocks were being refreshed, and the answer was worse than the slice size: it was the same 500 every time.** Measured on production after the first scheduled intraday run:

| | |
|---|---|
| freshly priced | **500 of 1,164 (43%)** |
| of the FIRST 500 in universe order | **500** |
| of the remaining 665 | **0** |
| the tail's price age | **90 to 119 minutes, and climbing all session** |

**The pacing worked; its cursor did not exist outside a run.** `liveRefreshOpts` took the offset from `refresh_state.priced`, and that row is only written by a MULTI-ROUND run. A plain Refresh and the intraday schedule deliberately write no such row -- one there raises the "refreshing" banner for every viewer, thirteen times a trading day -- so `done` was always 0. `notePriceRound` opened with `if (!opts.running) return`, and **the intraday route never called it at all**.

- **The cursor lives in `app_meta` under `price_cursor`**, not in `refresh_state`: one integer, no flag, no banner, and it survives the cold start a process variable would not. `readPriceCursor` / `writePriceCursor` follow the `hidden_columns` pattern.
- **It wraps**, so the universe rotates. At 1,165 symbols against a 500 slice that is three rounds -- 500, 500, 165 -- and with the schedule at 30 minutes every stock is priced about every 90 minutes instead of never.
- **A cursor past the end restarts** rather than slicing nothing, which is what happens the first time the universe shrinks below where the cursor had reached.
- **Inside a run the cursor is ignored.** A Refresh all inheriting whatever offset the schedule happened to leave would skip every stock before it and still report itself complete.
- **`PRICE_SLICE` is not the problem and was not touched.** 500 is measured: at 609 a round took 170s on a good day and 423s on a bad one and produced two 504s, so 500 holds a round near 155s against the platform's 300s kill.

Verified: 10 checks driving the REAL endpoint with the provider stubbed, reading who got priced out of `price_state` -- the per-symbol clock the Price pulled column uses -- rather than recomputing the slice arithmetic, which would only prove the same sum can be done twice. The fixture is 12 symbols against a slice of 5, so a cycle is 5 + 5 + 2 and **the wrap lands on a short round**, the case an even division would hide. **Proved by reverting**: the old line gives S00-S04 three times and 5 of 12 covered, which is the production symptom in miniature.

#### ...and then one dead ticker took the whole refresh down (2026-09-24)
**The cursor fix immediately produced two 502s in a row, and the cursor stuck at 1000 meant NOTHING was being refreshed — worse than the 500 it replaced.** Both read `Failed to reach Twelve Data: Cannot read properties of undefined (reading '0')`, which is why the diagnosis went at the provider for an afternoon. It was ours.

- **`BF` is not a symbol Twelve Data knows** -- Brown-Forman trades as BF.A and BF.B -- and **a batched `time_series` reply carries a PER-SYMBOL error object under that key, with no `values` at all**: `{status:'error', code:404, message:'**symbol** not found: BF.'}`. That is NOT the top-level `{status:'error'}` `pullPricesFor` tests for. The call succeeded; 164 of 165 symbols came back fine.
- **Every field on the row guards a missing series except two.** `volume` and `dollarVolume` were inline IIFEs testing `values[0]` rather than `values`, so a series that is UNDEFINED rather than empty threw a TypeError out of `stocks.map`, out of `computeStocks`, and left 1,164 stocks unrefreshed. Note the shape: `error: ok ? null : (s.message || …)` two lines below ALREADY expected this payload — the row was designed for it and two siblings had not been told.
- **The cursor did not cause this, it REACHED it.** BF sits past position 1000, a slice a single-round refresh had never once pulled live, so the bug had been latent since the day BF was added.
- **A catch that names the wrong culprit costs more than no message.** `computeStocks`'s wrapped every failure as "Failed to reach Twelve Data" and **discarded the stack**. A network failure IS a TypeError too (undici's `fetch failed`), so the test is the CAUSE CHAIN — undici hangs the reason off `err.cause`, our own faults have none. It now says "Refresh failed inside the app (not the provider)" and `console.error`s the stack, since Vercel does not show a function's stdout any other way.
- **Reproduced against production with every write blocked at the libSQL client**, which is how the stack was obtained without a deploy-and-wait cycle: `createClient` is wrapped before db.js requires it, and any statement that is not a `select`/`pragma`/`create … if not exists` is logged and skipped (2,327 blocked, nothing mutated). That is the general tool for "it only fails on production data" — the local server talks to the live database, so the guard has to be mechanical rather than careful.
- **A ruled-out hypothesis worth not re-testing**: the failing round reads 1,000 symbols off the archive against 665 for the rounds that worked, so `readBarsFullFor` binds 1,001 parameters. Probed against production at 665 / 900 / 998 / 999 / 1000 / 1001 — **all fine**. There is no variable-count ceiling here.
- Verified: 16 checks with the dead symbol in the MIDDLE of the slice, so a round that merely stops early still fails — the round completes, the dead row carries the provider's own words and no fabricated price or volume, the other eleven are priced, **the price clock refuses to claim the dead one**, and the cursor still advances. **Proved by reverting**: the old expressions fail with production's exact error at the same line. Live after the deploy, the 12:45 slot: `REFRESHED in 21.9s`, positions 1000-1163 all priced, cursor wrapped to 0.
- **BF was removed from the universe** (owner), the same ending `BBX` and `PXD` got: a permanently unfillable ticker is a gap every future Fill missing re-attempts forever.

### Running the intraday schedule from a laptop (2026-09-24, owner's request)
**The intraday refresh had never once fired.** `GET /api/cron/intraday` shipped on 2026-09-15 with the note *"Setting it up is on cron-job.org, not in this repo -- nothing runs until that job exists"*, and that job was never created: on 2026-09-24 the run history held **95 runs, every one `trigger=manual`, and zero of kind `intraday`.** The owner asked for the schedule to run from their laptop instead.

- **`intraday-ping.js` is an HTTP call, not a refresh.** It reads `CRON_SECRET` and `APP_URL` out of `.env` and asks the deployed app to do the work. **It must never become `node server.js`**: booting a local server against the production database to do the same job is the documented way to burn credits and fight the live instance for the same rows.
- **The secret comes from `.env`, not from the command line or the task definition.** A scheduled task inherits almost none of a shell's environment, and a secret passed as an argument lands in the task XML and in history.
- **The server decides whether to act, which is what makes a dumb scheduler safe.** The route refuses at weekends, outside **9:38 AM - 4:00 PM New York** (`INTRADAY_START_MIN` / `INTRADAY_END_MIN`), while another refresh is running, and when the NYSE is shut for a holiday or an early close. A schedule that is broad, slightly early, or firing on Christmas morning costs one credit and changes nothing, and daylight saving needs no thought at either end.
- **Exit 0 on a skip, 1 only on unreachable or refused.** A skip is the design working; a task that goes red every weekend teaches its owner to ignore it.
- **`intraday-task.ps1` registers the Windows task**: weekdays, 09:45, repeating every 30 minutes for six hours -- thirteen firings, last at 15:45. There is deliberately no 16:00 slot: the 4:15 PM nightly is what records the settled close.

**A LAPTOP IS A WEAK CRON HOST, and three Task Scheduler defaults make it weaker.** `AllowStartIfOnBatteries` and `DontStopIfGoingOnBatteries` are set because **Task Scheduler refuses to start a task on battery by default** -- an unplugged laptop would silently skip every slot. `StartWhenAvailable` runs a missed slot when the lid opens, and `WakeToRun` asks the machine to wake for one, **which does not work on Modern Standby (S0) machines** -- `powercfg /a` says which. None of that makes a laptop reliable; it makes it tolerable. An always-on scheduler (cron-job.org, as originally chosen, or a Vercel cron) is still the better host, and the endpoint is identical for all three.

- **The schedule is in LOCAL time and this machine is on Eastern**, so 09:45 here is 09:45 in New York. If the laptop moves timezone the trigger moves with it and the server refuses whatever lands outside its window -- which is the argument for the window living on the server rather than in the schedule.
- **Both files are ASCII only.** Windows PowerShell 5.1 reads a `.ps1` as ANSI unless it carries a BOM, so an em-dash in a comment is a parse error at a line nowhere near it; and `Get-Content` decodes the log as ANSI, so a UTF-8 dash in a message arrives as mojibake. Verified: registered, inspected (`DaysOfWeek 62` = Mon-Fri, `PT30M for PT6H`, battery start allowed, wake on, run-if-missed on), triggered once end to end, and the log read back clean.

### A SLOT is several rounds, and only the last one is expensive (2026-09-24, owner's request)
**"Why are we not refreshing the entire universe every 30 minutes?"** Because one round cannot: prices are 1 credit a symbol against a hard **610/minute** ceiling, so a round takes `PRICE_SLICE` (500) and no more, and a lap of 1,164 stocks took three slots — **90 minutes**. The 500 is also what keeps a round inside the platform's 300s kill (at 609 a round took 170s good, 423s bad, and produced two 504s).

**Nothing about the CADENCE forbids it — the scheduler was firing once per slot, and one call was one round.** So the fix is in the ping, not the pacing.

- **THE NAIVE VERSION IS THREE FULL ROUNDS AND IT TRIPLES THE DATABASE READ FOR NO EXTRA PRICES.** An ordinary round re-reads the ~470-day archive window for everything it did not price live, rescores, and rewrites the snapshot — **work that is identical whether the round priced 500 symbols or 5**. Measured in the test: 6 window reads against 2.
- **So a slot is `rounds - 1` LIGHT rounds and one FULL one.** The light round is the body that already existed behind `/api/refresh-prices` — next slice, archive it, move the cursor, no window, no scoring, no snapshot — **extracted as `runPriceSlice()` rather than copied**, and reached at `/api/cron/intraday?light=1` so it sits behind the same four gates (weekday, 9:38-16:00 New York, nothing else running, NYSE open) as every other intraday call. A separate route would have needed its own copy of those or it would price on Christmas.
- **THE FULL ROUND MUST BE LAST, and that ordering IS the feature.** It reads its unpriced remainder off the archive, which by then holds the bars the light rounds just wrote — so the closing snapshot is fresh for **every** stock, not just its own slice. Nothing in `computeStocks` needed changing for that; it falls out of the archive being the join.
- **A slot is ONE run of N rounds, not N runs.** The first call opens it, `?run=` attaches the rest, and only the full round closes it — so `/refreshes` reads "3 rounds, 1,164 priced" rather than 39 runs a day that each look like a whole refresh. A slot abandoned half way is swept like any other run that stopped reporting; `startRun` writes no `refresh_state`, so there is still no banner and no stand-aside.
- **The SERVER plans the slot.** `?dry=1` now reports `total`, `slice` and `rounds`, because both of the first two move and a copy of that arithmetic in a scheduler goes stale in silence. **An older server answers neither and the ping falls back to one full round** — exactly the previous behaviour, which is what makes the deploy order not matter. The Windows task is unchanged.
- **Measured on the first real slot, and the light rounds are far cheaper than estimated**: `500 symbols in 4.0s`, `500 in 3.5s`, then the rebuild in `21.2s` — **2m34s wall clock, of which 130s is the two mandatory 62s gaps**. Afterwards all 1,164 carried a price under three minutes old. Roughly 1,250 credits a slot (~16k a trading day, and Pro has no daily cap), no minute anywhere near 610.

| | rounds/slot | coverage | 470-day window reads/slot |
|---|---|---|---|
| before | 1 full | every 90 min | 2 |
| three full rounds | 3 full | every 30 min | **6** |
| **2 light + 1 full** | 3 | **every 30 min** | **2** |

- Verified: **26 checks** driving the real routes with the provider stubbed and **`Date.now` OFFSET (not frozen) to a Wednesday mid-session**, so the gate is exercised rather than dodged and elapsed-time code still works. The load-bearing check compares the slot against a **measured baseline** rather than a hardcoded number — three rounds cost the same two window reads as one ordinary round — because hardcoding it would make the test a statement about today's call sites instead of about the saving. **Proved by reverting**: disabling the light branch gives 6 against 2, the exact 3x this avoids. Plus the closing snapshot fresh for all twelve fixture stocks including the ten only a light round touched, the cursor wrapping, one run of three rounds, and `/api/refresh-prices` still working after the extraction.
- **The provider's closes move with an `EPOCH` counter in that fixture**, which is the only way to tell a price refreshed by this slot from one left over from the seed. A fixture with static prices would have passed whether or not the light rounds' work reached the snapshot.
