## Charts and the stock page

### The jump bar — the console's chips, on a page whose sections come and go (2026-10-04, owner's request)
**A row of chips under the masthead, one per card: Price · News · All fields · Earnings · About · Similar stocks · SEC EDGAR · Insiders · Short interest.** Asked for as *"the menus that you have on Admin page, can you add something similar to individual stock page"* — same `.jump` chips, same glyphs out of the same house sprite, nine of which `/stock` did not carry and now does.

- **IT IS BUILT FROM THE PAGE, NEVER FROM A LIST WRITTEN BESIDE IT, and that is the one real difference from `/admin`'s eight fixed sections.** Three cards are only rendered when there is something to render (Earnings, About, Similar stocks) and three more ship `hidden` and are either revealed or **removed** by their own fetches long after the first paint (SEC EDGAR, Insiders, Short interest). A written list would offer a fund chips for filings it never makes. A card opts in with **`data-jump`** — its label, allowed to be shorter than its heading, which is how *"Everything the screener knows"* becomes **All fields** — and `data-icon`.
- **ONE `MutationObserver`, not a call at the end of each loader.** That would be three call sites to keep in step and a fourth card later would be the one somebody forgets. It watches **`childList` as well as the attribute**, because those loaders also call `card.remove()` — every early return removes a card that was still hidden, but **the catch at the end of each can fire after it has been shown**, which would leave a chip pointing at nothing.
- **THE SIGNATURE GUARD IS NOT AN OPTIMISATION — it is what stops the observer feeding itself.** The bar lives inside the subtree being watched, so rewriting its markup is itself a mutation. **Measured by reverting: the page never reaches `networkidle` and the navigation times out.** My own comment first called it a performance measure, which was true and understated it; the revert is recorded in the harness as *expect a HANG* rather than as an ambiguous abort.
- **`section.card` takes a `scroll-margin-top`**, or a chip parks its card under the sticky island — the `/help` lesson. Measured as drawn rather than asserted off the href: every chip is clicked and the card's top compared against the island's bottom (76 against 57). **Proved by reverting**: at 0 the card lands at 0, under an island ending at 57.
- **On a phone the chips are ONE ROW that scrolls sideways in its own box**, not four wrapped rows — a screen of furniture in front of the thing being read. The page itself still cannot be panned. **Proved by reverting**: 3 rows and 130px.
- **One chip is not a bar**, so with fewer than two cards nothing is drawn. Unreachable as the page stands (Price, News and All fields are unconditional), so it is **driven directly** rather than left unproven — hide the rest, assert the bar goes, put them back.
- Verified: **26 checks against the drawn page**, every one comparing the chips against the cards that are **actually there** rather than against a literal — in both directions, so neither a missing chip nor an extra one passes. **Proved by reverting eight times.**
  - **THE HIDDEN-CARD FILTER COULD NOT BE SEEN TO DO ANYTHING at first, and the window is the reason.** Those three cards are hidden only until their fetches land, and `waitForSelector` already resolves after that — so by the time anything was measured the hidden ones had removed themselves and the revert failed on an unrelated check. It is now checked on a page whose three requests are **held open**, which is the state a reader genuinely sees for the first second or two of every load.
  - **PRODUCTION CORRECTED MY FIXTURE'S IDEA OF A FUND.** It assumed a fund has no earnings, no description and no short interest; **SPY has all three and draws six chips, not three.** The mechanism was right and the label overclaimed, so the label was narrowed to what the fixture actually shows. The claim that survives is *the bar equals the page*, which is what the live check measured on MU (9 chips, 9 cards, none orphaned) and SPY (6 and 6).
  - **`$` DOES NOT EXIST ON THIS PAGE** — it uses `document.getElementById` throughout, 49 times. The painter was written with the console's `$` and threw on every load; the nav was in the DOM and empty, so the first failure read as *"there is no bar"*. `pageerror` is what named it.

Charts are hand-rolled inline SVG in `rowcard.js` — no library, no build step. `chartSVG(closes)` returns `{ svg, log }`.

- **The y-axis goes logarithmic above a 4× range.** MU ran from $1.69 to $932, so on a linear axis nineteen of twenty years flatten onto the floor and only the last month is visible. The caption says "log scale" when it happens, because an unmarked log axis misleads. A single year rarely trips it, so the default view stays linear.
- **The chart window is 253 bars, not 252.** A change is measured across the *gaps* between bars, so matching `pctChange(values, ONE_YEAR)` — bar 0 against bar 252 — needs one bar more than there are intervals. One short and the headline disagreed with the card's own 1Y row by 27 points on a name that gapped on earnings a year ago.
- **`/api/history` reads the archive only** — no API call, so it costs nothing and works for members. It returns `dates`, `closes` and `volumes`; ~1.9 KB for a year of closes. Fetched per symbol on hover and cached by `symbol|days`, so the card never waits on the network to appear. **The `days` floor is 2**, because the stock page's shortest range is a trading week — it was 20, which silently returned 20 bars for a 1W request.
- **No area fill — the price is a stroked line only.** Keep `fill` off `.ln`: the stroke and fill rules are deliberately *not* grouped, because setting `fill` on the line path closes it back to its start and paints a filled wedge. The area path masked that for a while.
- **`chartSVG(closes, { volumes, ticks })` draws volume bars and price gridlines**; without them it is a plain line. It returns `{ svg, log, ticks }`, each tick carrying `top` as a fraction of the viewBox so the caller can place an HTML label at the same height.
- **The price axis replaced the floating high/low captions** on the stock page — with real gridlines the two said the same thing twice. Ticks are evenly spaced along whichever scale is in use and rounded to significant figures; even spacing beats round numbers, because on a log axis round values land unevenly and the gridlines look accidental. The hover card passes none — at 104px there is no room — so the two surfaces share one function without the card becoming cramped. Bars are scaled to the largest in view, not an absolute, so a quiet stretch still shows its own shape, and each is green or red by whether that session closed above the one before it.
- **Axis labels are HTML, not SVG text.** The chart stretches with `preserveAspectRatio="none"`, which would distort any glyph inside it. The same reason the price captions are absolutely-positioned spans, anchored by percentage to the bands they describe rather than to the box.
- **Stock page ranges are 1W/2W/1M/3M/6M/1Y/5Y/Max** in trading days (5/10/21/63/126/253/1260/5200).
- **The symbol in the masthead is the stock picker.** Clicking it opens a filtered list of the whole universe, alphabetically, matching on symbol *or* company name. It is styled as the heading it replaced — only the caret says it opens — so the page gained navigation without gaining a control or a row.
  - **The list comes from `universe` on `/api/stock`**, `[{symbol, name}]`. That route already holds the whole snapshot in memory to work out the rank, so the field costs a `map` and ~3 KB rather than a query or a second request.
  - **Selecting navigates (`location.href`) rather than swapping in place**: back and forward keep working, and the per-symbol history fetch dominates the timing anyway.
  - Arrow keys move, Enter goes, Escape and an outside click close. On open the current symbol is the active row and is scrolled to **`center`**; arrowing uses `nearest`, so the list moves as little as possible.
- **Employees, website and the company description come from `/profile`, which `fetchProfile()` already calls for the sector** — the call costs the same whether one field is read or five, so these were free and were simply being discarded.
  - **They are deliberately absent from the snapshot**, and therefore from `/api/stocks` and from the chatbot. Eighty-odd descriptions is ~70 KB on every screener load and ~20k tokens of prose that answers none of the numeric questions the bot is asked. `/api/stock` reads them from the profile row instead, via `readProfile(symbol)` — a single-row read added for exactly this, since `readProfiles()` would pull 84 blobs to render one page.
  - **`buildSections(s, { extra: { info: [...] } })` appends rows to a named group.** Opt-in, and only `stock.html` passes it: these values are not in the snapshot, so putting them in `FIELD_SPEC` would print an em-dash in the hover card on every other page. A row with `href` renders as a link (`.rc-link`).
  - **A row with no value is omitted, not shown empty.** A profile last pulled before these fields existed simply has fewer rows, which reads better than a column of dashes — they fill in as each profile refreshes.
  - **`.about` carries NO max-width (owner, 2026-09-26).** It was `1100px` — a reading measure, which is the right default for prose and made the description **the only constrained block on the page**: the earnings note, the peers note and the closing line all run the full card, so on a wide monitor it stopped ~800px short of everything above and below it and read as a layout fault rather than as a measure. At 2400px the same text goes from six lines to two. The cost is a long line on a very wide window; a cap can come back as a number nearer the card if that ever bites.
  - **A `max-width` is invisible until the container is wider than it**, so a check at one comfortable laptop width passes either way — `about-width-test.js` measures at **2400px and 1500px**, and measures **the text's own client rects, not the paragraph's box**: a block fills its parent whatever `max-width` says, so the box was 2286px wide while the ink stopped at 1100. The tolerance is **the widest word, measured in the page**, since a full line can only fall short by the word that would not fit on it — a fixed 40px tolerance failed on a legitimately-wrapped line. **Proved by reverting**: the cap back fails 6 of 13.
- **The sector/portfolio/rank chips sit inside `.head`, not on a row of their own.** A separate band cost 26px plus 30px of margins and pushed the chart 265px down the page; inline they cost nothing above ~900px wide and simply wrap below it.
- **Indicators stack as panes below the price, in the order RSI then volume** — RSI is 0–100 and price is in currency, so it cannot share the price axis. Turning a pane on **grows the chart** rather than squeezing the price panel: the svg stretches with `preserveAspectRatio="none"`, so a taller viewBox alone would just squash every band.
  - **WHAT MUST STAY CONSTANT IS PIXELS PER VIEWBOX UNIT**, and that couples two files. The bands live in `rowcard.js` (`PRICE_H`, `PANE_GAP` 13, `RSI_H` 58, volume `PANE_H` 44) and the box heights in `stock.html`; the 18px of bottom padding is the date axis and is *not* part of the svg, so a height is `viewBox H × px-per-unit + 18`. Change one without the other and the bands silently shrink instead of the price growing — which looks subtly wrong rather than broken.
  - **The price panel grew TWICE on 2026-09-21**, each step 30% on the one before, at the owner's request (taller chart, *not* taller bands). `PRICE_H` **150 → 195 → 254**, so the viewBox is **317** with volume alone and **388** with RSI too, and the boxes are **468px / 577px** (were 320/427). Measured through both steps: the price panel went **213 → 277 → 359px** and **216 → 281 → 365px**, while **volume stayed 62/63px and RSI stayed 83px** throughout — which is the whole point, and only holds because px-per-unit was held constant each time.
  - **Only the paned case moved.** `PRICE_H` is `anyPane ? 195 : 104`, and the 104 branch is the hover card and the tile/phone stock card, which pass no panes.
  - Verified: 13 checks measuring **bands in PIXELS** off `getBoundingClientRect()` and each pane's reported viewBox fraction — never the viewBox numbers that produced them — in both RSI states, plus the RSI threshold labels still landing inside their own band and the unpaned chart still 104 units. **RSI defaults ON**: the first cut of the test assumed off, swapped its two phases and compared every reading against the wrong baseline.
- **`chartSVG` returns `panes`**, each with `top`/`bottom` as fractions of the viewBox, so the caller positions HTML labels against real geometry. The volume label moves down on its own when RSI appears — an earlier hardcoded percentage had to be fixed once already.
- **Those fractions are of the viewBox, and the svg does not fill the box** — 18px at the bottom is reserved for the date axis. Every pane label therefore goes through `atY()`, which converts with `calc(f * (100% - 18px))`. A raw `top: %` lands things progressively low and pushed the oversold label clean out of its pane.
- **The chart has a hover crosshair** — a dotted line, a dot on the price, a date pill on the axis, and a readout of price, day change, volume and RSI. All of it is HTML overlaid on the svg, never drawn inside it: `preserveAspectRatio="none"` would stretch the dot into an ellipse and distort any text. `panes.price.at(v)` exists so the dot can sit on the line without the caller knowing whether the scale is log or linear. The static change caption and MA legend fade while hovering, so the top-left says one thing at a time.
- **`priceTicks()` returns low-to-high**, so in the DOM the *first* axis label is the bottom of the scale and the *last* is the top — the reverse of reading order. The end-clamping rules were briefly backwards and pushed the top label 6px above the chart, where `overflow: hidden` ate it.
- **The RSI band is taller than the volume band** (58 vs 44 viewBox units) because it carries threshold lines with words above and below them, where volume only needs bars. It also paints its own `.pane-bg`, a shade off the chart's ground, so the indicator reads as a separate instrument. 70 is red and 30 is green — the colours mean overbought and oversold; 50 stays neutral because it is only an anchor for the eye.
- **`RowCard.rsiSeries()` uses Wilder smoothing**, matching `rsi()` in server.js. Wilder is an exponential average and converges slowly, so it is computed over the padded window and sliced. Cross-checked against the screener's own RSI column for six symbols: **gap 0.000**, at both 300- and 453-bar warm-ups.
- MACD is the obvious next pane and is already computed per row (`macdLine`, `macdSignal`, `macdHist`); adding it is a pane entry rather than a rewrite.

Two raw prices on one axis is the misleading version — NVDA at $180 against KSS at $12 flattens one into the floor and the chart says nothing. The test builds its fixture at a **100x price difference** in opposite directions precisely so a raw overlay could not pass it.

It labels by the **display name**, for which `/api/stock`'s universe now carries `short` beside `name`: "NVIDIA" beats "NVIDIA Corporation" beside a line, and `shortName` is already stamped on every row so it costs nothing. The legal name stays, so the symbol picker's filter matches what it always matched.

## Bollinger bands on the chart (2026-10-16)
The owner asked for "Bollinger style bands ... as an option". A **Bands** button beside 50D and 200D draws the 20-day average plus and minus two standard deviations of the same 20 closes (population figure, as Bollinger defined it): a blue tint between two thin blue edges (`.bb-fill`, `.bb-edge` in `app.css`), drawn under the price line, with a legend entry "Bands, 20-day ± 2 s.d.".

- **One function**: `RowCard.bands(values, n, k)` returns the mean, the two edges and z for every window, oldest-first, with null until the window is full and wherever a close in it is missing. It also produces the screener's 200D z-score (n = 200), so the chart and the column cannot disagree about a standard deviation. `bands-test.js` checks it against hand figures and a two-pass reference.
- **`chartSVG` takes `band: { upper, lower }`**: one closed tint path and two edge paths per unbroken run. The band joins the scale, so it is never clipped.
- **Off by default, per device** (`localStorage` key `stock.bands`), like AO: the chart was already full. Computed over everything fetched, like the averages, so it starts at the left edge.
