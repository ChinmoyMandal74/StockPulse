## Auth model
The app is a **door**: the screener is shared, and accounts only decide who gets in.

- **Anonymous** → `/` redirects to `/login`; `/api/stocks` and `/api/portfolios` return 401.
- **`guest`** (2026-09-13) → the "Try as guest" button on the login page: a deterministic HMAC cookie (`st_guest`, 24h, `guest-access-v1` salted with ADMIN_PASSWORD — rotating the password revokes every guest cookie), **no account, no sessions row**. Guests see **`GUEST_SYMBOLS`** and nothing else — **twenty since 2026-09-26** (it was five: NVDA/JPM/PTON/JOBY/DELL). **Chosen for SPREAD rather than fame**, greedily against the live snapshot: all eleven sectors, all six verdicts, all six cap bands and five of the six trend states, every row with enough history to score. A preview whose rows are all mega-cap technology shows the product's range to nobody, and a row that renders blanks is worse than one row fewer. **The first cut WAS all mega-cap tech** — a pure size tiebreak filled nine of the last eleven slots with correlated names, so the fill is by least-represented sector instead, **enforced server-side on every route** — the CDN incident's lesson. Filtered: `/api/stocks` (+ portfolio names), sparklines, news/latest, the stock-page universe list. Symbol-guarded 403: history, stock, news, model, and the `/stock/:symbol` page redirects. Member-only (`requireMember`): portfolios, chat, contact, signal, lab APIs; the research pages redirect `/`; gateAssets 403s the research JSONs (`single-closes`, `strategy-*`, `lab-grid`) for guests. Prefs: **a guest must never touch the `'admin'` prefs key** (`prefsKey()` falls through to it) — GET returns `{}`, PUT 403s. `/api/me` carries `guest: true`; the screener hides the research menu and Ask button and rewords the read-only note.
  - **THREE PAGES CLAIMED A GUEST GETS “EVERY FEATURE” and none of them ever did** (found 2026-09-26, while widening the preview): the screener's own guest note, the landing page's FAQ and the login page's footer. A guest has never had the research pages, personal lists, the studio, the pivot, compare or alerts. The screener's note also still told guests the assistant *needs an account* — it needs **admin**. All three now say what a guest actually gets. This is the same error the Intro carousel carried, fixed the same morning: **a claim has to leave with the feature.** **Acceptance = the route walk as a guest AND as a member** — both were run on introduction: guest saw exactly 5 symbols everywhere and 403/redirects elsewhere; the member path was untouched (93 rows, all pages, research JSONs).
#### The read-only note was wrong in BOTH directions (2026-09-27, owner: "this is no longer correct")
**"The data updates when the site owner refreshes. You can sort, collapse column groups and keep your own themes."** Neither half had been true for a while, and the second is the *inverse* of the failure this file keeps recording — not a claim that outlived its feature, but **features that outlived the claim**.

- **The data has not waited on the owner since the intraday schedule went live (2026-09-24)**: prices refresh about every half hour through the US session and a full refresh runs after the close. A member reading that sentence would have had no idea the screen moves on its own.
- **"Sort, collapse column groups and keep your own themes" was three of a dozen things.** A member has column filters, screens (with starred favourites), personal column views, tiles, personal themes, alerts, the pivot, compare, the group pages, the promo studio, the research pages and the phone page. Named now: *your filters, views, themes, starred screens and alerts are your own.*
- **"STARRED screens", not "screens".** A member cannot write a screen — those are the owner's — only star one; claiming the screens would be the same overclaim in the other direction, which is what this fix is about. There is a check for the word.
- **`/help` §7 carried the same stale fact** and is fixed with it ("a full refresh runs on its own every evening" and no mention of intraday). `/about` and `/landing` were already correct, and `/alerts` already said it precisely — **four surfaces state this one fact, so check all four when the schedule changes.**
- **THE NOTE MUST STAY ONE LINE.** It is `flex: none` above a page that never scrolls, so a second line is taken from the table's rows. Measured with `getClientRects().length`, not guessed.
**AND IT CAN BE DISMISSED FOR GOOD (owner, the same day: "it is taking vertical space").** Measured: **69px** — 61 of note plus its 8px margin — off a page that never scrolls, so every one of those pixels was coming out of the table's rows. The × takes them back (783 → 852px of `.table-wrap`, two more rows on a 760px window).

- **`prefs.noteOff` for a member, `localStorage` for a guest.** The choice belongs to the person and should follow them to another machine — the rule `tickerOff` already follows — but **a guest has no prefs key at all** (`prefsKey()` would fall through to `'admin'`, which a guest must never touch, so GET returns `{}` and PUT 403s). One flag, two stores, decided by who is asking.
- **A hide with no way back is a trapdoor**, and this note is the only place that says what a member may and may not do. `⋯ → Read-only note` toggles it, labelled with the direction it will go — the news ticker's bargain exactly.
- **`paintNote()` HAS TO RUN AFTER `loadPrefs`, and the first cut did not.** `applyAdminUI()` draws the note during `checkAuth`, which runs *before* prefs land, so the stored flag arrived after the decision had been made: the dismissal worked and then came undone on every reload — the precise opposite of "for ever". Caught by the test, not by reading. **Proved by reverting**: dropping that one call fails 4, including the guest's.
- Every visibility change calls `paintWindow(true)` — the ticker's rule, the phone bar's and the announcement's: no resize event fires when a sibling goes, so without it the table keeps its old height and the reclaimed space is simply blank.
- Verified: **32 checks** — the member fixture is THREE steps (the first account registered becomes the admin, so registering once and calling it a member tests an admin, who sees no note at all), each dead phrase absent, each live one present, one line at 1600px with the table still filling what is left, the guest note untouched, and the admin still seeing none. **Proved by reverting**: the old sentence fails 10.

- **`member`** → sees the same shared screener, read-only (sort, collapse, personal portfolios). **Members and guests see an identity watermark** (2026-09-14): `private/wmark.js`, one script tag on each of the eight data pages, tiles the account email (guests: "Guest preview") across the viewport at 5% opacity — `.wmark` in app.css, fixed, `pointer-events: none`, z-index 70 so a capture of anything carries it. It prevents nothing (nothing in a browser stops a screenshot); it SIGNS captures, which is the honest version of "restrict screenshots". The owner is skipped, as is open mode. Copy-blocking was considered and rejected: `/api/stocks` hands any signed-in browser the whole table as JSON, so blocking Ctrl+C is friction dressed as security.
- **`admin`** → everything: add/remove tickers, Refresh, Refresh all, the forward-returns view, visitor log, user management.

### The privileged role is `admin`, and it was `owner` until 2026-09-26
**Renamed at the owner's instruction.** Seventeen occurrences across four files and ONE production row, which is why this was a real rename rather than the display-only treatment Action→Advice and Scores→`rank` got — those are keys in saved prefs, class names and column ids, where the blast radius is every stored preference. A role is one column value in a four-row table.

- **`ROLE_ADMIN` is the only value written; `isAdminRole()` accepts BOTH for ever.** The cost of accepting a word nothing writes any more is one comparison; the cost of not accepting it is an instance whose only privileged account has silently become a member. **A migration you can be locked out by is not worth its tidiness.** There is a test that authenticates a row still holding `'owner'` BEFORE any migration runs.
- **The migration lives in `init()`, batched with the universe backfill** — `update users set role='admin' where role='owner'`, idempotent, a no-op for ever after the first cold start. In `init()` so production, a fresh database and the test harness all converge with no manual step and no window in which an instance serves against the old value; **batched** because a cold start is paid before the instance can answer anything, and two independent idempotent writes have no business being two round trips. Exported as `runRoleMigration()` so it is testable as a thing rather than only as a side effect.
- **`ROLE_ADMIN_MIGRATION` can be deleted once no deployment predates it.** Left in place deliberately: it costs nothing inside a batch that already runs.
- **NOTE THE OVERLOAD.** `'admin'` was already a stored value — the fallback `user_key` in `prefs`, `chat_usage` and `user_portfolios`, and the actor in the activity log, for the legacy password cookie and open mode. Different table, different column, no collision; but the word now means two things and `who ? who.email : 'admin'` sits a few lines from `isAdminRole(u.role)`.
- `set-password.js --role owner` still works and stores `admin`, so an old note or muscle memory is not a trap.
- `ADMIN_PASSWORD` unset → fully open, everyone is admin (local dev). Set → sign-in required.

**Every account carries a NAME** since 2026-09-26 — asked for at registration, capped at 80 characters, **CR and LF stripped rather than escaped** (the contact form's rule: the name goes into the subject of the approval request, and a newline in a header is how a subject becomes extra headers). The column is NULLABLE because four accounts existed before it did, and a not-null default would have invented a name for each; everything that displays a user falls back to the email, which is what it showed before. It earns its place at the approval gate — deciding whether to let `kenjkatz@gmail.com` in is easier when the subject line says who that is.

### Sign in with Google — built 2026-09-26
**Authorization-code flow over plain `fetch`, no dependency**, the reasoning Resend already follows. Google answers *who is this, the first time*; the callback then mints the **same 32-byte `sessions` row** every other sign-in uses, so `getSessionUser`, the 30-day expiry and revocation are all untouched. That is why it is small. Routes: `/api/auth/google/start`, `/api/auth/google/callback`, `/api/auth/google/pending`, `/api/auth/google/finish`, plus the public page `/join`.

- **OFF IS THE DEFAULT.** `GOOGLE_READY` needs `GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID`, `GOOGLE_CLIENT_SECRET` **and** `ADMIN_PASSWORD` (which keys the HMAC below). Without all three every route answers 404 and `/api/me` reports `googleReady: false`, which is what keeps the button off the login page — **a button that leads to a 404 is worse than no button.**
- **THE ADMIN ACCOUNT IS NOT REACHABLE THIS WAY** (the owner's constraint, recorded before the code existed). A Google signup is **always a member**, and on an instance with **zero accounts it is refused outright** rather than bootstrapping the first (admin) account. `ADMIN_PASSWORD` and the password form stay the only way in there.
- **THE INVITE CODE IS ENFORCED ON THIS PATH TOO, and that is the whole reason the flow has four routes rather than two.** `SIGNUP_CODE` is checked in exactly one place — inside `POST /api/register` — which a callback never touches, and `REQUIRE_APPROVAL=false` makes "lands pending" a no-op. Shipped as originally scoped this would have been **an unlocked side door past the gate the invite code exists to be**. The owner chose *ask after Google comes back*: a returning user never sees a code field, and a new person proves who they are first, then gets `/join`. Same tolerant compare as the register route (trim + case-fold), because it is read off a phone.
- **The carrier between "Google says this is jo@" and "jo typed the code" is an HMAC-SIGNED COOKIE.** Unsigned, anyone could hand the finish route an arbitrary verified-looking address and mint an account for it. Keyed on `ADMIN_PASSWORD`, the guest cookie's trick; 15 minutes, so password rotation invalidating one in flight costs nothing. There is a test that posts a forged payload and is refused.
- **THE `id_token` SIGNATURE IS DELIBERATELY NOT VERIFIED.** Google's own docs sanction skipping it: the token arrives in the body of a server-to-server exchange we initiated, over TLS, authenticated with our client secret — no untrusted party is in the path, so JWKS/`kid`/RS256 would buy nothing and add the only fiddly part. **The CLAIMS are all checked**: `aud`, `iss`, `exp`, `nonce`, and `email_verified`.
- **`email_verified` is the one check standing between this and account takeover.** Linking to an existing password account on an email match is safe *only* while Google asserts the address; without it anyone could claim any address and inherit the account under it.
- **Match on `sub`, never email** — `sub` is stable, the address under it is not. `linkGoogleSub` is guarded on `google_sub is null`, so a sub can never be moved off an account by a later sign-in.
- **`password_hash` and `salt` are NOT NULL and SQLite cannot relax that** without rebuilding the table, so a Google-only account gets a **random unusable password**. Not a placeholder: it means `verifyPassword` can never accidentally succeed, and a password attempt against such an account returns the **same generic 401** as an unknown address — "this account uses Google" is a leak, and there is a test asserting the two responses are byte-identical.
- **Exactly the scopes `openid email profile`.** Non-sensitive, so the consent screen needs no verification review and there is no 100-login cap. Anything else changes both and is not a small decision — the test asserts the scope string exactly rather than loosely.
- `/join` is **public by necessity** (the person has no session yet) and is not a door: it can do nothing without the signed cookie, and with none it says *Nothing to finish* — **not worded as an error**, because opening the address directly or leaving it a quarter of an hour is the normal way to arrive there.
- Failures redirect to `/login?gerr=<slug>`. **The slug is ours, never the provider's words** — an upstream error body can restate the request and the client secret travels in the same exchange.
- `listUsers()` names its columns and allowlists its output, so the new `google_sub` is **not** exposed by `/api/users` — worth knowing if an admin badge for "uses Google" is ever wanted.
- Verified: **45 checks** on the real routes with Google stubbed at `fetch`, **every claim bent one at a time** (forged state, wrong nonce, wrong `aud`, wrong `iss`, expired, unverified email, no email — each asserted to make no account), the empty-instance refusal, the invite code refused and then accepted phone-typed, the member role, a second sign-in going straight in, linking to an existing password account and its password still working, the no-enumeration pair, and the forged carrier. Plus **14** on the default-off state (every route 404, no button drawn, `/join` calm and scroll-free at 390px).
- **A browser cannot walk this flow by navigating to Google.** `pg.goto` to `accounts.google.com` leaves the page on a dead cross-origin error page and the callback then reads as `gerr=state`. Do the handshake in Node, take the `sp_gflow` cookie off the 302, and hand it to the context with `addCookies`.

**Google must never be the only way into the `admin` account** (the owner's constraint). The admin keeps a password, and the `ADMIN_PASSWORD` escape hatch stays. The remaining design notes are in [docs/backlog.md](docs/backlog.md) entry 11.

Accounts live in Turso (`users`, `sessions`). Passwords are hashed with **`crypto.scrypt`** and a per-user random salt — no dependency needed. Sessions are random 32-byte tokens in the `sessions` table with a 30-day expiry, so a single session can be revoked; they are *not* the old deterministic HMAC cookie, which cannot work for more than one user.

- **The first account created becomes `owner`**, so a fresh install bootstraps itself. Everyone after is a `member` — and since 2026-09-14 **every member registration starts `pending` and cannot sign in until the owner approves it** (`users.status`, backfilled `'active'` for everyone existing). Enforced three ways: registration creates **no session** for a pending account, login answers 403 "awaiting approval" (only AFTER the password verifies, so the endpoint still cannot enumerate accounts — a wrong password gets the generic 401), and `getSessionUser` refuses pending rows outright. The sign-up notice to the operator IS the approval request ("Approval needed: Name <email>", button to `/users`); the **welcome email moves to approval time**, when it is true. `/users` shows a pending badge, an Approve button (`POST /api/users/:id/approve`, idempotent) and an Awaiting-approval stat; Delete is how a request is declined. The login page tells a registrant they are waiting rather than bouncing them to a page that would refuse them; approval is logged to the activity log (`account` / `approved:<email>`). Guests are unaffected — the preview stays the try-before-you-register door.
- **`SIGNUP_CODE`** (optional env var) gates registration. Without it anyone who finds the URL can sign themselves up — set it before making the site public.
- **Approval can be switched off: `REQUIRE_APPROVAL=false` (2026-09-26, before the public launch).** A gate somebody has to click is a gate people wait behind, and one registrant had already sat pending for days. With it off a member is `active` immediately, gets a session, and `SIGNUP_CODE` becomes the only door. **It DEFAULTS TO ON, so a deploy can never be the thing that opens registration** — only setting the variable is.
  - **THE ONE COMBINATION THAT MUST NOT HAPPEN BY ACCIDENT is approval off AND no code**, which hands a working account to anyone who finds `/login`. It is allowed — it is the owner's instance — but **never silently**: the server warns at boot in as many words, because the way you get there is setting one env var and forgetting the other.
  - The owner is still emailed on a signup with approval off; the notice reads **"New account"** rather than "Approval needed" and says the person is already in. Knowing who joined is worth an email even when there is nothing to decide.
- **`/api/register` is throttled per IP per day** (`SIGNUP_PER_IP_DAY`, 5). It was unthrottled, so a script could mint accounts and fire one email at the owner for each — the *data* was safe, since pending accounts cannot sign in, but the inbox and the Resend quota were not. **Counted only after every validation passes**, so a malformed body cannot burn the quota (the chat's rule), and keyed on the IP rather than the address, because the address is the thing being made up. Reuses `chat_usage` under a `signup:` prefix, as the contact form does.
- **`POST /api/login` with a password but no email still checks `ADMIN_PASSWORD`.** That escape hatch is deliberate: a broken `users` table can't lock you out of your own instance. **Do not change the HMAC salt `'stock-tracker-admin-v1'`** in `adminToken()` or every legacy admin cookie dies.
- Login failures are counted on the user row (`failed_count`, `locked_until`) and lock the account for 15 minutes after 8 tries. Tracked per user, not per IP, because an in-memory counter is useless on serverless.
- Wrong password and unknown email return the **same** error, so the endpoint can't enumerate accounts.
- **Password reset is by email**, via `POST /api/forgot` → a link to `/reset?token=…` → `POST /api/reset`. `set-password.js` remains the local fallback for when mail is unavailable or an account is locked out of its own inbox; it prompts rather than taking the password as an argument, which would leak it into shell history. `--role owner|member` changes a role, refusing to demote the last owner.
  - **The token is never stored — only its SHA-256**, for the same reason sessions and passwords are hashed: a database read must not hand anyone a working reset link.
  - **Single use and 30 minutes.** Valid tokens are deleted as they are read, so a link cannot be replayed even while unexpired, and issuing a new one deletes any older link for that account.
  - **`/api/forgot` answers identically whether or not the address exists** — the login endpoint already refuses to confirm which emails have accounts, and this one would otherwise give it away. It also refuses to issue more than one token a minute per account, or it becomes a way to flood someone's inbox.
  - **The password length is checked before the token is consumed**, so a typo does not burn the link.
  - `setPassword()` already drops every session and clears the lockout counter, which is what a reset should do.
- A signed-in user can change their own password via `POST /api/password` (needs the current one). Both paths drop that user's sessions, so a change signs other devices out.
- **`/users` is list-and-delete only, never create.** Accounts come from sign-up (gated by `SIGNUP_CODE`); an admin-created account would need a password set for it, which means either mailing a secret or inventing one — `set-password.js` already covers the rare case properly.
- **`deleteUser()` purges `prefs` and `chat_usage` too.** Those are keyed on email, not user id, so without it a new account registered at the same address would inherit the deleted one's saved column layout and its chat quota for the day.
- **Two guards, both enforced server-side and mirrored in the UI as a disabled button:** **an `admin` account cannot be deleted AT ALL** (2026-09-26, owner's instruction — change the role to member first), and nobody can delete the account they are signed in as — that would revoke their own session mid-request. The first was "not the LAST owner", which with a single admin was the same rule **by arithmetic only**, and would have stopped protecting the account the moment a second admin existed; the test promotes a second admin and asserts the deletion is still refused, which is the case the old rule allowed. `listUsers()` also returns active session count, last sign-in and any lockout, which is what makes the page worth opening.

#### Disable — the reversible half of this page (2026-10-01, owner's request)
**`POST /api/users/:id/status` with `active` or `disabled`, driven by a Disable/Enable button on `/users`.** Until this, the only way to stop somebody signing in was Delete, which destroys the row, their themes, their views, their saved layout and their chat usage and **cannot be undone**. A disable takes nothing: Enable puts the person back exactly where they were.

- **THE WHOLE FEATURE IS THE LIVE SESSION, and anything that only blocks `/login` does nothing you could see for a MONTH.** A session lasts 30 days, so a disabled account stays signed in and keeps reading every page until the cookie expires. `setUserStatus` therefore **deletes that user's sessions**, the rule `setPassword` already follows — and it is what makes the maintenance page's *Signed in now* truthful the moment the button is pressed (measured: 3 → 2 on the fixture).
- **`getSessionUser` IS AN ALLOWLIST NOW, NOT A DENYLIST.** It read `u.status <> 'pending'`, which **admits any status nobody has thought about** — so on the day `disabled` was added, every disabled account's live session would have kept working and the feature would have looked inert. It is `u.status = 'active'`. A gate should refuse what it does not recognise, and there is a check that a status this code has never heard of is refused too.
  - **REVERTING THAT CHANGE FAILED NOTHING AT FIRST, which is the lesson rather than a footnote.** Session deletion already covers the disable path, so the two were two fixes for one problem and only the revert said which — the Spotlight card's trap, met again. The gate is now proved on its own terms: the suite sets the status **directly in the database, leaving the session row in place**, and asserts the cookie stops working. That is not a contrived scenario — the status column is the invariant, and anything that sets it without dropping sessions (a direct edit, a future route, a restore) leaves a live cookie on a non-active account.
- **THE SAME TWO GUARDS DELETE HAS — an admin, and the account you are signed in as.** The owner asked for these in two steps and the second one matters: first *"You can disable any user except me"*, which took the admin-role guard off, and then **"Don't disable the admin"** once the gap below was reported. So the rule is the TARGET'S ROLE, not who is asking, and `/users` greys both buttons on an admin row.
  - **THE SELF-GUARD WAS NOT ENOUGH, AND THE REASON IS THE ESCAPE HATCH.** A session minted by the legacy `ADMIN_PASSWORD` login has no user row, so `currentUser` is null and the self-guard **cannot fire** — anyone on that hatch could have switched the admin row off. (Recoverable, since the hatch still works, but not something to be one click from.) A role guard does not depend on knowing who is asking, which is exactly why it closes this.
  - **THE SELF-GUARD IS NOW UNREACHABLE, and it is KEPT deliberately rather than deleted.** Everyone who can call the route is an admin (`requireAdmin`) and every admin is refused by the role guard above; the only other caller is the hatch, where it could not fire anyway. **Its revert proof therefore fails 0, which is recorded in the harness's own case name rather than left to look like a guard that never mattered.** It stays by the `CHAT_DAILY_LIMIT_MEMBER` rule — *keep what one guard flips back on, delete what can never be* — and here one guard really would: the owner's first instruction was the opposite of their second, inside an hour, and if the role guard ever comes off again the self-guard is the only thing left.
  - **The way through is DEMOTION, two deliberate steps**, exactly as for delete — and that is asserted as working rather than left implied: a demoted admin can be switched off.
- **A `pending` registration is refused, and that is NOT a restriction on who.** It already has Approve and Delete, and Enable on it would be **a silent second Approve that skips the welcome email** approval exists to send — while Disable would destroy the pending state with no way back to it. **The route cannot reach `pending` at all**: the status is validated against a two-value literal, so it can never be used to un-approve somebody and make the welcome send twice.
- **The login refusal is checked AFTER the password verifies**, exactly like the pending gate and for the same reason: a wrong password still gets the generic 401, so the endpoint cannot be used to find out which addresses have accounts. There is a check that a wrong password on a disabled account is **byte-identical** to the answer an unknown address gets. Telling somebody who HAS proved the password costs nothing and saves them guessing at a broken sign-in.
- **The page: a badge, a dimmed row, and a count.** `switched off` is **neutral, never amber** — amber means *notice this, something is wrong or old* everywhere in this app and is already spent on the pending count, which genuinely wants a decision; an account the owner deliberately turned off wants nothing. **The row is dimmed as a whole**, which is the part that reads down a list of twenty where a badge has to be looked for. Confirmed **only on the way off** — switching somebody back on restores what they had and needs no second thought.
- The activity log records `disabled:<email>` / `enabled:<email>` — facts, never content, and only when the status actually changed.
- Verified: **43 checks** against the real server, plus **22** driving the real page. The member is **signed in first and the cookie kept**, because that cookie is the thing every other assertion would pass over; and the page suite **promotes a member to admin and reloads**, asserting the button greys out, because a page that does not mirror the server offers a click that 409s. **Proved by reverting seven times, six of which bite**: the denylist fails 2, sessions left in place 2, the login gate 2, the admin-role guard 3, the pending guard 3, the status enum 4, and `requireAdmin` 3 — the seventh being the unreachable self-guard above.
  - **TWO ASSERTIONS HAD TO BE CORRECTED RATHER THAN WORKED AROUND when the role guard came back**, and both were asserting the previous instruction: the page suite claimed the admin row's Disable was *live*, and the API suite claimed another admin *could* be switched off. A third was subtler and is the better lesson — the self-guard check demanded the words *"signed in as"* in the refusal, and with the role guard in front it is now the ROLE guard answering, so **it was asserting which guard replied rather than what happened**. It asserts the outcome (409, nothing changed, your own session intact) instead.
  - **The self-guard revert needed a shadow rather than an anchor.** `if (me && me.id === id) {` is word-for-word the delete route's own guard so it cannot be anchored directly, and a multi-line anchor is refused because **server.js is CRLF on disk**. The revert shadows `currentUser` for that route's body instead, which makes `me` null and the guard stop firing — the same trick the `isCron` revert uses.
  - **`login-busy-test.js` fails 1 and it is PRE-EXISTING**, confirmed by stashing.
- Password hashing (`hashPassword`, `newSalt`, `verifyPassword`) lives in **db.js**, beside the users table — `server.js` and `set-password.js` both import it so scrypt parameters can't drift apart.
- `isAdmin(req)` and `isSignedIn(req)` are **async** now — always `await` them. `requireAdmin` / `requireAuth` are middleware built on the `route()` wrapper.

### Agreeing to the terms — a tick at sign-up, a record, and one prompt for existing members (2026-10-09, owner's request)
**Asked as "I need new users to sign the terms and conditions page, I don't have a lawyer, what do you recommend".** The recommendation taken: a required, unticked box rather than a signature, a record of every agreement, and the terms versioned so a material change asks again. The operator is **Tickrlab LLC**, a New York limited liability company (articles supplied by the owner), and `/terms` now names it.

- **`TERMS_VERSION` in server.js IS THE DATE PRINTED ON `/terms`** (`data-version` on the page's date line; a test asserts the two agree). Changing the terms materially means changing both, and every member is then asked again.
- **`REQUIRE_TERMS` DEFAULTS TO ON**, the `REQUIRE_APPROVAL` bargain: a deploy can never be the thing that stops asking. The test harness (`uni-boot.js`) defaults it to `false`, because a hundred older suites register accounts with no tick; the suites about this feature set it to `true`.
- **AT SIGN-UP**: `POST /api/register` refuses without `terms === true` — strictly, since every non-empty string is truthy and this field's whole job is to be evidence. The Google path asks on `/join` and `POST /api/auth/google/finish` requires it only where an account is about to be CREATED; a returning or linked account passes through and is the gate's business.
- **THE RECORD**: `terms_acceptances` (user_id, email, version, at, via, ip), append-only, plus `users.terms_version` for the gate. `store.recordTerms` writes both in one batch. `via` is `signup`, `google` or `prompt`.
