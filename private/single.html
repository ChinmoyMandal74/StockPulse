The rule

Hold the stock only when it has risen over the lookback and sits above its moving average; otherwise hold cash. Size the position by the stock's own volatility, so a calm stretch is a full position and a violent one is a fraction of it. This never picks between stocks — it decides how much of one stock to own. Stock
Lookback
Sessions of trend measured, ending the skip below. 252 is the 12-month standard.
Skip the last
The most recent sessions are left out of the trend, because that is where short-term reversal lives.
Moving-average filter
A second, independent condition. Both must agree before anything is held.
Target volatility
Annualised. Position = target ÷ the stock's own volatility, so risk per position is roughly constant. Applied inside the simulation, so the cap below is honoured at whatever you set — which is what the portfolio page cannot do. Match buy-and-hold before reading the CAGR against it.
Volatility window
Sessions used to measure realised volatility. 20 reacts to a shock within a fortnight; 60 barely notices it.
Maximum position
1× is the published form — no leverage, so the alternative to a full position is cash.
Rebalance
How often the position is resized on schedule.
Exit when the trend breaks
Also trade the day the signal turns on or off, not only on the schedule. Without it a rule that should be in cash sits in a falling stock until the next rebalance.
Transaction cost
Basis points against turnover, each way.
Yield on idle cash
Credited on the uninvested share, and subtracted from both Sharpes as the risk-free rate. Buy-and-hold is always fully invested, so it earns none.
