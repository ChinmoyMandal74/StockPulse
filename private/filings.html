Reading the filings…

A 13D is filed by a holder who may seek to influence or control the company; a 13G is the short form for a passive one, such as an index fund. "Amended" updates an earlier filing, and "no holding" is an amendment saying the stake has fallen below what must be reported. Joint filers (a fund, its manager and its owner) report one holding several times; it is shown once, at the largest stake stated. An 8-K's items say what kind of event it reports; the ones marked in amber are worth noticing (a bankruptcy, a restatement warning, an auditor change, a listing notice and the like). The filing itself, linked on each row, is where what happened is written. A filing is a record, not a reading of the stock.
