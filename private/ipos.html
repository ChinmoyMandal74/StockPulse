SEC filings

IPO pipeline

Companies that have filed with the SEC to sell shares to the public for the first time in the US, each with every registration filing on its record: the confidential drafts once the SEC releases them, the public registration statement (Form S-1, or F-1 for a foreign company) and its amendments, the final prospectus that sets the price, and any withdrawal. Read each morning from the SEC's index of the previous business day. A company has no share price here until it trades.

Reading the filings…

The proposed ticker, the exchange, the price range and the number of new shares the company offers are read from the cover page of its newest registration statement; a range appears only once the company prints one, normally in an amendment a week or two before pricing. "Priced" means the final prospectus (Form 424B) is filed; "Listed" means the SEC now lists a ticker for the company. A registration with no filing for nine months or more is marked in amber: many are never completed and never withdrawn. Blank-cheque companies (SPACs) and companies that were already filing annual or quarterly reports are left out. A filing is a record, not a reading of the company.
