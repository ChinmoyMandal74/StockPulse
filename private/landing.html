Earnings, read by AI

Every results release, read the day it is filed.

When a company files its quarterly results with the SEC, an AI model reads the press release and copies out the figures exactly as printed: at 9:15 am or 6:15 pm Eastern, whichever comes first after the filing (a filing later in the evening is read the next morning). Our own code then checks every one against the release's text. A figure it cannot find there is left blank, never guessed.

  • Revenue, operating income, net income and GAAP EPS, each with the change on a year earlier
  • The company's own adjusted EPS, under its own name for it
  • Guidance in the company's words, with next quarter's revenue against this quarter and full-year EPS against last year
  • Whether the company raised, lowered or reaffirmed its outlook, shown only when it says so, with its words quoted
  • Checked again against the 10-Q once it is filed
  • In the screener, on every stock page and on the public results page. A description of what was reported. It never feeds the Signal

How we work

A reading of where a stock stands. Not a tip.

Every stock gets one of six words after each close, from a fixed set of rules, and the rule that fired is shown beside it. The words describe strength. They are not instructions to trade, and nothing here ranks winners or sets price targets.

Questions

The ones people ask.

What does "beta" mean here?

The product works and is used every day, but it is still changing. Some things will move or be renamed, and there may be rough edges.

Is it really free?

Yes, during the beta. Research, backtesting and AI will come later as paid options, and what each plan includes will be said clearly before anything is charged.

Can I look before signing up?

Yes. The guest preview opens the whole table on twenty stocks, every column and every Signal, with no account. Joining the beta opens all of them; ask for an invite code on the sign-up form if one is needed.

Is this investment advice?

No. Every reading describes where a stock stands, with the rule that produced it. It knows nothing about you, it is not a recommendation, and nothing on the site ranks winners or sets targets.

Which stocks are covered?

US-listed stocks and a few index funds, {{symbols}} today, including every S&P 500 company. The list is chosen by hand and includes companies that have fallen a long way, so the screen is not only today's leaders.

Where does the data come from?

Prices from Twelve Data. Company figures from each company's results release while it is the newest, then its 10-Q or 10-K, with Twelve Data where neither gives one; market value, forward estimates and short interest from Twelve Data. Company filings, insider trades, 8-Ks, large-holder filings, 13Fs and fund holdings from the SEC; short interest from FINRA; options from the OCC; exchange rates from the European Central Bank; headlines from public news feeds. The Signals and every derived figure are worked out here from those inputs.

Can I use the cards in my own posts?

Yes. That is what they are for. Each card carries the Tickr Lab mark, its date and a not-advice line.
