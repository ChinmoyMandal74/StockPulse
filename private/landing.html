What it is

A screener that explains itself.

Most screens hand you a number and leave. Every score here opens into the factors behind it, every verdict names the single rule that produced it, and every percentage can be traced to bars going back to 2005.

Measures

A fixed scale, not a ranking

Every measure is taken against constants rather than against today's list — so a number means the same thing in a weak quarter as a strong one, and adding a stock doesn't move everyone else's.

Advice

Rules, not a black box

Ordered rule lists where the first match wins, and the caution rules sit above every buy rule. Click a verdict and the whole ladder opens: which rung fired, which were never reached, and how far the price can fall before the answer changes.

Your view

85 columns, or the nine you want

Filter every column, save your own column views, keep personal lists over the shared universe, and start from ready-made screens — day gainers, fresh highs, cheap and growing.

What we will not do

Tell you something works when it doesn't.

Every signal here has been tested against twenty years of bars, and the results are written down — including the ones that failed. Seven framings have come back flat, and each feature built on one was removed rather than quietly kept.

The screener is descriptive rather than predictive, and that is a legitimate thing for it to be: a consistent way to see where every stock stands and how each got there.

Under it

The data, and how fresh it is.

Nightly

Fifteen minutes after the close

Prices, scores and verdicts are rebuilt every trading evening, with prices refreshed through the session. Every run is logged: how long it took, what it cost, what failed.

History

Twenty years of daily bars

Over a million rows, back to 2005, kept so a chart, a backtest and the screener all read from the same archive rather than three different ones.

Fundamentals

Recorded, not just shown

Revenue, margins, cash, debt, share count and the dividend are stored each night, so the movement is there later — the data provider only ever returns today.

Questions

The ones worth asking.

Is this investment advice?

No. The Advice column is a mechanical reading of the table — rules applied to numbers, naming the rule that fired. It knows nothing about you, your tax position or your timeline, and it is not a recommendation. Nothing here is personalised advice.

Can I try it without an account?

Yes. The guest preview shows a handful of stocks with every column and every feature, needs no account and no card, and stores nothing about you beyond a visit log.

Where does the data come from?

Prices and fundamentals come from Twelve Data; headlines from public news feeds; the NASDAQ reference list from NASDAQ. Quality, the trend states and the Advice verdicts are computed here from those inputs.

How often is it wrong?

A feed can be. ADR fundamentals in particular can be incoherent, and a refused data pull leaves a gap rather than a guess. Where a number is uncertain the table shows it blank instead of filling it in.

Who is it for?

Someone who follows a few dozen to a few hundred stocks and wants one consistent view of them, rather than a tip service. It is a personal project run in the open, not a brokerage.
